## Detailed Dialectical Analysis & Rebuttals

### 1. Cutaneous Barrier Biophysics: Isotonic Ion Equilibrium vs. Water-Activity Leverage
- **The Alternative Position:** Classical skin-barrier studies argue that ambient humidity fluctuations dissipate almost entirely within the outer dead stratum corneum (SC). Blank et al. (1984) showed that steady-state transepidermal water loss (TEWL) and deep SC hydration profiles change modestly across 0–80% RH because surface drying sharply reduces outer SC water diffusivity. Warner, Myers, and Taylor (1988) measured a steep water mass-fraction jump (~70% w/w to ~35% w/w) at the SC–stratum granulosum (SG) interface, attributing it to lipid-keratin partitioning rather than hypertonicity, and von Zglinicki et al. (1993) found via frozen-hydrated X-ray microanalysis that $\text{Na}^+$, $\text{K}^+$, and $\text{Cl}^-$ per unit water stay near dermal equilibrium—setting tight bounds against a standing macroscopic salt lake at SG1/SG2. Furthermore, claudin-1/claudin-4 tight junctions at SG2 restrict paracellular ions far more strongly than water (Furuse et al., 2002; Kirschner et al., 2013), non-peptidergic *Mrgprd*+ nociceptors densely innervate the outer SG (Dussor et al., 2008), and thermo-mechanical hygrosensation models attribute human wetness/dry-cold perception purely to evaporative heat transfer ($L_v \approx 2.45\text{ MJ/kg}$) plus tactile A$\beta$/mechanoreceptor input (Filingeri et al., 2014; Typolt & Filingeri, 2020).
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Ion-blocking, water-permeable SG2 tight junctions *create* trans-membrane chemical-potential suction:** Kirschner et al.'s (2013) finding that SG2 claudin-1/4 seals block paracellular $\text{Na}^+/\text{Cl}^-/\text{Ca}^{2+}$ diffusion while permitting trans-epidermal water flux does not refute osmotic transduction—it explains *why* evaporative water loss lowers local trans-membrane water chemical potential ($\mu_w = \mu_w^\circ + RT\ln a_w$, Equation 3) and shrinks AQP3-expressing apical cells without requiring bulk interstitial $\text{NaCl}$ accumulation. By Equation 4 ($dc_{\text{osm}}/da_w = -55.8\text{ Osm/kg}$), 99.8% dissipation across the SC ($\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.001$) still exerts $+0.14\text{ MPa}$ ($+55.8\text{ mOsm/kg}$ equivalent) osmotic suction across membranes at the SG2 plane.
  2. **Living outer-SG keratinocytes prove the osmotic signal arrives in vivo:** If the viable SG remained constitutively isotonic in dry air, outer-SG keratinocytes would not mount an energy-intensive osmoregulatory response to low ambient humidity. Instead, experimental dry air (silica gel) and mild hyperosmolarity (~405 mOsm/L) induce 3- to 5-fold upregulation of $\text{Na}^+/\text{Cl}^-$-coupled **taurine (*SLC6A6*/TAUT)**, betaine, and *myo*-inositol transporters peaking specifically in the outermost granular layer (Janeke/Siefken et al., 2003; Grafe et al., 2004; Warskulat et al., 2004), activate **NFAT5/TonEBP** to preserve epidermal integrity (Miyakawa et al., 1999; Choi et al., 2020; Muhammad et al., 2021), upregulate **AQP3** osmotic water channels (Sugiyama et al., 2001; Ma et al., 2002) that govern keratinocyte volume-regulated differentiation (Zijl et al., 2025), and drive osmolyte-induced reinforcement of claudin-1/4 tight junctions themselves (El-Chami et al., 2021)—while acute barrier disruption collapses the steep outer-SG $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{K}^+$ gradients measured by PIXE (Mauro et al., 1998).
  3. **Human intraepidermal TRPM8 anatomy and zero-evaporation anomalies:** Ultrastructural EM proves human unmyelinated nerve endings reach the granular layer (Kennedy & Wendelschafer-Crabb, 1993; Hilliges, Wang, & Johansson, 1995), human skin biopsy quantification proves **intraepidermal TRPM8**—and *not* TRPA1—tracks human cooling perception (Weyer-Menkhoff et al., 2019), SG2 tight junctions actively prune intraepidermal fibers (Takahashi et al., 2019), and evaporative heat-loss models cannot explain isothermal somatic DRG osmosensation (Quallo et al., 2015) or non-evaporative oil-droplet TRPM8 shaking (Selescu et al., 2025). **Protocol H2-Gel** ($J_{\text{evap}} \equiv 0$, IR-$T$ matched submersion, where hydrated skin conducts heat *faster* physically) decisively separates the two models.

### 2. Pacemaker Architecture: HCN1 / $\text{K}^+$ Brakes / T-Type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}3$ vs. Depolarized Terminal L-Type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$
- **The Alternative Position:** In the canonical Hodgkin–Huxley / Huber–Braun cold-receptor literature, rhythmic firing and threshold diversity are governed by **HCN1 ($I_h$)** (setting 5–7 Hz subthreshold resonance and interburst timing; Orio et al., 2009, 2012) balanced against **$\text{K}_\text{v}1.1/1.2$ ($I_{\text{KD}}$)** (Viana et al., 2002; Madrid et al., 2009), two-pore $\text{K}_{2\text{P}}$ channels **TREK-1, TRAAK, TREK-2, and TASK-3** (Noël et al., 2009; Morenilla-Palao et al., 2014; Pereira et al., 2014; De-la-Torre et al., 2026), **$\text{K}_\text{v}7$/KCNQ M-currents** (Vetter et al., 2013; Abd-Elsayed et al., 2015), small-conductance $\text{SK}2$ channels (Maingret et al., 2008), sex-divergent $I_h/I_K$ expression (Caudle et al., 2017), and $\text{K}^+$-channel-enriched $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$-negative cold neurons (Luiz et al., 2019), simulated without L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels or mitochondria (Olivares et al., 2015). Classical somatic DRG voltage-clamp classifies L-type current as high-voltage-activated (>−10 mV) while assigning subthreshold $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ entry to T-type ($\text{Ca}_\text{v}3.2$) channels (Nowycky, Fox, & Tsien, 1985; Fox, Nowycky, & Tsien, 1987; Rozanski et al., 2013), and Mikulec et al. (1995) reported in their title that diltiazem "spares" rabbit corneal A$\delta$ and C-fiber cold receptors.
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Somatic holding potentials (−70 mV) miss chronic terminal depolarization ($\ge -40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$):** Prolonged (30–60 s) constant-current polarization of intact guinea-pig corneal nerve terminals proved that cold endings sit **chronically depolarized ($\ge -40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$) at physiological resting temperature** (Brock, Pianova, & Belmonte, 2001; Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002). Across $-40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$, canonical T-type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}3$ channels are **steady-state inactivated** (inactivating across $-60\text{ to }-40\text{ mV}$; Fox, Nowycky, & Tsien, 1987), whereas neuronal **$\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$** activates ~25 mV more hyperpolarized than $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ (Xu & Lipscombe, 2001) and adult rat sensory neurons express **low-voltage-activated, dihydropyridine-sensitive L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels open between action potentials** that directly regulate burst frequency (Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996—fulfilling the terminal low-threshold $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ conductance deduced by Schäfer, Braun, & Rempe, 1991).
  2. **Single-terminal optical $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ imaging and primary inspection of *Mikulec et al. (1995)*:** When $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ is imaged directly inside single intact corneal nerve terminals rather than somata, electrically evoked $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ transients are blocked predominantly by **nifedipine and diltiazem** (Gover, Kao, & Weinreich, 2003). Moreover, inspection of the concentration–response data inside Mikulec et al. (1995) shows that 10–250 µM diltiazem actually produced a **concentration-dependent depression of rabbit corneal cold-fiber discharge** (while sparing A$\delta$ mechanoreceptors, and while T-type blocker $\text{Ni}^{2+}$ had no significant effect!). Likewise, Vetter et al. (2013) demonstrated that $\text{K}_\text{v}7$/M-current blockade sensitizes high-threshold cold nociceptors while **leaving low-threshold corneal cold thermoreceptors completely unaffected**.
  3. **Timescale complementarity:** $I_h$ (5–7 Hz subthreshold resonance, ~150 ms period; Orio et al., 2009) and $I_{\text{KD}}$/TASK-3 set baseline excitability and fast intraburst interspike intervals (represented in $g_L, E_L$), whereas purely electrical $I_h/\text{K}^+$ models cannot account for multi-second (5–60 s) burst arrest by metabolic poisons (Pierau et al., 1974), $\text{CO}_2$/intracellular $\text{H}^+$ (Hensel, 1953; Andersson et al., 2004), or the $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-reversible menthol paradox (Schäfer, Braun, & Isenberg, 1986).

### 3. Burst-Termination Clock: $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-PLC$\delta$ $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ Depletion & PMCA $\text{H}^+$ Import vs. Mitochondrial TMBIM5–MICU1
- **The Alternative Position:** Textbook TRPM8 desensitization is mediated by $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx activating $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-sensitive PLC$\delta$ to deplete membrane $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$, shifting $V_{1/2}$ positively (Liu & Qin, 2005; Rohacs et al., 2005; Daniels, Takashima, & McKemy, 2009; Yudin et al., 2011; cryo-EM structures in Yin et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2026). Separately, in rat trigeminal ganglion somata, Hwang et al. (2011) attributed $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-evoked intracellular acidification and action-potential inhibition primarily to **electroneutral PMCA ($\text{Ca}^{2+}/2\text{H}^+$) counter-transport** (recovered via NHE1/pNBC1), Poburko, Santo Domingo, and Demaurex (2011) showed cytosolic PMCA acid generation secondarily acidifies mitochondria, extracellular acidification inhibits both menthol and icilin in FLIPR assays (Behrendt et al., 2004; cf. Kühn, Kühn, & Lückhoff, 2009), and mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ antiport was debated between LETM1 (Shao et al., 2016) and TMBIM5 (Austin et al., 2022).
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Three qualitative falsifiers of pure $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ depletion:** First, $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ depletion desensitizes **cold and menthol via a shared conformational pathway** (Rohacs et al., 2005; Yudin et al., 2011; Yin et al., 2024), whereas intracellular acidification ($\text{pH}_i$ 7.3 → 6.8) **potently inhibits cold and icilin gating while sparing menthol** (Andersson, Chase, & Bevan, 2004—consistent with cryo-EM evidence that menthol allosterically stabilizes $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ engagement against rundown, Kim et al., 2026). Second, PLC$\delta$ hydrolysis is independent of mitochondrial $\Delta\Psi_m$, whereas cold-terminal endings are densely packed with mitochondria (Hensel et al., 1974; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018) and DRG $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-evoked cytosolic acidification ($74 \to 107\text{ nM } [\text{H}^+]_i$) is abolished by mitochondrial poisons (Werth & Thayer, 1994; Thayer & Wang, 1995). Third, macroscopic $\text{PIP}_2$ depletion studies apply intense cold ($10\text{–}18\,^\circ\text{C}$) or saturating menthol ($100\text{–}500\,\mu\text{M}$), whereas physiological osmo-thermal bursting operates near threshold ($30\text{–}34\,^\circ\text{C}$, $300\text{–}515\text{ nM } [\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$).
  2. **Dynamical necessity of the cooperative >300 nM MICU1/TMBIM5 threshold over linear PMCA:** Gover et al. (2007) and Hwang et al. (2011) establish that PMCA extrudes $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ (and imports $\text{H}^+$) **linearly** from resting levels (~100 nM). In nonlinear dynamics, an unthresholded linear negative feedback damps perturbations monotonically to a stable fixed point rather than generating multi-second relaxation bursts. Self-sustained relaxation oscillation requires a **steeply cooperative delayed threshold**—precisely supplied when burst $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ crosses the ~300 nM MICU1/MICU3 gate ($n \approx 5$; Werth & Thayer, 1994; Logan et al., 2014; Ashrafi et al., 2020) to trigger TMBIM5–MICU1 mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ antiport (Austin et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025) in femtoliter boutons where matrix $\text{H}^+$ buffering (~5 mM) is 4-fold lower than cytosol (~20 mM; Poburko et al., 2011). **Falsification #6** (simultaneous $\text{pH}_i$ + $\text{PIP}_2$ imaging under Ru360 vs. PMCA block vs. $\text{diC}_8\text{-PIP}_2$) resolves the exact quantitative shares.

### 4. Neuropathic Cold Allodynia: Silent $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$+ Nociceptors, TRPV1 Co-Expression & Hemoglobin Acidosis vs. TRPM8 Matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ Overload
- **The Alternative Position:** Influential pain studies argue neuropathic cold allodynia arises outside canonical TRPM8 mitochondrial cycling: in vivo DRG imaging shows oxaliplatin, nerve injury, and ciguatoxin unmask **silent *Trpm8*-negative $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$+ cold nociceptors** via $\text{K}_\text{v}1$ brake loss (MacDonald et al., 2021); oxaliplatin binding to hemoglobin impairs proton buffering, causing bulk DRG acidosis that sensitizes **TRPA1** and is reversed by acetazolamide or topiramate (Potenzieri et al., 2020); corneal dry-eye cold nociception requires **TRPV1 co-expression** and substance P release within TRPM8+ fibers (Li et al., 2019); human diabetic skin biopsies show weak cross-sectional correlation between fiber loss and pain (Sorensen, Molyneaux, & Yue, 2006); and George et al. (2022) assayed mechanical rather than cold allodynia in diabetic *MCU*-KO mice.
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Proton-gating sign separation cleanly partitions TRPA1 noxious cold pain from L-type/TRPM8 cool dysesthesia:** Because intracellular acidosis **inhibits cold/osmotic TRPM8** (Andersson et al., 2004) while sensitizing TRPA1/ASICs, Potenzieri et al.'s (2020) bulk hemoglobin-buffering acidosis explains *noxious icy (<15 °C) TRPA1 allodynia* alongside silent $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$+ nociceptor recruitment (MacDonald et al., 2021) and inflammatory TRPV1 co-release (Li et al., 2019), **whereas** innocuous cool/evaporative dysesthesia (20–28 °C) requires TRPM8 upregulation (Gauchan et al., 2009; Kono et al., 2012) and is **prevented in vivo by nifedipine and diltiazem via L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$/NFAT signaling** (Kawashiri et al., 2012).
  2. **Biphasic dynamical trajectory resolves *Sorensen et al. (2006)* and subtype-selective diabetic loss (*Alamri, Brock, et al., 2019*):** Our ODE predicts a **two-stage metabolic trajectory**: mild early $\Delta\Psi_m$ decline ($\Psi_m = 0.85 \to 0.42$) erases the $\text{H}^+$ reset clock and locks intact terminals into hyperexcitable tonic $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ firing (painful early dysesthesia *before* fiber loss; Sorensen et al., 2006), which subsequently drives chronic mitochondrial matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload ($c_m = 5.36\text{ a.u.}$), Drp1 fission (George et al., 2022), and **preferential dying-back of tonically cycling TRPM8+ corneal complex endings ahead of quiescent (<1 Hz) TRPV1-only endings** (Alamri, Brock, et al., 2019; Yorek MS et al., 2016).

### 5. Human Genetics: *TRPM8* SNP Meta-Analyses, Menthol Normosensitivity in Dry Eye & Silent *MICU1*/*TMBIM5* Charts
- **The Alternative Position:** While *TRPM8* `rs10166942` tracks latitudinal adaptation, DRG *TRPM8* expression, chronic migraine allodynia, and cold pain in several cohorts (Chasman et al., 2011; Key et al., 2018; Gavva et al., 2019; Ling et al., 2019; cf. *TRPM8*/*F2RL1* cold-pain SNPs in Soeda et al., 2021), a migraine meta-analysis noted heterozygous CT risk complexity (Siokas et al., 2022), and Kaido et al. (2021) found **menthol cold detection thresholds did not differ** between dry-eye patients and healthy controls despite heightened qualitative unpleasantness. Furthermore, *SCN10A* SFN cohorts (3.7% of 1,139 patients; Han et al., 2014; Eijkenboom et al., 2019) report warmth/burning pain more frequently than isolated cold allodynia, and human *MICU1* (Kohlschmidt et al., 2021; Beheshti et al., 2025), *TMBIM5* (Zhang et al., 2022), and *LETM1* (Kaiyrzhanov et al., 2022) phenotyping highlights myopathy and neurological deficits without spontaneous cold complaints.
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Unchanged menthol threshold in dry eye (*Kaido et al., 2021*) is a *positive* prediction of our model:** Because menthol **bypasses both osmotic gating and intracellular $\text{H}^+$ negative feedback** (Andersson et al., 2004; Kim et al., 2026; **Figure 3H**), probing dry-eye corneas with menthol circumvents the exact osmo-metabolic burst loop sensitized by tear hyperosmolarity—producing normal detection threshold alongside tonic plateau unpleasantness! Meanwhile, unlike non-coding tag SNPs subject to ancestry-dependent linkage disequilibrium (Siokas et al., 2022), rare **coding GoF variants** in both *TRPM8* (R30Q, D665N, V915M; Gualdani et al., 2021; Ghovanloo et al., 2024) and *SCN10A* (G1662S, I206M; Han et al., 2014; Chidiac et al., 2021; Ghovanloo et al., 2026) reproducibly trigger corneal post-LASIK pain and cooling hyperexcitability.
  2. **Why *MICU1* LoF predicts *blunted* cold adaptation, not spontaneous cold pain:** In **Table 4 & Figure 4D**, *MICU1* LoF produces constitutive resting matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload (25× WT) that **collapses oscillatory cold-burst amplitude by −91%** via premature proton leak—predicting subtle *blunting* of dynamic cold/wetness adaptation (consistent with impaired pain perception noted in *MICU1* deficiency by Kohlschmidt et al., 2021) that standard neuromuscular exams never assay without targeted QST.

### 6. Clinical Pharmacology: Hemodynamic Raynaud's CCB Trials & Sign–Symptom Dissociation in Topical TRPM8 Trials
- **The Alternative Position:** Cochrane systematic reviews find that oral $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel blockers confer only modest benefit in primary Raynaud's phenomenon (SMD ≈ 0.23; Ennis et al., 2016; moderate efficacy across 38 primary/secondary trials in Rirash et al., 2017), classically attributed purely to vascular smooth-muscle $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ vasodilation, while ACEi/ARBs have been tested for vasa nervorum blood flow in diabetic rats (Coppey et al., 2006) rather than human cold sensitivity. In dry eye, topical TRPM8 agonism relieved neuropathic pain in a 15-patient open pilot (Yoon et al., 2021), missed co-primary endpoints in Phase 2b COMET-1 despite 0.003% activity (Wirta et al., 2022), and won FDA approval (Lee, 2025) after Phase 3 COMET-2/COMET-3 ($N = 931$; Pattar et al., 2026) met primary Schirmer tearing endpoints (42.6–53.2% vs. 8.2–14.4%) while achieving statistically significant SANDE symptom separation in COMET-2 but not COMET-3.
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Quantitative explanation of systemic DHP noise (**Figure 5B**):** Oral low-dose DHPs preferentially dilate vascular $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ ($IC_{50} \approx 10\text{–}50\text{ nM}$, warming skin and washing out epidermal hyperosmolarity) while exerting incomplete block on ~20-fold less sensitive neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ (Xu & Lipscombe, 2001) and paradoxically *activating* perivascular nociceptive TRPA1 (Fajardo et al., 2008)—quantitatively explaining why systemic CCB sensory outcomes are compressed in perfused skin and why **avascular corneal NTI pharmacology** and **IR-temperature/PPG-clamped DHP vs. ACEi/ARB crossovers** (Protocol H2-Gel) are required to isolate the neuronal L-type limb.
  2. **Autonomic tearing vs. peripheral burst-to-tonic sensory accommodation:** Basal/bursting TRPM8 afferent inflow drives a high-gain brainstem lacrimal reflex (Parra et al., 2010; Pattar et al., 2026), whereas sustained synthetic agonist occupancy pushes chronically depolarized corneal terminals across the burst-to-tonic bifurcation (Carr et al., 2003) into $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ slow inactivation and $\text{H}^+/\text{PIP}_2$ sensory accommodation—explaining strong, reproducible Schirmer tearing alongside faster accommodation of subjective ocular discomfort across COMET-1/2/3.

  - **R1 (Matched Linear PMCA $\text{Ca}^{2+}/2\text{H}^+$ Only, $n=1, \alpha_H=16.5$):** **0 bursts**, damped 667.5 nM plateau ($\text{pH}_{\min} = 7.07$); 2D $(n, \alpha_H)$ bifurcation scan proves sustained bursting requires $n \ge 2.0$, and even 3× over-driven PMCA ($\alpha_H=48$) fails empirically by firing **6 bursts during FCCP ($\Psi_m=0.05$)**.
  - **R2 ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$-PLC$\delta$ $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ Depletion Only):** **0 bursts**, adapts identically under FCCP ($\Psi_m=0.05$) and desensitizes 35 µM menthol (531.0 nM, $\text{PIP}_{2,\min}=0.33$) symmetrically with osmo-cold (*Yin et al., 2024*).
  - **R3 (Pure $I_h + I_{\text{KD}} + \text{T-Type Ca}_\text{v}3.2$, $g_{\text{CaL}}=0$):** Stalls subthreshold at 243.8 nM (0 bursts) due to $\text{Ca}_\text{v}3.2$ steady-state inactivation at $\ge -40\text{ mV}$.
  - **M2 Coupled Hybrid (`TMBIM5 + PMCA + Basal PIP2`):** Retains **97% burst amplitude (254.4 vs. 261.2 nM, 3 bursts)** under intracellular $\text{diC}_8\text{-PIP}_2$ clamping, whereas **50 mM pipette HEPES** (704.3 nM plateau, $\text{pH}_{\min}=7.23$) or **Ru360** (755.6 nM plateau, $\text{pH}_{\min}=7.22$) abolishes bursting.
