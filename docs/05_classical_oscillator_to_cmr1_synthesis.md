# From Oscillating Cold Fibers (1935–2000) to CMR1/TRPM8 (2002–2026): A 90-Year Synthesis

## 1. Why Modern TRP Biology Forgot Half the Mechanism

When **CMR1 (Cold and Menthol Receptor 1 / TRPM8)** was cloned in 2002 (McKemy, Neuhausser & Julius, *Nature*; Peier et al., *Cell*), somatosensory neuroscience adopted a seductive single-molecule cartoon: *"Cold opens CMR1/TRPM8 → afferent fires → brain perceives cold."*

In doing so, post-2002 textbooks largely sidelined **seven decades of classical intact-fiber electrophysiology (1935–2002)** showing that mammalian cold receptors are **not passive thermometers**—they are **intrinsic, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-regulated, metabolically sensitive, oscillating inward-current pacemakers** whose discharge pattern, bursting bifurcations, and sensitivity to $\text{CO}_2$/acidosis, external $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, metabolic pumps, and menthol cannot be explained by a standalone cold-gated labeled line.

Re-integrating this pre-molecular record with **CMR1/TRPM8 biophysics**, **osmosensation**, and **mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ buffering** resolves five long-standing anomalies at once—and provides a 100% literature-grounded foundation for a standalone theoretical paper.

## 2. Historical Chronology: Four Eras of Cold Thermoreception

### Era I (1935–1975): Spontaneous Bursting, Menthol Shift, Paradoxical Cold, $\text{CO}_2$ Inhibition, & Metabolic Sensitivity
- **Zotterman (1935, 1936) & Hensel & Zotterman (1951a, *Acta Physiol Scand*):** Isolated single cutaneous/lingual cold fibers in mammals; established ongoing **static spontaneous discharge** at physiological skin temperatures (30–35 °C) plus sharp **dynamic overshoot** on cooling and silence on warming.
- **Hensel & Zotterman (1951b, *Acta Physiol Scand* — *"The effect of menthol on the thermoreceptors"*):** Demonstrated that **menthol** shifts cold-fiber static activity toward warmer temperatures decades before any receptor protein was known.
- **Dodt & Zotterman (1952, *Acta Physiol Scand*):** Proved **"paradoxical cold"**—specific cold fibers fire vigorously again when heated above ~45 °C, immediately warning that peripheral cold-fiber discharge does not map 1:1 onto objective cooling.
- **Hensel (1953) & Classical $\text{CO}_2$/Acidosis Studies:** Demonstrated that elevated local **$\text{CO}_2$ tension / acidification depresses static cold-fiber discharge** while exciting warm fibers—**51 years before Andersson, Chase & Bevan (2004)** proved that intracellular protons ($\text{H}^+$) directly block cold- and icilin-activated CMR1/TRPM8 while sparing menthol!
- **Iggo (1969, *J Physiol*) & Darian-Smith, Johnson & Dykes (1973):** Characterized primate and feline cutaneous cold fibers, documenting **periodic bursting discharges** whose burst structure (spikes/burst and interburst period) carries static thermal information even where mean firing rate is ambiguous (bell-shaped static rate curve!).
- **Pierau, Torrey & Carpenter (1974, 1975) & Spray (1974):** Demonstrated that **ouabain** (inhibiting the electrogenic $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase) and metabolic perturbations profoundly alter cold-receptor activity and adaptation—establishing early that peripheral cold transduction is tightly coupled to **cellular bioenergetics and resting membrane polarization**.

### Era II (1979–2000): The Subthreshold Oscillating Inward Current & Calcium-Regulated Pacemaker
Long before CMR1/TRPM8 was cloned, Hans A. Braun, Klaus Schäfer, Herbert Hensel, and colleagues proved mathematically and electrophysiologically that cold fibers run an **endogenous subthreshold membrane oscillator**:
- **Bade, Braun & Hensel (1979) & Braun, Bade & Hensel (1980, *Pflügers Arch*):** Analyzed static and dynamic bursting in feline nasal/lingual cold fibers; deduced an underlying **cyclic receptor potential oscillation** that crosses spike threshold rhythmically (producing regular bursts, skipped spikes, and period-doubling transitions across temperature).
- **Schäfer, Braun & Hensel (1982, *J Gen Physiol*):** Established that **extracellular and intracellular $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ control burst generation** in cold receptors—implicating a $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-dependent feedback conductance in resetting each oscillation cycle.
- **Schäfer, Braun & Isenberg (1986, *J Gen Physiol*, [PMC2228855](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC2228855)):** *Crucial forgotten finding!* Analyzed **menthol** on feline cold fibers and discovered **non-monotonic, biphasic oscillator modulation**:
  - Burst frequency **increases with warming**, while impulses per burst **increase with cooling**.
  - Micromolar menthol accelerates bursting at warm temperatures (>25 °C) and converts tonic fibers into bursters, **reduces** burst frequency at mid temperatures, and **suppresses all bursting/activity at low temperatures**.
  - **External $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ completely abolished the stimulating effect of menthol**—leading them to conclude (16 years before CMR1!) that menthol alters cold-receptor bursting through $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ currents and a $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-controlled repolarizing feedback mechanism!
- **Swandulla, Carbone, Schäfer & Lux (1986, 1987):** Showed directly in vertebrate DRG neurons that **menthol blocks low-threshold voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ currents and accelerates inactivation of high-threshold $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ currents**—meaning menthol is NOT a "pure" CMR1-only probe; it directly perturbs the L/T-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ leg of the cold-fiber oscillator!
- **Braun, Wissing, Schäfer & Hirsch (1994, *Nature* 367:270–273) & Huber–Braun Model (Braun et al., 1998, *Int J Bifurcation Chaos*; 2000, *Neurocomputing*):** Formalized the canonical **oscillating inward-current cold receptor**:
  - Fast spike pair ($I_{Na} + I_K$) riding atop a **slow subthreshold oscillating inward current ($I_{sd}$: persistent $\text{Na}^+$ / subthreshold $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ conductance)** opposed by a **slow $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-dependent repolarizing feedback ($I_{sr}$)**.
  - Demonstrated that **oscillation + noise** enables multimodal transduction (thermal + mechanical + chemical/electrical) far beyond a static scalar rate code!

### Era III (1998–2009): Focal Corneal Nerve-Terminal Biophysics (Brock Corpus), Native $I_{\text{cold}}$, Cloning of CMR1/TRPM8, & Terminal L-Type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$
- **Brock, McLachlan & Belmonte (1998, *J Physiol* 512:211–217) & Brock, Pianova & Belmonte (2001, *J Physiol* 533:493–501):** Introduced focal extracellular suction-pipette recordings of **nerve terminal impulses (NTIs)** in the isolated guinea-pig cornea—proving TTX-resistant terminal electrogenesis, ongoing rhythmic bursting in cold terminals vs. silent/irregular TRPV1+ polymodal terminals, and passive electrotonic invasion of depolarized cold terminal tips.
- **Suto & Gotoh (1999, *Neuroscience*) & Reid & Flonta (2001, *Nature* 413:480; Reid, Babes & Pluteanu 2002, *J Physiol*; de la Peña et al. 2005, *J Physiol*):** Patch-clamp and $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-imaging demonstrations of native cooling- and menthol-activated **inward cation/$\text{Ca}^{2+}$ current ($I_{\text{cold}}$)** in rat/mouse DRG and trigeminal neurons, showing rapid $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-dependent adaptation.
- **McKemy, Neuhausser & Julius (2002, *Nature* 416:52–58) & Peier et al. (2002, *Cell* 108:705–715):** Cloned **CMR1 / TRPM8**—identifying the molecular cold- and menthol-gated non-selective $\text{Na}^+/\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel.
- **Viana, de la Peña & Belmonte (2002, *Nature Neuroscience* 5:254–260) & Madrid et al. (2006, 2009, *J Neurosci*):** Proved against single-channel reductionism that cold-neuron threshold and bursting depend on the **combinatorial ionic complement** ($I_{\text{cold}}/\text{TRPM8}$ balanced against the slow outward brake $I_{\text{KD}}$ [$\text{K}_\text{v}1.1/1.2$] and $I_h$ [HCN]) across retrogradely labeled mouse and guinea-pig corneal cold neurons.
- **Carr & Brock (2002), Carr, Pianova & Brock (2002, *J Gen Physiol* 120:395–405, [PMC2229520](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC2229520)), Carr et al. (2003, *J Gen Physiol* 121:427–439, [PMC2217380](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC2217380)), Brock et al. (2006, *J Physiol*), & Carr, Pianova, McKemy & Brock (2009, *J Physiol* 587:1249–1264):** *Pivotal intact-terminal physiology!* Passing prolonged 30–60 s constant polarizing currents across corneal cold nerve terminals proved:
  1. Corneal cold terminals sit **chronically depolarized ($\ge -40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$)** at physiological temperature—parking the membrane directly inside the persistent window inward $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ current of neuronal **$\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$** ($V_{1/2}\approx -40\text{ mV}$; Xu & Lipscombe 2001; Lipscombe, Helton & Xu 2004) and slowly inactivating small-DRG **$\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$** (Scroggs & Fox 1992), matching depolarized trigeminal/DRG subthreshold oscillations (Puil et al. 1988; Amir et al. 1999);
  2. Cold terminals express high-density TTX-resistant **$\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$** held at the edge of fast/slow inactivation (Blair & Bean 2002, 2003), generating cyclical bursts with progressive intraburst amplitude tapering, cooling/rewarming hysteresis, burst-to-tonic bifurcations (<30 °C), and mobile terminal spike-initiation loci.
- **Gover, Kao & Weinreich (2003, *J Neurosci* 23:4793–4797) & Gover et al. (2007, *J Physiol* 578:481–490):** Imaged $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ directly inside **single intact corneal sensory nerve terminals**, proving AP-evoked terminal $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx is mediated predominantly by **nifedipine/diltiazem-sensitive L-type VGCCs**, while brief low-amplitude spike trains are cleared linearly by **PMCA** (leaving high-threshold cooperative mitochondrial uptake for sustained bursts; Werth & Thayer 1994).
- **Andersson, Chase & Bevan (2004, *J Neurosci* 24:5364–5369):** Proved **intracellular acidification ($\text{H}^+$)** potently inhibits **cold- and icilin-activated CMR1/TRPM8** while **sparing menthol**—providing the exact molecular explanation for Hensel's 1953 $\text{CO}_2$ cold suppression AND creating a direct bridge to **Werth & Thayer (1994)** mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-driven acidification!
- **Voets et al. (2004, *Nature*), Rohacs et al. (2005, *Nat Neurosci*), & Zimmermann et al. (2007, *Nature*):** Defined voltage-shift thermodynamics ($V_{1/2}$), $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ gating, and cold-resistant $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ electrogenesis.

### Era IV (2009–2026): Osmosensation, Mitochondria-Packed Bulbar Endings, Selective Diabetic Loss, & Breaking Labeled Lines
- **Orio et al. (2009, 2012) & Olivares et al. (2015, *PLoS ONE*, [PMC4591370](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC4591370)):** Explicitly merged CMR1/TRPM8 back into the **Huber–Braun oscillating inward-current model**, showing TRPM8 is required for both ongoing basal drive and dynamic cooling responses while slow conductances ($I_{sd}, I_{sr}, I_h$) sculpt rhythmic bursting.
- **Parra et al. (2010, *Nat Med*; 2014, *Pain* 155:1481–1491), Quallo et al. (2015, *Nat Commun*, [PMC4455064](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC4455064)), Yorek MS et al. (2016, *IOVS*, [PMC5113984](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC5113984)), Belmonte, ..., Brock et al. (2017, *Ocul Surf*), González-González et al. (2017), Alcalde et al. (2018), Piña et al. (2019), & Malik et al. (2025):** Proved CMR1/TRPM8 is a physiological **peripheral osmosensor** in mouse and guinea-pig cornea—accelerating NTI firing linearly above **340 mOsm/kg** (vs. $\ge 600\text{ mOsm/kg}$ for polymodals), driving blinking/squinting, lost in diabetic neuropathy, and clinically validated by FDA approval of the topical TRPM8 agonist acoltremon.
- **Ivanusic, Wood & Brock (2013, *J Comp Neurol*), Bron, Wood, Brock & Ivanusic (2014), Alamri, Bron, Brock & Ivanusic (2015), Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic & Brock (2018, *PLoS ONE*, [PMC5874071](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC5874071)), & Alamri, Brock et al. (2019, *IOVS* 60:209–217):** Proved in mouse and guinea-pig cornea that functionally identified TRPM8+ cold thermoreceptors form **mitochondria-packed large bulbar "complex endings"** (extending Hensel, Andres & von Düring 1974 and Heppelmann et al. 2001) segregated from ramifying TRPV1+ and *PIEZO2*+ endings—and that **diet-induced obesity and diabetes preferentially destroy TRPM8+ cold complex endings over TRPV1+ polymodal endings**, matching *MCU*-dependent diabetic calcium overload (George et al. 2022)!
- **Gkika et al. (2015, [PMC4284226](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC4284226)) & Hsieh, Eichler et al. (2021, [PMC8387397](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8387397)):** Discovered **TCAF1/TCAF2** accessory regulators of TRPM8 trafficking and gating (under human-specific evolutionary selection).
- **Paricio-Montesinos et al. (2020, *Neuron*, [PMC7272120](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7272120)):** Demonstrated that warmth coding is combinatorial (requiring TRPM8 silencing), breaking pure labeled-line thermoreception.
- **Selescu, Zimmermann, Babes et al. (2025, *Commun Biol*, [PMC12830821](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12830821)):** Proved non-evaporative TRPM8 activation drives convergent **"wet dog shakes"** across mammals and birds.
