## Abstract

Since the cloning of **CMR1 (TRPM8)** in 2002, mammalian cold perception has been largely conceptualized as a static rate-coded labeled line, while wetness and dry-cold perceptions have been attributed solely to evaporative cooling coupled with mechanoreception. This reductionism leaves unresolved seven decades of pre-molecular intact-fiber electrophysiology (1935–2002)—which showed that mammalian cold receptors operate as endogenous, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-controlled, $\text{CO}_2$-inhibited, metabolically coupled subthreshold oscillators—as well as non-evaporative TRPM8-dependent shaking, corneal-versus-cutaneous qualia divergence, and severe cold dysesthesia in metabolic neuropathies. Although TRPM8 is an established peripheral osmosensor in both trigeminal corneal and somatic dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, its cutaneous osmosensory viability across the hydrophobic stratum corneum (SC) has never been formalized quantitatively. Here, we combine a thermodynamic trans-epidermal water-activity ($a_w$) leverage analysis with a 5-variable electro-metabolic relaxation oscillator coupled to a fast $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8/\text{K}_\text{v}$ action-potential spiking layer and a dermal microvascular perfusion module. First, we demonstrate that because $dc_{\text{osm}}/da_w = -(M_w a_w)^{-1} \approx -55.8\text{ Osm/kg}$ (+55.8 mOsm/kg per $-0.001\,\Delta a_w$), an intact SC and stratum granulosum (SG2) tight-junction barrier blocking **99.8%** of dry-air water-activity loss still transmits **+55.8 mOsm/kg** (50% RH) to **+89.3 mOsm/kg** (20% RH heated winter air) directly to SG-terminating TRPM8+ free nerve endings—shifting the modeled cutaneous bursting threshold from 20.0 °C up to 29.0 °C while keeping intact skin silent at warm rest (33 °C), whereas barrier disruption (99.0% shielding) unmasks +280 mOsm/kg corneal-scale hypertonicity. Second, using one fixed parameter set, the oscillator reproduces: (1) self-sustained rhythmic action-potential bursting (~515 nM $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$, 180° phase-locked mitochondrial $\text{pH}_i$ dips to 6.88) abolished by $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ or L-type blockade and collapsed into toxic plateaus by mitochondrial uncoupling; (2) **isothermal hyperosmolar bursting (+89 to +98 mOsm at 33 °C, zero cooling)** and tonic $\text{H}^+$-insensitive menthol plateaus; (3) genotype-to-dynamics phenotypes of human *TRPM8* (D665N/R30Q, rs10166942[C]), *SCN10A* (I206M/G1662S), *MICU1* (25× baseline matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload), *TMBIM5* uncoupling, and diabetic nociceptor *MCU* deletion (−97% matrix overload); (4) microvascular warming and osmotic-washout confounding of cutaneous dihydropyridine (Bay K 8644/nifedipine) pharmacology; and (5) Monte Carlo proof that outdoor relative humidity inverts true indoor vapor pressure deficit (VPD) signaling, whereas within-subject crossovers achieve 80–96% power at $N = 10\text{–}20$. Finally, integrating full dialectical rebuttals to the six major competing paradigms in the literature with head-to-head 6-ODE rival simulations and empirical OpenMM Amber14/OBC2 energy minimization plus PROPKA 3.5 electrostatic $\text{p}K_a$ and tetrameric Wyman proton-linkage calculations across 11 experimental cryo-EM TRPM8 conformations (8BDC, 9P8Y, 9ZCU, 9ZF0, 9PB5, 9ZCV, 9VJN, 8E4L, 9VJR, 9VJQ, 9VNA) and human TMBIM5 (AF-Q9H3K2) resolves a two-hub intracellular proton mechanism—where pre-S1/TRP-box His667 shifts to $\text{p}K_a = 7.05$ in the desensitized state to match experimental $\text{pH}_{1/2} = 7.13$, menthol depresses lower-S4 His845 $\text{p}K_a$ to 3.22–4.23 to escape $\text{H}^+$ block, and TMBIM5 Asp294–Asp325 splits into a $\text{p}K_a = 4.84 / 9.60$ carboxyl proton cannon—yielding single-residue mutagenesis kill-switches alongside seven decisive human, corneal, and cellular falsification criteria.

## 1. Introduction

Molecular cloning frequently resolves transducer identity while inadvertently obscuring intact-system physiology established decades earlier. When the cold and menthol receptor **CMR1 (TRPM8)** was cloned in 2002 (McKemy, Neuhausser, & Julius, 2002; Peier et al., 2002) following characterization of native cold-evoked inward currents ($I_{\text{cold}}$) in dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons (Reid & Flonta, 2001), textbook models rapidly compressed peripheral cold coding into a linear labeled line: skin cooling opens TRPM8, discharge rate rises monotonically, and central pathways read out scalar cold intensity. Similarly, because mammals lack dedicated insect hygroreceptors, human perception of skin wetness and dry-cold air has been attributed predominantly to evaporative heat loss stimulating thermal TRPM8 paired with tactile mechanoreception (Filingeri et al., 2014; Typolt & Filingeri, 2020; Buoite Stella et al., 2022).

However, nearly seventy years of pre-molecular single-fiber electrophysiology (1935–2002) established six foundational properties that contradict a passive rate-coded thermometer:

1. **Bell-Shaped Static Ambiguity and Periodic Bursting (1935–1980):** Mammalian lingual, nasal, and primate cutaneous cold fibers fire spontaneously at normal resting skin temperatures (30–35 °C) with a bell-shaped static firing curve peaking at 20–28 °C, disambiguated by periodic grouped bursting whose interburst frequency increases with warming while intraburst spike count increases with cooling (Zotterman, 1936; Hensel & Zotterman, 1951a; Iggo, 1969; Darian-Smith, Johnson, & Dykes, 1973; Bade, Braun, & Hensel, 1979; Braun, Bade, & Hensel, 1980).
2. **Paradoxical Cold, Ischemia, and $\text{CO}_2$/Acidosis Depression (1951–1956):** Cold fibers fire again above ~45 °C ("paradoxical cold"; Dodt & Zotterman, 1952), respond acutely to ischemia (Hensel, 1953), and are selectively depressed by elevated $\text{CO}_2$ / tissue acidosis—an observation left unexplained until Andersson, Chase, and Bevan (2004) demonstrated that intracellular protons ($\text{H}^+$) inhibit cold- and icilin-activated TRPM8 while sparing menthol.
3. **Bioenergetic Coupling and Mitochondria-Packed Bulbar Endings (1974–1975):** Serial electron microscopy revealed that intraepithelial cold-receptor terminals form enlarged bulbar swellings densely packed with mitochondria and glycogen (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Heppelmann et al., 2001), and blocking nerve-terminal bioenergetics (e.g., ouabain) severely disrupts cold-receptor static discharge and dynamic adaptation (Pierau, Torrey, & Carpenter, 1974).
4. **Endogenous Subthreshold Oscillating Inward Currents (1979–2000):** Bursting originates from a slow oscillating inward current ($I_{sd}$) opposed by slow $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-dependent repolarizing feedback ($I_{sr}$) (Braun et al., 1980, 1994, 1998, 2000; Schäfer, Braun, & Hensel, 1982; Schäfer, Braun, & Rempe, 1991), paralleling intrinsic subthreshold membrane-potential oscillations and low-voltage-activated, dihydropyridine-sensitive L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ currents unmasked between spikes when adult trigeminal or DRG neurons sit at depolarized potentials (Puil, Gimbarzevsky, & Spigelman, 1988; Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996; Amir, Michaelis, & Devor, 1999).
5. **The Menthol–Calcium Paradox (1986–1987):** Sixteen years before CMR1 was cloned, Schäfer, Braun, and Isenberg (1986) showed that micromolar menthol produces non-monotonic reorganization of cold-fiber bursting that is **completely abolished by elevated external $\text{Ca}^{2+}$**, and Swandulla et al. (1987) demonstrated that menthol directly blocks voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels in DRG neurons—proving menthol perturbs the $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-coupled oscillator rather than acting as a clean thermal mimetic.
6. **Chronically Depolarized, TTX-Resistant Corneal Terminals and Prolonged-Holding Inward $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ Dynamics (1998–2009):** Cold specificity requires combinatorial balance between $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-permeable $I_{\text{cold}}$ (Suto & Gotoh, 1999; Reid & Flonta, 2001; Reid, Babes, & Pluteanu, 2002; de la Peña et al., 2005), slow outward $I_{\text{KD}}$ ($\text{K}_\text{v}1.1/1.2$), two-pore $\text{K}^+$ channels, and $I_h$ (HCN) (Viana, de la Peña, & Belmonte, 2002; Madrid et al., 2006, 2009; Noël et al., 2009; Orio et al., 2009, 2012; Morenilla-Palao et al., 2014; De-la-Torre et al., 2026). Crucially, focal extracellular recordings of nerve terminal impulses (NTIs) from single guinea-pig corneal free nerve endings—pioneered by Brock, McLachlan, and Belmonte (1998) and extended across cold versus polymodal terminals (Brock, Pianova, & Belmonte, 2001; Carr & Brock, 2002)—proved via prolonged (30–60 s) polarizing holding currents that cold nerve endings express high densities of TTX-resistant $\text{Na}^+$ channels ($\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$) while sitting **chronically depolarized ($\ge -40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$) at physiological resting temperature** (Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002). Subsequent guinea-pig corneal NTI analyses demonstrated directional cooling/rewarming waveform hysteresis, an endogenous terminal burst pacemaker that bifurcates into continuous tonic discharge below 30 °C in ~31% of units (Carr et al., 2003), distinct $\text{K}^+$-channel control of bursting versus warming silence (Brock, Acosta, Al Abed, Pianova, & Belmonte, 2006), and mobile spike-initiation loci inside the terminal arbor (Carr, Pianova, McKemy, & Brock, 2009; Belmonte, Brock, & Viana, 2009), while direct optical $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ imaging in single intact corneal nerve terminals proved that electrically evoked $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ entry is mediated predominantly by nifedipine- and diltiazem-sensitive L-type voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels (Gover, Kao, & Weinreich, 2003).

A parallel molecular revolution across both guinea-pig and mouse corneal preparations established **TRPM8 as an endogenous peripheral osmosensor**. Adapting Brock's focal NTI paradigm to excised mouse eyes demonstrated that basal TRPM8 activity detects tear-film evaporation to drive tearing (Parra et al., 2010), physiological tear hyperosmolality linearly accelerates mouse corneal cold-receptor NTI bursting starting right above 340 mOsm/kg whereas polymodal nociceptors require $\ge 600\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (Parra, Gonzalez-Gonzalez, Gallar, & Belmonte, 2014), hyperosmolarity (>300 mOsm/kg) directly gates TRPM8 to regulate blinking while hypoosmolarity inhibits it (Quallo et al., 2015), inter-blink tear osmolality climbs dynamically from ~300 to >450 mOsm/kg across functionally distinct mouse TRPM8+ cold-receptor classes (Hirata et al., 2017; González-González et al., 2017; Alcalde et al., 2018; Vereertbrugghen & Galletti, 2022), chronic dryness or axonal injury remodels corneal TRPM8 excitability (Kovács et al., 2016; Belmonte, Nichols, Cox, Brock, et al., 2017; Piña et al., 2019)—culminating in pivotal Phase 3 COMET-2/COMET-3 clinical efficacy and dry-eye approval of the topical TRPM8 agonist acoltremon (Pattar et al., 2026; Malik et al., 2025)—and hyperosmolar eye drops trigger TRPM8-dependent (BCTC-blocked) reflex squinting that is blunted in diabetic peripheral neuropathy (Yorek MS et al., 2016), where mouse corneal TRPM8+ cold complex endings selectively degenerate (Alamri, Brock, et al., 2019). Critically, Quallo et al. (2015) demonstrated that **physiological hyperosmolarity directly excites isolated somatic DRG TRPM8+ neurons**—not solely corneal trigeminal afferents—while human skin biopsy studies proved that TRPM8, rather than TRPA1, is expressed directly on human intraepidermal nerve fibers and quantitatively tracks human skin cooling perception (Weyer-Menkhoff et al., 2019), establishing the intrinsic biophysical and human anatomical substrate for cutaneous osmosensation. Coupled with non-evaporative TRPM8-dependent "wet dog shakes" in mammals and birds (Selescu et al., 2025), orthogonal qualia across adjacent $V_1$ trigeminal territories (cornea vs. lid margin vs. eyelid skin), and combinatorial TRPM8-silencing requirements for warm perception (Paricio-Montesinos et al., 2020), these data motivate a unified mechanistic framework.

Here, we develop an open-source, fixed-parameter biophysical dynamical-systems model linking **cutaneous/corneal TRPM8 osmo-thermal co-gating** to an **electro-metabolic relaxation oscillator ($\text{TRPM8} - \text{Na}_\text{v}1.8 - \text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/1.3 - \text{mitochondrial MICU1/MCU/TMBIM5 } \text{H}^+$)**, test its predictions against human genetic cohorts, diabetic *MCU*-knockout data, and cutaneous microvascular dihydropyridine pharmacology, and specify seven severe experimental falsifications grounded in psychrometric Monte Carlo power modeling.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Thermodynamic Formulation of Osmo-Thermal Co-Gating

At exposed biological boundaries undergoing water loss (corneal tear film, lip vermilion, or epidermis undergoing transepidermal water loss, TEWL), evaporation simultaneously cools the surface ($\Delta T < 0$) and concentrates residual solutes ($\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{ext}} > 0$). Extending the voltage-shift gating model of Voets et al. (2004) and $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$/membrane-tension coupling (Rohacs et al., 2005) to incorporate osmotic free energy and intracellular proton inhibition yields:

$$P_{\text{open}}(V, T, \text{Osm}, \text{pH}_i) = \left[ 1 + \exp\left( \frac{\Delta G_{\text{total}}}{RT} \right) \right]^{-1} \qquad (1)$$

$$\Delta G_{\text{total}} = \Delta H(T) - T\Delta S(T) - zFV + \Delta G_{\text{osm}}(\text{Osm}_{\text{ext}} - \text{Osm}_{\text{int}}) + \Delta G_{\text{tens}}(\gamma) + \Delta G_{\text{H}^+}(\text{pH}_i) \qquad (2)$$

In the conductance model, external hyperosmolarity ($\Delta\text{Osm} > 0$, mOsm/kg above 300) shifts TRPM8 half-activation voltage $V_{1/2}$ hyperpolarized by $-0.72\text{ mV/mOsm}$ alongside thermal gating ($+5.0\text{ mV}/^\circ\text{C}$ relative to 25 °C).

### 2.2 Trans-Epidermal Water-Activity Leverage Across the Stratum Corneum and Stratum Granulosum

Unlike the corneal tear film—an exposed aqueous meniscus whose osmolality is measured directly in mOsm/kg—the cutaneous **stratum corneum (SC)** is an anucleated, hydrophobic lipid–keratin barrier devoid of continuous free interstitial fluid, precluding direct microdialysis or osmometry without barrier disruption. In skin biophysics, hydration state across the SC and its boundary with the viable **stratum granulosum (SG)** is therefore quantified via **water activity ($a_w$)** and **water mass fraction (% w/w)** (Warner, Myers, & Taylor, 1988; Caspers et al., 2001; Sparr & Wennerström, 2001; Sparr et al., 2013). At thermodynamic equilibrium, water chemical potential ($\mu_w = \mu_w^\circ + RT\ln a_w$) maps local $a_w$ directly to equivalent solution osmolality $c_{\text{osm}}$ ($\text{Osm/kg}$) and osmotic pressure $\Pi$ ($\text{Pa}$):

$$c_{\text{osm}} = -\frac{\ln(a_w)}{M_w}, \qquad \Pi = -\frac{RT}{\bar{V}_w}\ln(a_w) \qquad (3)$$

where $M_w = 0.018015\text{ kg mol}^{-1}$ is the molar mass of water and $\bar{V}_w \approx 1.81 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}$ is its partial molar volume at skin temperature ($T = 306.15\text{ K}$, $33\,^\circ\text{C}$). Linearizing Equation 3 around the physiological viable-epidermis boundary ($a_{w,0} = 0.9946$, corresponding to $c_{\text{osm},0} = 300.6\text{ mOsm/kg}$ and $\Pi_0 = 0.76\text{ MPa}$) yields the local osmotic leverage of small water-activity perturbations transmitted across an SC + SG2 tight-junction barrier with shielding efficiency $S_{\text{SC}} \in (0, 1)$:

$$\Delta c_{\text{osm,SG}} \approx -\frac{\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}}}{M_w\,a_{w,0}} = -\frac{(1 - S_{\text{SC}})\,\Delta a_{w,\text{surf}}}{M_w\,a_{w,0}}, \qquad \left(\frac{dc_{\text{osm}}}{da_w}\right)_{\!a_{w,0}} = -55.8\ \text{Osm kg}^{-1} \qquad (4)$$

Thus, every **0.001 (0.1% RH) drop in local water activity** inside the apical stratum granulosum generates **+55.8 mOsm/kg** of equivalent extracellular hyperosmolarity ($\Delta \Pi \approx +0.14\text{ MPa}$).

### 2.3 Five-Variable Electro-Metabolic Relaxation Oscillator

To evaluate whether coupling TRPM8, cold-resistant $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ (Brock et al., 1998; Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002; Zimmermann et al., 2007; Gasperini et al., 2011), terminal and small-sensory-neuron L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels ($\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ poised in the chronically depolarized $-45\text{ to }-25\text{ mV}$ window; Scroggs & Fox, 1992; Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996; Xu & Lipscombe, 2001; Gover, Kao, & Weinreich, 2003; Lipscombe, Helton, & Xu, 2004; Bourinet et al., 2014; Isensee et al., 2021; Su et al., 2019; Li Q et al., 2019), plasma-membrane $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-ATPase extrusion (Gover, Moreira, Kao, & Weinreich, 2007) paired with high-threshold axonal mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ uniporter (MCU/MICU1) sequestration (Werth & Thayer, 1994; Nita et al., 2016; Ashrafi et al., 2020), and coupled mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ antiport (TMBIM5–MICU1; Austin et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025) produces self-sustained bursting, we formulated a 5-state ordinary differential equation (ODE) system tracking slow burst-envelope membrane potential $V$ (mV), cytosolic $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ ($c$, $\mu$M), mitochondrial matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ load $c_m$ (a.u.), cytosolic proton excess $\Delta[\text{H}^+]_i$ above baseline pH 7.30 ($H_0 = 50.12\text{ nM}$), and cooperative $\text{H}^+$-block gating $s_{\text{pH}} \in (0,1)$:

$$C_m \frac{dV}{dt} = -k \left[ I_{\text{TRPM8}}(V, T, \text{Osm}, s_{\text{pH}}) + I_{\text{Nav1.8}}(V) + I_{\text{CaL}}(V, c) + I_L(V, c) \right] \qquad (5)$$

$$\frac{dc}{dt} = k \left[ J_{\text{in,M8}} + J_{\text{in,CaL}} - J_{\text{PMCA}}(c) - J_{\text{MCU}}(c, \Psi_m) + J_{\text{m,out}}(c_m) \right] \qquad (6)$$

$$\frac{d(\Delta[\text{H}^+]_i)}{dt} = k \left( \alpha_H J_{\text{MCU}}(c, \Psi_m) - \frac{\Delta[\text{H}^+]_i}{\tau_H} \right), \qquad \frac{ds_{\text{pH}}}{dt} = k \frac{s_\infty([\text{H}^+]_i) - s_{\text{pH}}}{\tau_s} \qquad (7)$$

### 2.4 Coupled Fast $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8/\text{K}_\text{v}$ Action-Potential Spiking Layer

To visualize single-unit action-potential discharge alongside slow envelope bursting (temporal resolution $\Delta t = 5\text{ ms}$), we coupled an inhomogeneous spike generator to $V(t)$ incorporating: (i) low spontaneous resting discharge near $-46\text{ mV}$ (~0.25 Hz), (ii) steep voltage-dependent firing acceleration across $-43$ to $-24\text{ mV}$ (up to ~6 Hz), (iii) asymmetric broad $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8/\text{Ca}_\text{v}$ overshooting spikes (+48 mV) with afterhyperpolarization (AHP) and progressive $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ slow-inactivation amplitude tapering near the depolarized $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ peak ($-20$ to $-14\text{ mV}$; Blair & Bean, 2002, 2003; directly matching intraburst NTI amplitude decline and mobile terminal initiation sites recorded by Carr et al., 2003, and Carr, Pianova, McKemy, & Brock, 2009), and (iv) abrupt burst termination during mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$-driven repolarization ($\frac{dV}{dt} < -0.5\text{ mV/s}$).

### 2.6 Psychrometric Confounding and Statistical Power Simulations

To quantify ecological confounding in mobile-app studies, we simulated $N = 2{,}000$ synthetic indoor/outdoor observations across seasonal temperatures ($-10$ to $+35\,^\circ\text{C}$) using Magnus–Tetens saturation vapor pressure equations converting outdoor dew point ($T_d$) into true indoor-corrected relative humidity and local VPD (kPa). Between-subject cross-sectional power was contrasted against within-subject repeated ecological momentary assessment (EMA, 14 check-ins/person) and paired crossover trials (Protocol H2-Gel and Protocol H4) via Monte Carlo resampling.

### 2.7 Numerical Integration and Code Availability

All ODE trajectories were integrated over 420 s (300 s stimulus window, $t \in [60, 360]\text{ s}$) using a stiff Radau ordinary differential equation solver in SciPy (relative tolerance $10^{-6}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-8}$; backward differentiation formula solver for two-dimensional parameter grids). Burst peaks were quantified using automated peak detection (minimum prominence $\ge 60\text{ nM}$), and sustained limit-cycle amplitude was measured as the steady-tail $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ excursion over the final two-thirds of stimulation. ## 3. Results

### 3.1 Anatomical Asymmetry, Epidermal Water-Activity Leverage, and Worked Cutaneous Osmotic Examples

Comparing the proven corneal osmosensory interface against intact skin defines—and quantitatively resolves—the central biophysical objection to cutaneous osmosensation (**Figure 1**). In the avascular mouse and guinea-pig cornea, dense unmyelinated C- and Aδ-fiber trunks form the subbasal nerve whorl (**Figure 1A**) and project intraepithelial free terminals within microns of the evaporating tear film (**Figure 1C**), experiencing direct, unbuffered osmotic excursions (300–450+ mOsm/kg, linearly accelerating TRPM8+ cold-terminal NTI bursting immediately above 340 mOsm/kg while sparing polymodal nociceptors below 600 mOsm/kg; Parra et al., 2014) with zero microvascular perfusion (ΔFlow = 0; **Figure 1B, left**). Crucially, combined focal NTI recording and 3D confocal/immunohistochemical reconstruction across guinea-pig and mouse corneas proved that functionally identified TRPM8+ cold thermoreceptors—unlike thin ramifying TRPV1+ polymodal endings or *PIEZO2*+ mechanonociceptors (Bron, Wood, Brock, & Ivanusic, 2014; Alamri, Bron, Brock, & Ivanusic, 2015)—terminate exclusively in clustered **large *en passant* boutons and superficial bulbar "complex endings"** (Ivanusic, Wood, & Brock, 2013; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018), a specialized ultrastructure packed with dense accumulations of mitochondria (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Heppelmann et al., 2001) that places plasma-membrane TRPM8 and L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels in immediate femtoliter apposition to the MICU1/MCU/TMBIM5 proton-reset machinery.

In intact human skin (**Figure 1B, right**), ambient drying operates across a sharply stratified hydration and water-activity architecture rather than a uniform passive wall:

1. **Outer Stratum Corneum (*stratum disjunctum*):** Surface water content drops to ~15–30% w/w (Warner et al., 1988; Caspers et al., 2001) and equilibrates near ambient relative humidity ($a_w \approx 0.20\text{–}0.60$). By Equation 3, 50% RH ($a_w = 0.50$) represents an enormous thermodynamic suction ($\Pi \approx 97.5\text{ MPa}$, equivalent to $\sim 38{,}500\text{ mOsm/kg}$), rising to $\sim 226.5\text{ MPa}$ ($\sim 89{,}300\text{ mOsm/kg}$) in 20% RH heated winter air, though sequestered largely across dead lipid–keratin lamellae.
2. **Mid Stratum Corneum (NMF Osmotic Swelling Zone):** As corneocytes ascend into the intermediate $a_w = 0.80\text{–}0.95$ zone, enzymatic **filaggrin proteolysis** activates—a process shown by Scott & Harding (1986) to occur *exclusively* between 80% and 95% RH (blocked above ~95% and below ~80%). This floods mid-SC corneocytes with hygroscopic Natural Moisturizing Factor (NMF) amino acids (~20–30% dry weight; internal equivalent osmolality $\sim 2{,}850\text{–}12{,}400\text{ mOsm/kg}$).
3. **Basal Cornified Layer (*stratum compactum*, innermost 1–3 SC layers):** Electron-probe X-ray microanalysis revealed a sharp mass-fraction water discontinuity right at the SC–SG boundary (~70% w/w in viable SG dropping abruptly to ~30–40% in the lowest SC; Warner et al., 1988). Crucially, however, cryo-SEM proved that these deepest basal corneocytes **do not swell even when soaked in water** (Bouwstra et al., 2003) because high basal water activity ($a_w > 0.95$) suppresses filaggrin proteolysis (low NMF), while frozen-hydrated X-ray microanalysis showed monovalent ions ($\text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+, \text{Cl}^-$) per solvent water remain near equilibrium with the viable epidermis (von Zglinicki et al., 1993). Accordingly, mathematical skin-hydration models place the basal SC inner boundary at the physiological water-activity baseline $a_w \approx 0.9946\text{–}0.9960$ ($\approx 222\text{–}301\text{ mOsm/kg}$; Sparr & Wennerström, 2001; Sparr et al., 2013).
4. **Stratum Granulosum (SG1/SG2) — The Living Osmoregulatory & Neurosensory Frontier:** Directly beneath the basal SC, living SG keratinocytes actively transduce ambient drying: human epidermis expresses a steep upward gradient of high-affinity $\text{Na}^+/\text{Cl}^-$-coupled **taurine (*SLC6A6*/TAUT)**, betaine, and *myo*-inositol transporters peaking in the outermost granular layer (Janeke et al., 2003; Grafe et al., 2004; Warskulat et al., 2004; El-Chami et al., 2014, 2021), governed by **NFAT5 (TonEBP)** (Miyakawa et al., 1999; Choi, Lee-Kwon, & Kwon, 2020; Muhammad et al., 2021) alongside **AQP3** osmotic water channels and cell-volume mechanosensing (Sugiyama et al., 2001; Ma et al., 2002; Zijl et al., 2025). Critically, both rodent and human neuroanatomical studies place cutaneous cold sensors directly at this frontier: ultrastructural electron microscopy confirms unmyelinated human nerve endings ascending into the human stratum granulosum (Kennedy & Wendelschafer-Crabb, 1993; Hilliges, Wang, & Johansson, 1995), human intraepidermal TRPM8 nerve density quantitatively tracks skin cooling perception (Weyer-Menkhoff et al., 2019), and genetically labeled **TRPM8+ free nerve endings ascend straight into the stratum granulosum** (Dhaka et al., 2008), where **SG2 tight junctions actively prune epidermal nerve terminals** immediately *underneath* the paracellular seal in healthy skin—and lose pruning containment, breaching upward toward SG1 and the basal SC, during chronic dryness or barrier disruption (Takahashi et al., 2019).

**Worked Cutaneous Osmotic-Leverage Example (Equations 3–7).** Because water-activity leverage near physiological hydration is extraordinarily steep ($dc_{\text{osm}}/da_w = -55.8\text{ Osm/kg}$, i.e., **+55.8 mOsm/kg per $-0.001\,\Delta a_w$**; Equation 4), the stratum corneum and SG2 tight junctions can block **99.8% of the external humidity drop ($S_{\text{SC}} = 0.998$, a 500-fold attenuation)** and *still* transmit physiologically decisive hyperosmolarity to SG-terminating TRPM8+ fibers (**Table 2**):

- **Case 1 — Hydrated Skin Control ($a_{w,\text{SG}} = 0.9946$, $c_{\text{osm}} = 300.6\text{ mOsm/kg}$, $\Delta\text{Osm} = 0$):** Under our fixed ODE parameters (Table 1), hydrated skin remains quiet at warm rest (33 °C: 96.5 nM), mild cool (29 °C: 99.5 nM), room temperature (25 °C: 114.7 nM), and even 22.0 °C (Protocol H2-Gel submersion temperature: 171.5 nM), requiring cooling to **20.0 °C** before igniting rhythmic bursting (2 peaks, 490.9 nM).
- **Case 2 — Intact Skin in Moderate Dry Air (50% RH, $\Delta a_{w,\text{surf}} = -0.50$, $S_{\text{SC}} = 0.998$):** Even with 99.8% barrier shielding, the 0.2% residual leakage lowers apical SG water activity by just $\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.0010$ ($0.9946 \to 0.9936$), elevating local osmolality from $300.6 \to 356.4\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (**$\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{SG}} = +55.8\text{ mOsm/kg}$**, $\Delta V_{1/2,\text{M8}} = -40.2\text{ mV}$, equivalent to $-8.0\,^\circ\text{C}$ of thermal priming). In the exact fixed-parameter ODE, intact dry skin **stays quiet at warm rest (33 °C: 114.9 nM, $\text{pH}_i = 7.29$)**—explaining why healthy skin does not spontaneously ache or burst in warm dry rooms—yet **shifts the cutaneous bursting threshold upward by +9 °C (from 20.0 °C to 29.0 °C: 2 peaks, 487.9 nM)** and fires **4 vigorous bursts (529.0 nM, $\text{pH}_{\min} = 6.87$) upon zero-evaporation 22.0 °C submersion (Protocol H2-Gel)**, where contralateral hydrated skin remains silent (171.5 nM). Even stricter **99.85% shielding** ($\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.00075$, $\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{SG}} = +41.9\text{ mOsm/kg}$) still ignites 3 full bursts at 22–25 °C (506.7–523.6 nM).
- **Case 3 — Intact Skin in Heated Winter Indoor Air (21 °C, 20% RH, VPD = 1.99 kPa, $\Delta a_{w,\text{surf}} \approx -0.80$, same $S_{\text{SC}} = 0.998$):** The exact same 99.8% barrier yields $\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.0016$ ($a_w: 0.9946 \to 0.9930$), raising SG osmolality to $389.9\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (**$\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{SG}} = +89.3\text{ mOsm/kg}$**, $\Delta V_{1/2,\text{M8}} = -64.3\text{ mV}$). - **Case 4 — Winter Xerosis / Barrier Disruption / Atopic Dermatitis ($S_{\text{SC}} = 0.990$, Impaired SG2 Pruning):** When tight junctions weaken and intraepidermal nerves sprout past SG2 toward the basal SC (Takahashi et al., 2019), reducing shielding merely from 99.8% to 99.0% ($\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.0050$, $a_w = 0.9896$) drives apical SG osmolality to **$580.3\text{ mOsm/kg}$ ($\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{SG}} = +279.8\text{ mOsm/kg}$, $\Pi = 1.47\text{ MPa}$)**—unmasking raw corneal-scale hypertonicity and directly predicting heightened dry-cold sensitivity and neurogenic itch in barrier-impaired skin.

Within either corneal or cutaneous TRPM8+ terminals, the proposed electro-metabolic oscillator operates through three tightly coupled phases (**Figure 2**): (1) basal TRPM8 inward current holds the terminal depolarized near $-45\text{ mV}$ at the $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ activation knee while resting $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i \approx 100\text{ nM}$ keeps the MICU1 gate closed (**Figure 2A**); (2) mild cooling ($\Delta T < 0$) and/or hyperosmolarity ($+\Delta\text{Osm}$) recruits regenerative $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ action-potential bursts and small-DRG L-type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ influx, driving $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ past the ~300 nM MICU1 threshold into the 500 nM range (**Figure 2B**); and (3) rapid MCU $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ sequestration coupled to the physical **TMBIM5–MICU1** $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ exchange complex pulses cytosolic $\text{H}^+$ ($\text{pH}_i$: $7.30 \to 6.88$), selectively closing cold/icilin/osmotic TRPM8 (while sparing menthol), repolarizing $V_m$ to $-51\text{ mV}$ until PMCA/NHE clearance resets the cycle (**Figure 2C**).

Deleting *MCU* (97% reduction in uniporter conductance) **slashes mean matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ load by −97% (5.36 → 0.16 a.u.)**, protecting axons from mitochondrial fission at the explicit dynamical trade-off of abolishing rhythmic $\text{H}^+$-clocked cold adaptation ($\text{pH}_i = 7.29$ flat).

### 3.4 Microvascular Warming and Osmotic Washout Confound Cutaneous $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ Dihydropyridine Pharmacology

Testing L-type channels in perfused skin with dihydropyridines (DHPs) introduces three intersecting confounds: (1) high-affinity vascular smooth-muscle $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ ($IC_{50}\approx 10\text{–}50\text{ nM}$) vs. ~10-fold lower-affinity neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ activating ~25 mV more negative inside the $-45\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$ cold-terminal window (Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996; Xu & Lipscombe, 2001; Lipscombe, Helton, & Xu, 2004); (2) direct off-target activation of perivascular **TRPA1** by *both* DHP blockers (nifedipine, nimodipine) and agonist **Bay K 8644** (Fajardo, Meseguer, Belmonte, & Viana, 2008); and (3) bidirectional TRPM8→microvascular reflex vasodilation (>11-fold human skin conductance rise with topical menthol; Dillon, Lichter, & Alexander, 2021).

Coupling microvascular thermal and osmotic-washout dynamics into our ODE model (**Figure 5**) exposes sharp diagnostic signatures:
- **Avascular / Bath-Clamped Tissue ($\Delta\text{Flow} = 0$, Figure 5A):** With zero blood flow and background HC-030031 TRPA1 blockade, potentiating neuronal L-type channels with **(−)-(S)-Bay K 8644** converts mild osmotic bursting (+82 mOsm, 33 °C; 485.5 nM) into a **sustained non-adapting high-$\text{Ca}^{2+}$ plateau (804.7 nM, mean matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ load = 6.28 a.u.)**—mirroring classic nifedipine-sensitive L-type plateau potentials (Morisset & Nagy, 1999; Fossat et al., 2010)—because potentiated $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ sustains depolarization even after mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$ closes TRPM8. Co-applying **nifedipine** (or stereoisomer **(+)-(R)-Bay K 8644**) cleanly collapses the plateau back to quiet baseline (225.5 nM), consistent with systemic nifedipine and diltiazem preventing L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-driven TRPM8 hyperexcitability in vivo (Kawashiri et al., 2012).
- **Perfused-Skin Vasodilation Confound (Figure 5B):** On dry skin (+98 mOsm, 33 °C), a **pure vascular vasodilator (ACEi/ARB: +2.0 °C warming + 50% osmotic washout, neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}$ untouched)** completely silences bursting (101.5 nM flat), superimposing identically on low-dose perfused nifedipine (101.1 nM) even though the identical 40% partial neuronal block (0.60× baseline L-type conductance) still fires 4 vigorous bursts (376.3 nM) when perfusion is clamped.
- **Perfused-Skin Vasoconstriction Mirror Confound (Figure 5C):** From quiet mild-dry skin (+72 mOsm, 150.5 nM), **vascular-only vasoconstriction** (−2.0 °C cooling + 30% reduced washout) falsely ignites 3 full bursts (516.6 nM), while perfused Bay K 8644 jumps directly to an 873.5 nM plateau.
- **2D Neurovascular De-Confounding Map (Figure 5D):** Sweeping perfusion shift against neuronal $g_{\text{CaL}}$ separates three distinct dynamical phases (*Silent/Subthreshold*, *Rhythmic Bursting*, and *L-Type Plateau Overload*), demonstrating why the avascular central cornea—where single-terminal confocal imaging directly proved nifedipine- and diltiazem-sensitive L-type dominance of action-potential $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx (Gover, Kao, & Weinreich, 2003; cf. Mikulec et al., 1995)—the isolated skin–nerve preparation, and thermal-clamped human **DHP vs. ACEi/ARB** cohorts (Protocol H2-Gel) cleanly orthogonalize neural gating from hemodynamics.

### 3.5 Outdoor Relative Humidity Sign-Inversion and Small-$N$ Within-Subject Statistical Power

Monte Carlo psychrometric modeling ($N = 2{,}000$, **Figure 6**) uncovered a severe ecological-inference artifact: correlating *outdoor* relative humidity against indoor thermal ratings **inverts the true physical sign** ($r = -0.067, p = 0.01$) because humid 0 °C winter air (80% outdoor RH) drops to **19.7% RH ($\text{VPD} = 1.99\text{ kPa}$)** when heated indoors to 21 °C. Computing true indoor-corrected **Local VPD** from outdoor dew point cleanly recovers the underlying physical association ($r_{\text{partial}} = -0.178, p = 1.1 \times 10^{-15}$). Furthermore, eliminating between-subject clothing and baseline variance via within-subject repeated designs yields **96% statistical power at $N = 10$ participants** (14 mobile-app check-ins/person) and **80–92% power at $N = 15\text{–}20$ participants** for paired crossover challenges (Protocol H2-Gel and Protocol H4), versus $N \approx 180$ for cross-sectional single reports.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Unifying Classical Pre-Molecular and Corneal Cold-Fiber Anomalies

Our electro-metabolic relaxation oscillator provides a single quantitative mechanism for six long-orphaned observations in peripheral thermoreceptor physiology: (1) **Hensel's (1953) $\text{CO}_2$/acidosis depression** directly engages the intracellular $\text{H}^+$ brake on cold/osmotic TRPM8; (2) **Schäfer, Braun, and Isenberg's (1986) biphasic menthol anomaly** and **Swandulla et al.'s (1987) VGCC block** arise because menthol both bypasses the $\text{H}^+$ reset clock (**Figure 3H**) and interferes directly with the L-type amplification limb; (3) **Pierau et al.'s (1974) bioenergetic sensitivity** and **the dense mitochondrial packing of TRPM8+ complex bulbar endings** across cutaneous and mouse/guinea-pig corneal epithelia (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Heppelmann et al., 2001; Ivanusic, Wood, & Brock, 2013; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018) reflect strict dependence of burst termination on local mitochondrial $\Delta\Psi_m$ and $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ exchange; (4) **Brock and Carr's chronic terminal depolarization, intraburst NTI tapering, and $\text{K}^+$ dissociation** (Brock et al., 2001, 2006; Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002; Carr et al., 2003; Carr, Pianova, McKemy, & Brock, 2009) place the intact cold ending squarely inside the persistent window inward current of $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ (Schäfer, Braun, & Rempe, 1991; Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996; Xu & Lipscombe, 2001; Gover et al., 2003) and slow $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ inactivation (Blair & Bean, 2003); (5) **subtype-selective diabetic and oxaliplatin cold neuropathies**—specifically the preferential degeneration of TRPM8+ corneal cold complex endings over quiescent TRPV1+ polymodal endings in diabetic/high-fat-fed mice (Alamri, Brock, et al., 2019; Yorek MS et al., 2016) and L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-coupled TRPM8 cold hyperalgesia prevented by nifedipine or diltiazem in oxaliplatin-treated rats (Gauchan et al., 2009; Kono et al., 2012; Kawashiri et al., 2012; Su et al., 2019)—emerge when partial $\Delta\Psi_m$ collapse breaks the $\text{H}^+$ reset clock and traps tonically active cold terminals in sustained mitochondrial matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload (**Figures 3F, 4E**); and (6) **George et al.'s (2022) protective *MCU* deletion** represents an exact dynamical trade-off—sacrificing acute $\text{H}^+$-clocked oscillatory coding to abolish toxic matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ accumulation and preserve distal unmyelinated axons.

#### 4.2.1 Cutaneous Barrier Biophysics: Isotonic Ion Equilibrium vs. Water-Activity Leverage

- **The Alternative Position:** Classical skin-barrier studies argue that ambient humidity fluctuations dissipate almost entirely within the outer dead stratum corneum (SC). Blank et al. (1984) showed that steady-state transepidermal water loss (TEWL) and deep SC hydration profiles change modestly across 0–80% RH because surface drying sharply reduces outer SC water diffusivity. Warner, Myers, and Taylor (1988) measured a steep water mass-fraction jump (~70% w/w to ~35% w/w) at the SC–stratum granulosum (SG) interface, attributing it to lipid–keratin partitioning rather than hypertonicity, and von Zglinicki et al. (1993) found via frozen-hydrated X-ray microanalysis that $\text{Na}^+$, $\text{K}^+$, and $\text{Cl}^-$ per unit water stay near dermal equilibrium—setting tight bounds against a standing macroscopic salt lake at SG1/SG2. Furthermore, claudin-1/claudin-4 tight junctions at SG2 restrict paracellular ions far more strongly than water (Furuse et al., 2002; Kirschner et al., 2013), non-peptidergic *Mrgprd*+ nociceptors densely innervate the outer SG (Dussor et al., 2008), and thermo-mechanical hygrosensation models attribute human wetness and dry-cold perception purely to evaporative heat transfer ($L_v \approx 2.45\text{ MJ/kg}$) plus tactile A$\beta$/mechanoreceptor input (Filingeri et al., 2014; Typolt & Filingeri, 2020).
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Ion-blocking, water-permeable SG2 tight junctions *create* trans-membrane chemical-potential suction:** Kirschner et al.'s (2013) finding that SG2 claudin-1/4 seals block paracellular $\text{Na}^+/\text{Cl}^-/\text{Ca}^{2+}$ diffusion while permitting trans-epidermal water flux does not refute osmotic transduction—it explains *why* evaporative water loss lowers local trans-membrane water chemical potential ($\mu_w = \mu_w^\circ + RT\ln a_w$, Equation 3) and shrinks AQP3-expressing apical cells without requiring bulk interstitial $\text{NaCl}$ accumulation. By Equation 4 ($dc_{\text{osm}}/da_w = -55.8\text{ Osm/kg}$), 99.8% dissipation across the SC ($\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.001$) still exerts $+0.14\text{ MPa}$ ($+55.8\text{ mOsm/kg}$ equivalent) osmotic suction across membranes at the SG2 plane.
  2. **Living outer-SG keratinocytes prove the osmotic signal arrives in vivo:** If the viable SG remained constitutively isotonic in dry air, outer-SG keratinocytes would not mount an energy-intensive osmoregulatory response to low ambient humidity. Instead, experimental dry air (silica gel) and mild hyperosmolarity (~405 mOsm/L) induce 3- to 5-fold upregulation of $\text{Na}^+/\text{Cl}^-$-coupled **taurine (*SLC6A6*/TAUT)**, betaine, and *myo*-inositol transporters peaking specifically in the outermost granular layer (Janeke et al., 2003; Grafe et al., 2004; Warskulat et al., 2004), activate **NFAT5/TonEBP** to preserve epidermal integrity (Miyakawa et al., 1999; Choi, Lee-Kwon, & Kwon, 2020; Muhammad et al., 2021), upregulate **AQP3** osmotic water channels (Sugiyama et al., 2001; Ma et al., 2002) that govern keratinocyte volume-regulated differentiation (Zijl et al., 2025), and drive osmolyte-induced reinforcement of claudin-1/4 tight junctions themselves (El-Chami et al., 2021)—while acute barrier disruption collapses the steep outer-SG $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{K}^+$ gradients measured by PIXE (Mauro et al., 1998).
  3. **Human intraepidermal TRPM8 anatomy and zero-evaporation anomalies:** Ultrastructural EM proves human unmyelinated nerve endings reach the granular layer (Kennedy & Wendelschafer-Crabb, 1993; Hilliges, Wang, & Johansson, 1995), human skin biopsy quantification proves **intraepidermal TRPM8**—and *not* TRPA1—tracks human cooling perception (Weyer-Menkhoff et al., 2019), SG2 tight junctions actively prune intraepidermal fibers (Takahashi et al., 2019), and evaporative heat-loss models cannot explain isothermal somatic DRG osmosensation (Quallo et al., 2015) or non-evaporative oil-droplet TRPM8 shaking (Selescu et al., 2025). **Protocol H2-Gel** ($J_{\text{evap}} \equiv 0$, IR-$T$ matched submersion, where hydrated skin conducts heat *faster* physically; **Table 8**) decisively separates the two models.

#### 4.2.2 Pacemaker Architecture: HCN1 / $\text{K}^+$ Brakes / T-Type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}3$ vs. Depolarized Terminal L-Type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$

- **The Alternative Position:** In the canonical Hodgkin–Huxley / Huber–Braun cold-receptor literature, rhythmic firing and threshold diversity are governed by **HCN1 ($I_h$)** (setting 5–7 Hz subthreshold resonance and interburst timing; Orio et al., 2009, 2012) balanced against **$\text{K}_\text{v}1.1/1.2$ ($I_{\text{KD}}$)** (Viana et al., 2002; Madrid et al., 2009), two-pore $\text{K}_{2\text{P}}$ channels **TREK-1, TRAAK, TREK-2, and TASK-3** (Noël et al., 2009; Morenilla-Palao et al., 2014; Pereira et al., 2014; De-la-Torre et al., 2026), **$\text{K}_\text{v}7$/KCNQ M-currents** (Vetter et al., 2013; Abd-Elsayed et al., 2015), small-conductance SK2 channels (Maingret et al., 2008), sex-divergent $I_h/I_K$ expression (Caudle et al., 2017), and $\text{K}^+$-channel-enriched $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$-negative cold neurons (Luiz et al., 2019), simulated without L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels or mitochondria (Olivares et al., 2015). Classical somatic DRG voltage-clamp classifies L-type current as high-voltage-activated (>−10 mV) while assigning subthreshold $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ entry to T-type ($\text{Ca}_\text{v}3.2$) channels (Nowycky, Fox, & Tsien, 1985; Fox, Nowycky, & Tsien, 1987; Rozanski et al., 2013), and Mikulec et al. (1995) reported in their title that diltiazem "spares" rabbit corneal A$\delta$ and C-fiber cold receptors.
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Somatic holding potentials (−70 mV) miss chronic terminal depolarization ($\ge -40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$):** Prolonged (30–60 s) constant-current polarization of intact guinea-pig corneal nerve terminals proved that cold endings sit **chronically depolarized ($\ge -40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$) at physiological resting temperature** (Brock, Pianova, & Belmonte, 2001; Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002). Across $-40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$, canonical T-type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}3$ channels are **steady-state inactivated** (inactivating across $-60\text{ to }-40\text{ mV}$; Fox, Nowycky, & Tsien, 1987), whereas neuronal **$\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$** activates ~25 mV more hyperpolarized than $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ (Xu & Lipscombe, 2001) and adult rat sensory neurons express **low-voltage-activated, dihydropyridine-sensitive L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels open between action potentials** that directly regulate burst frequency (Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996—fulfilling the terminal low-threshold $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ conductance deduced by Schäfer, Braun, & Rempe, 1991).
  2. **Single-terminal optical $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ imaging and primary inspection of Mikulec et al. (1995):** When $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ is imaged directly inside single intact corneal nerve terminals rather than somata, electrically evoked $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ transients are blocked predominantly by **nifedipine and diltiazem** (Gover, Kao, & Weinreich, 2003). Moreover, inspection of the concentration–response data inside Mikulec et al. (1995) shows that 10–250 µM diltiazem actually produced a **concentration-dependent depression of rabbit corneal cold-fiber discharge** (while sparing A$\delta$ mechanoreceptors, and while T-type blocker $\text{Ni}^{2+}$ had no significant effect). Likewise, Vetter et al. (2013) demonstrated that $\text{K}_\text{v}7$/M-current blockade sensitizes high-threshold cold nociceptors while **leaving low-threshold corneal cold thermoreceptors completely unaffected**.
  3. **Timescale complementarity:** $I_h$ (5–7 Hz subthreshold resonance, ~150 ms period; Orio et al., 2009) and $I_{\text{KD}}$/TASK-3 set baseline excitability and fast intraburst interspike intervals (represented in $g_L, E_L$), whereas purely electrical $I_h/\text{K}^+$ models cannot account for multi-second (5–60 s) burst arrest by metabolic poisons (Pierau et al., 1974), $\text{CO}_2$/intracellular $\text{H}^+$ (Hensel, 1953; Andersson et al., 2004), or the $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-reversible menthol paradox (Schäfer, Braun, & Isenberg, 1986).

#### 4.2.3 Burst-Termination Clock: $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-PLC$\delta$ $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ Depletion & PMCA $\text{H}^+$ Import vs. Mitochondrial TMBIM5–MICU1

- **The Alternative Position:** Textbook TRPM8 desensitization is mediated by $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx activating $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-sensitive PLC$\delta$ to deplete membrane $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$, shifting $V_{1/2}$ positively (Liu & Qin, 2005; Rohacs et al., 2005; Daniels, Takashima, & McKemy, 2009; Yudin et al., 2011; cryo-EM structures in Yin et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2026). Separately, in rat trigeminal ganglion somata, Hwang et al. (2011) attributed $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-evoked intracellular acidification and action-potential inhibition primarily to **electroneutral PMCA ($\text{Ca}^{2+}/2\text{H}^+$) counter-transport** (recovered via NHE1/pNBC1), Poburko, Santo Domingo, and Demaurex (2011) showed cytosolic PMCA acid generation secondarily acidifies mitochondria, extracellular acidification inhibits both menthol and icilin in FLIPR assays (Behrendt et al., 2004; cf. Kühn, Kühn, & Lückhoff, 2009), and mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ antiport was debated between LETM1 (Shao et al., 2016) and TMBIM5 (Austin et al., 2022).
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Three qualitative falsifiers of pure $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ depletion:** First, $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ depletion desensitizes **cold and menthol via a shared conformational pathway** (Rohacs et al., 2005; Yudin et al., 2011; Yin et al., 2024), whereas intracellular acidification ($\text{pH}_i$ 7.3 → 6.8) **potently inhibits cold and icilin gating while sparing menthol** (Andersson, Chase, & Bevan, 2004—consistent with cryo-EM evidence that menthol allosterically stabilizes $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ engagement against rundown, Kim et al., 2026). Second, PLC$\delta$ hydrolysis is independent of mitochondrial $\Delta\Psi_m$, whereas cold-terminal endings are densely packed with mitochondria (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018) and DRG $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-evoked cytosolic acidification ($74 \to 107\text{ nM } [\text{H}^+]_i$) is abolished by mitochondrial poisons (Werth & Thayer, 1994; Thayer & Wang, 1995). Third, macroscopic $\text{PIP}_2$ depletion studies apply intense cold ($10\text{–}18\,^\circ\text{C}$) or saturating menthol ($100\text{–}500\,\mu\text{M}$), whereas physiological osmo-thermal bursting operates near threshold ($30\text{–}34\,^\circ\text{C}$, $300\text{–}515\text{ nM } [\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$).
  2. **Dynamical necessity of the cooperative >300 nM MICU1/TMBIM5 threshold over linear PMCA:** Gover et al. (2007) and Hwang et al. (2011) establish that PMCA extrudes $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ (and imports $\text{H}^+$) **linearly** from resting levels (~100 nM). In nonlinear dynamics, an unthresholded linear negative feedback damps perturbations monotonically to a stable fixed point rather than generating multi-second relaxation bursts. Self-sustained relaxation oscillation requires a **steeply cooperative delayed threshold**—precisely supplied when burst $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ crosses the ~300 nM MICU1/MICU3 gate ($n \approx 5$; Werth & Thayer, 1994; Logan et al., 2014; Ashrafi et al., 2020) to trigger TMBIM5–MICU1 mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ antiport (Austin et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025) in femtoliter boutons where matrix $\text{H}^+$ buffering (~5 mM) is 4-fold lower than cytosol (~20 mM; Poburko et al., 2011). **Falsification #6** (**Table 8**; simultaneous $\text{pH}_i$ + $\text{PIP}_2$ imaging under Ru360 vs. PMCA block vs. $\text{diC}_8\text{-PIP}_2$) resolves the exact quantitative shares.

#### 4.2.4 Neuropathic Cold Allodynia: Silent $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$+ Nociceptors, TRPV1 Co-Expression & Hemoglobin Acidosis vs. TRPM8 Matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ Overload

- **The Alternative Position:** Influential pain studies argue neuropathic cold allodynia arises outside canonical TRPM8 mitochondrial cycling: in vivo DRG imaging shows oxaliplatin, nerve injury, and ciguatoxin unmask **silent *Trpm8*-negative $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$+ cold nociceptors** via $\text{K}_\text{v}1$ brake loss (MacDonald et al., 2021); oxaliplatin binding to hemoglobin impairs proton buffering, causing bulk DRG acidosis that sensitizes **TRPA1** and is reversed by acetazolamide or topiramate (Potenzieri et al., 2020); corneal dry-eye cold nociception requires **TRPV1 co-expression** and substance P release within TRPM8+ fibers (Li F et al., 2019); human diabetic skin biopsies show weak cross-sectional correlation between fiber loss and pain (Sorensen, Molyneaux, & Yue, 2006); and George et al. (2022) assayed mechanical rather than cold allodynia in diabetic *MCU*-KO mice.
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Proton-gating sign separation cleanly partitions TRPA1 noxious cold pain from L-type/TRPM8 cool dysesthesia:** Because intracellular acidosis **inhibits cold/osmotic TRPM8** (Andersson et al., 2004) while sensitizing TRPA1/ASICs, Potenzieri et al.'s (2020) bulk hemoglobin-buffering acidosis explains *noxious icy (<15 °C) TRPA1 allodynia* alongside silent $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$+ nociceptor recruitment (MacDonald et al., 2021) and inflammatory TRPV1 co-release (Li F et al., 2019), **whereas** innocuous cool/evaporative dysesthesia (20–28 °C) requires TRPM8 upregulation (Gauchan et al., 2009; Kono et al., 2012) and is **prevented in vivo by nifedipine and diltiazem via L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$/NFAT signaling** (Kawashiri et al., 2012).
  2. **Biphasic dynamical trajectory resolves Sorensen et al. (2006) and subtype-selective diabetic loss (Alamri, Brock, et al., 2019):** Our ODE predicts a **two-stage metabolic trajectory**: mild early $\Delta\Psi_m$ decline ($\Psi_m = 0.85 \to 0.42$) erases the $\text{H}^+$ reset clock and locks intact terminals into hyperexcitable tonic $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ firing (painful early dysesthesia *before* fiber loss; Sorensen et al., 2006), which subsequently drives chronic mitochondrial matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload ($c_m = 5.36\text{ a.u.}$), Drp1 fission (George et al., 2022), and **preferential dying-back of tonically cycling TRPM8+ corneal complex endings ahead of quiescent (<1 Hz) TRPV1-only endings** (Alamri, Brock, et al., 2019; Yorek MS et al., 2016).

#### 4.2.5 Human Genetics: *TRPM8* SNP Meta-Analyses, Menthol Normosensitivity in Dry Eye & Silent *MICU1*/*TMBIM5* Charts

- **The Alternative Position:** While *TRPM8* rs10166942 tracks latitudinal adaptation, DRG *TRPM8* expression, chronic migraine allodynia, and cold pain in several cohorts (Chasman et al., 2011; Key et al., 2018; Gavva et al., 2019; Ling et al., 2019; cf. *TRPM8*/*F2RL1* cold-pain SNPs in Soeda et al., 2021), a migraine meta-analysis noted heterozygous CT risk complexity (Siokas et al., 2022), and Kaido et al. (2021) found **menthol cold detection thresholds did not differ** between dry-eye patients and healthy controls despite heightened qualitative unpleasantness. Furthermore, *SCN10A* SFN cohorts (3.7% of 1,139 patients; Han et al., 2014; Eijkenboom et al., 2019) report warmth/burning pain more frequently than isolated cold allodynia, and human *MICU1* (Kohlschmidt et al., 2021; Beheshti et al., 2025), *TMBIM5* (Zhang et al., 2022), and *LETM1* (Kaiyrzhanov et al., 2022) phenotyping highlights myopathy and neurological deficits without spontaneous cold complaints.
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Unchanged menthol threshold in dry eye (Kaido et al., 2021) is a *positive* prediction of our model:** Because menthol **bypasses both osmotic gating and intracellular $\text{H}^+$ negative feedback** (Andersson et al., 2004; Kim et al., 2026; **Figure 3H**, **Figure 8A–D**), probing dry-eye corneas with menthol circumvents the exact osmo-metabolic burst loop sensitized by tear hyperosmolarity—producing normal detection threshold alongside tonic plateau unpleasantness. Meanwhile, unlike non-coding tag SNPs subject to ancestry-dependent linkage disequilibrium (Siokas et al., 2022), rare **coding GoF variants** in both *TRPM8* (R30Q, D665N, V915M; Gualdani et al., 2021; Ghovanloo et al., 2024) and *SCN10A* (G1662S, I206M; Han et al., 2014; Chidiac et al., 2021; Ghovanloo et al., 2026) reproducibly trigger corneal post-LASIK pain and cooling hyperexcitability.
  2. **Why *MICU1* LoF predicts *blunted* cold adaptation, not spontaneous cold pain:** In **Table 4** and **Figure 4D**, *MICU1* LoF produces constitutive resting matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload (25× WT) that **collapses oscillatory cold-burst amplitude by −91%** via premature proton leak—predicting subtle *blunting* of dynamic cold/wetness adaptation (consistent with impaired pain perception noted in *MICU1* deficiency by Kohlschmidt et al., 2021) that standard neuromuscular exams never assay without targeted QST.

#### 4.2.6 Clinical Pharmacology: Hemodynamic Raynaud's CCB Trials & Sign–Symptom Dissociation in Topical TRPM8 Trials

- **The Alternative Position:** Cochrane systematic reviews find that oral $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel blockers confer only modest benefit in primary Raynaud's phenomenon (SMD ≈ 0.23; Ennis et al., 2016; moderate efficacy across 38 primary/secondary trials in Rirash et al., 2017), classically attributed purely to vascular smooth-muscle $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ vasodilation, while ACEi/ARBs have been tested for vasa nervorum blood flow in diabetic rats (Coppey et al., 2006) rather than human cold sensitivity. In dry eye, topical TRPM8 agonism relieved neuropathic pain in a 15-patient open pilot (Yoon et al., 2021), missed co-primary endpoints in Phase 2b COMET-1 despite 0.003% activity (Wirta et al., 2022), and won FDA approval (Lee, 2025) after Phase 3 COMET-2/COMET-3 ($N = 931$; Pattar et al., 2026) met primary Schirmer tearing endpoints (42.6–53.2% vs. 8.2–14.4%) while achieving statistically significant SANDE symptom separation in COMET-2 but not COMET-3.
- **Mechanistic Rebuttal:**
  1. **Quantitative explanation of systemic DHP noise (**Figure 5B**):** Oral low-dose DHPs preferentially dilate vascular $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ ($IC_{50} \approx 10\text{–}50\text{ nM}$, warming skin and washing out epidermal hyperosmolarity) while exerting incomplete block on ~20-fold less sensitive neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ (Xu & Lipscombe, 2001) and paradoxically *activating* perivascular nociceptive TRPA1 (Fajardo et al., 2008)—quantitatively explaining why systemic CCB sensory outcomes are compressed in perfused skin and why **avascular corneal NTI pharmacology** and **IR-temperature/PPG-clamped DHP vs. ACEi/ARB crossovers** (Protocol H2-Gel, **Table 8**) are required to isolate the neuronal L-type limb.
  2. **Autonomic tearing vs. peripheral burst-to-tonic sensory accommodation:** Basal/bursting TRPM8 afferent inflow drives a high-gain brainstem lacrimal reflex (Parra et al., 2010; Pattar et al., 2026), whereas sustained synthetic agonist occupancy pushes chronically depolarized corneal terminals across the burst-to-tonic bifurcation (Carr et al., 2003) into $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ slow inactivation and $\text{H}^+/\text{PIP}_2$ sensory accommodation—explaining strong, reproducible Schirmer tearing alongside faster accommodation of subjective ocular discomfort across COMET-1/2/3.

#### 4.2.7 Head-to-Head 6-ODE Rival Benchmarks (Linear PMCA, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-PLC$\delta$ $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$, and T-Type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}3.2$ vs. TMBIM5–MICU1)

To convert Subsections 4.2.2 and 4.2.3 from qualitative dialectics into quantitative dynamical tests, we integrated a unified 6-state ODE (**Figure 7A–D**) pitting our cooperative TMBIM5–MICU1 $\text{H}^+$ oscillator (**M1**, $n = 5$, $\alpha_H = 16.5$) directly against matched linear PMCA $\text{Ca}^{2+}/2\text{H}^+$ import alone (**R1**, $n = 1$, $\alpha_H = 16.5$; Gover et al., 2007; Hwang et al., 2011), $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-PLC$\delta$ $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ depletion alone (**R2**, $k_{\text{PLC}} = 0.11\text{ s}^{-1}$; Rohacs et al., 2005; Yudin et al., 2011; Yin et al., 2024), pure $I_h + I_{\text{KD}} + \text{T-type Ca}_\text{v}3.2$ gating without L-type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ (**R3**; Fox, Nowycky, & Tsien, 1987; Orio et al., 2009), and a coupled physiological hybrid (**M2**: TMBIM5–MICU1 $\text{H}^+$ + linear PMCA $\text{H}^+$ + basal PLC$\delta$ $\text{PIP}_2$). Under identical isothermal hyperosmolar drive (33 °C, +98 mOsm), **M1** generates 3 self-sustained relaxation bursts (252.4 nM amplitude, $\text{pH}_{\min} = 6.88$), whereas **R1** stalls in a damped 667.5 nM plateau (0 bursts, $\text{pH}_{\min} = 7.07$; **Figure 7A**), **R3** stalls subthreshold at 243.8 nM because intact terminal depolarization ($\ge -40\text{ mV}$; Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002) steady-state inactivates $\text{Ca}_\text{v}3.2$, and two-dimensional $(n, \alpha_H)$ bifurcation mapping (**Figure 7B**) confirms a Hopf cooperativity boundary ($n \ge 2.0$) below which unthresholded linear $n = 1$ proton feedback yields 0 sustained bursts across physiological gains (while artificially inflating PMCA proton gain 3-fold to $\alpha_H = 48$ fails empirically by firing 6 rapid bursts during FCCP uncoupling at $\Psi_m = 0.05$). Likewise, **R2** ($\text{PIP}_2$-only clock; **Figure 7C**) fails two orthogonal benchmarks: it adapts identically during FCCP uncoupling ($\Psi_m = 0.05$, $\text{pH}_{\min} = 7.30$) and desensitizes 35 µM menthol (531.0 nM, $\text{PIP}_{2,\min} = 0.33$) symmetrically with osmo-cold (Yin et al., 2024), contradicting both mitochondrial poison sensitivity (Pierau et al., 1974; Werth & Thayer, 1994) and menthol $\text{H}^+$ sparing (792.8 nM tonic plateau in M1; Andersson et al., 2004; Kim et al., 2026). In the full **M2** hybrid (**Figure 7D**), clamping membrane $\text{PIP}_2$ via intracellular $\text{diC}_8\text{-PIP}_2$ dialysis preserves **97% of sustained burst amplitude** (254.4 vs. 261.2 nM, 3 bursts), whereas clamping cytosolic $\text{pH}_i$ with 50 mM pipette HEPES (0 bursts, 704.3 nM plateau, $\text{pH}_{\min} = 7.23$) or blocking MCU with Ru360 (0 bursts, 755.6 nM plateau, $\text{pH}_{\min} = 7.22$) completely abolishes rhythmic reset despite ongoing $\text{PIP}_2$ hydrolysis ($\text{PIP}_{2,\min} = 0.71$).

#### 4.2.8 Empirical OpenMM/PROPKA Electrostatic $\text{p}K_a$ Analysis Across 11 Cryo-EM TRPM8 States, Tetrameric Wyman Proton Linkage, and Single-Residue Kill-Switches

At the atomistic scale, brute-force explicit-solvent molecular dynamics (MD) of the intact mitochondrial reset supercomplex remains premature because mammalian TMBIM5 (UniProt Q9H3K2) currently lacks an experimental cryo-EM structure, contains an intrinsically disordered N-terminal matrix tail (residues 1–113, pLDDT < 50), and co-assembles with MICU1 (UniProt Q9BPX6, disordered N-tail 1–185) and the >600 kDa MCU–EMRE–MICU1–MICU2 uniplex (PDB 6WDN; Fan et al., 2020) across curved cardiolipin cristae junctions at unresolved quaternary stoichiometry (Zhang et al., 2025). By contrast, empirical continuum-electrostatic $\text{p}K_a$ and Wyman proton-linkage calculations on experimental cryo-EM gating states of TRPM8 and the rigid high-confidence 7-TM core of AlphaFold v6 human TMBIM5 (residues 114–306, pLDDT > 90; Jumper et al., 2021; Varadi et al., 2024) are immediately tractable and directly test whether state-dependent histidine and aspartate $\text{p}K_a$ shifts reproduce physiological proton gating (**Figure 7E–F**, **Figure 8A–F**, **Tables 6–7**).

Although Andersson, Chase, and Bevan (2004) established that intracellular acidification inhibits cold- and icilin-activated TRPM8 with $\text{pH}_{1/2} \approx 7.13$ while sparing menthol, no study has mutated or electrostatically mapped TRPM8's intracellular proton sensor. Across experimental human TRPM8 structures, three transmembrane-domain histidines face the extracellular loops (His761, His765, His818; OpenMM/PROPKA $\text{p}K_a = 5.95\text{–}7.23$), leaving **only two conserved cytoplasmic histidine hubs** wired directly into channel gating (**Figure 7E**): (i) **His845** at the cytoplasmic S4 base (**3.24 Å** from gating sensor **Arg842**, **4.55 Å** from **Asp796**, **6.48 Å** from $\text{Ca}^{2+}$/icilin co-agonist **Asp802** in 8BDC; **5.11 Å** from TRP-helix **Glu1004** in AF-Q7Z2W7), and (ii) **His667** on cytoplasmic MHR4/pre-S1, forming a **2.96 Å** H-bond (2.87 Å in 9ZCU) with TRP-box **Gln992** (3 residues from $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$-coordinating **Lys995**), a **3.01 Å** salt-bridge/H-bond with **Glu622**, and sitting **3.26 Å** from post-LASIK corneal pain locus **Asp665** (D665N; Ghovanloo et al., 2024). Crucially, inspecting 8BDC (2.65 Å; Palchevskyi et al., 2023) against 9ZCU (2.86 Å; Choi, Lin, Cheng, & Julius, 2026) alone compares two **closed, ligand-free** human conformations (His845 OpenMM $\text{p}K_a = 4.66$ vs. $5.26$; His667 $\text{p}K_a = 6.28$ vs. $6.14$). We therefore performed PDBFixer sidechain reconstruction (restoring 17–27 $\text{C}_\beta$-stubbed surface/cavity sidechains in cryo-EM deposits), backbone-restrained OpenMM Amber14/OBC2 energy minimization (Eastman et al., 2017), and PROPKA 3.5 + Debye–Hückel cofactor evaluation (Olsson et al., 2011; Søndergaard et al., 2011) across **all 11 human cell-vesicle and mouse nanodisc cryo-EM gating conformations**—which share 100% conserved identity and numbering across all gating residues—alongside **human TMBIM5** (**Table 6**, **Figure 8**):

1. **Complementary Two-Hub Proton Gate in TRPM8 — His667 Sets the Primary $\text{pH}_{1/2} \approx 7.1$ Closure Midpoint While His845 Mediates Menthol $\text{H}^+$ Sparing:** Because **His845** is 62–95% buried inside the lower-S4/VSLD pocket adjacent to cationic Arg842, desolvation and Coulombic repulsion depress its closed apo $\text{p}K_a$ to **4.66–5.26** (**Figure 8A–B**). Conversely, solvent-exposed **His667** (12–39% buried at the pre-S1/TRP-box latch) shifts upward during channel closure/desensitization: from **6.01** in human 4 °C $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-free cold-activated TRPM8 (**9ZF0**) and **6.29** in nanodisc C3+$\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ open TRPM8 (**8E4L**; Yin et al., 2022) up to **6.27** in human 4 °C desensitized (**9ZCV**) and **7.05** in nanodisc $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-desensitized TRPM8 (**9VNA**, rising from 6.56 pre-minimization upon local relaxation of the Glu622–Asp665–His667–Gln992 triad; Kim et al., 2026)—matching Andersson et al.'s (2004) experimental $\text{pH}_{1/2} = 7.13$ within **0.08 pH units** (**Figure 8B**). By Wyman's thermodynamic linkage theorem across a symmetric 4-subunit homotetramer:

$$K_{\text{app}}(\text{pH}_i) = K_0 \left( \frac{1 + 10^{\text{p}K_a^{\text{O}} - \text{pH}_i}}{1 + 10^{\text{p}K_a^{\text{D}} - \text{pH}_i}} \right)^{\!4}, \qquad \Delta\Delta G_{\text{tet}}(\text{pH}_i) = -4RT \ln\!\left( \frac{1 + 10^{\text{p}K_a^{\text{D}} - \text{pH}_i}}{1 + 10^{\text{p}K_a^{\text{O}} - \text{pH}_i}} \right) \qquad (8)$$

the empirical His667 shift ($\text{p}K_a^{\text{O}} = 6.01\text{–}6.29 \to \text{p}K_a^{\text{D}} = 7.05$) stabilizes the closed/desensitized TRP-box state by **$+1.61\text{ to }+1.86\text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$ (a 15- to 23-fold shut bias) specifically at burst-peak $\text{pH}_i = 6.88$** (**Figure 8D, F**). Meanwhile, **His845** resolves the 22-year-old menthol paradox: whereas anionic $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ ($-4e$) upshifts His845 by $+1.40$ pH units in open 8E4L ($\text{p}K_a = 6.21$ apo, $6.04$ holo), **menthol binding inside the VSLD depresses His845 $\text{p}K_a$ to 4.23 in human 4 °C vesicles (9PB5, $\Delta\text{p}K_a = -1.24$ vs. 9ZF0) and 4.17 apo / 3.22 holo in 2.90 Å nanodisc 9VJQ** (where VSLD-bound $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ adds $-1.43\text{ to }-2.16$ pH units of repulsion; **Figure 8A–C**), expelling protons from S4 while allosterically clamping $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ against His667-mediated TRP-box rundown ($+0.01\text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$ at $\text{pH}_i = 6.88$; **Figure 8D, F**; consistent with His845Ala abolishing icilin gating while sparing menthol in Kim et al., 2026).
2. **Human TMBIM5 Asp294–Asp325 ($\text{p}K_a = 4.84 / 9.60$) Functions as an Electrostatic Carboxyl "Proton Cannon":** Within the OpenMM-minimized 7-TM core of human TMBIM5 (AF-Q9H3K2, residues 110–345; **Figure 7E**, **Figure 8E**), burying the **3.27 Å Asp294–Asp325** carboxyl–carboxyl dyad inside the low-dielectric transmembrane pore (80.0% and 90.7% burial, bridged by Ser321 at 2.68 Å and Lys297 at 3.24 Å) splits their $\text{p}K_a$ values by **4.76 pH units**: **Asp294** remains ionized ($\text{p}K_a = 4.84$, $-\text{COO}^-$) as a high-affinity $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ bait, whereas **Asp325** upshifts by **$+5.80$ pH units** above bulk aspartate (3.80) to **$\text{p}K_a = 9.60$**—holding Asp325 **99.6% protonated ($-\text{COOH}$) across physiological mitochondrial matrix/IMS pH (7.0–7.8)**. Coordinating a divalent $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ion ($+2e$) at the dyad midpoint collapses Asp325 $\text{p}K_a$ below 0 ($-8.06$), **obligatorily ejecting a stoichiometric proton ($\text{H}^+$)** upon MICU1-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ entry and explaining atomistically why pore-dead *TMBIM5* Asp294Arg/Asp325Arg (Zhang et al., 2025) flattens cytosolic $\text{pH}_i$ at 7.24 ($+0.14\text{ kcal mol}^{-1}$ residual closure work; **Figure 8F**, **Table 7**).

### 4.4 Peripheral Molecular Specialization Across Cornea, Palpebra, and Skin

Should regional or temporal dissociations survive Falsifications 1–7, four peripheral molecular modifiers can tune the identical *Trpm8* locus differently across adjacent territories: (1) **TRPM8 Channel-Associated Factors (*TCAF1*/*TCAF2*)**, which exert opposing potentiating/inhibitory control over TRPM8 trafficking and gating (Gkika et al., 2015) and underwent human-specific duplication and divergent natural selection (Hsieh, Eichler et al., 2021); (2) **Asn934 N-glycosylation and lipid-raft partitioning**, which shift TRPM8 $V_{1/2}$ by +60 mV and cold threshold by ~6 °C (Morenilla-Palao et al., 2009; Pertusa et al., 2012; Alcalde et al., 2018; Piña et al., 2019); (3) **short splice isoforms (4TM-TRPM8/sM8)** regulating ER–mitochondria $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ transfer (Bidaux et al., 2016); and (4) **local terminal morphology and channel-exchanger stoichiometry**—contrasting superficial mitochondria-packed bulbar complex endings in mouse and guinea-pig cornea (Ivanusic, Wood, & Brock, 2013; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018) against SG2-pruned cutaneous endings ($\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$, $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$, $I_{\text{KD}}$, TREK-1/TRAAK [Noël et al., 2009], TASK-1/3 [Morenilla-Palao et al., 2014], $\text{K}_\text{v}7$/M-channels that selectively brake cold nociceptors while sparing corneal cold thermoreceptors [Vetter et al., 2013], HCN1/2, MICU1/MCU, and TMBIM5/LETM1).

### 4.5 Methodological Limitations

Four limitations bound the present computational work and explicitly motivate independent experimental testing: (1) **Single-Compartment Bulbar Approximation:** Although serial-EM and 3D confocal reconstructions confirm that cold-receptor terminals form restricted ~1–3 µm mitochondria-packed bulbar swellings that favor tight femtoliter $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ coupling (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018), the 5-variable ODE omits multi-branch spatial electrotonic cable summation and mobile spike-initiation shifts across the terminal cluster (Carr, Pianova, McKemy, & Brock, 2009); (2) **Phenomenological Fast-Spiking Layer:** Action potentials in Figure 3A–B are coupled unidirectionally to the slow envelope $V(t)$ rather than solved via full multi-timescale Hodgkin–Huxley conductance equations, though the qualitative frequency-acceleration and $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ slow-inactivation tapering mirror recorded corneal NTI burst structure (Carr et al., 2003; Blair & Bean, 2003); (3) **Lumped Baseline $\text{H}^+$ Coupling, Continuum SC Shielding, and Static Continuum Electrostatics:** While Section 4.2.7 and Section 4.2.8 explicitly extend the baseline 5-ODE system to benchmark coupled 6-ODE PMCA/$\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ dynamics and static OpenMM/PROPKA $\text{p}K_a$ shifts across 11 cryo-EM conformations, explicit-lipid constant-pH molecular dynamics of the intact TMBIM5–MICU1–MCU cristae supercomplex awaits experimental structural resolution of mammalian TMBIM5 (Falsification 6), and the trans-epidermal barrier is summarized via effective water-activity shielding $S_{\text{SC}}$ rather than a full nonlinear moving-boundary lipid–corneocyte diffusion PDE (Sparr & Wennerström, 2001; Falsification 1); and (4) **Theoretical Scope:** No new human psychophysical or wet-lab electrophysiological recordings were collected in this study; all behavioral and pharmacological predictions are placed openly into the public domain for empirical confirmation or refutation under the pre-specified criteria in **Table 8**.
