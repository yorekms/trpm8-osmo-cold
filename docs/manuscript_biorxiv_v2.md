## Significance Statement

Textbook models treat cold perception as a monotonic TRPM8 labeled line and skin wetness as pure evaporative cooling. Yet seventy years of pre-molecular cold-fiber recordings revealed endogenous, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-controlled, metabolically sensitive, $\text{CO}_2$-inhibited burst oscillators, while modern studies proved TRPM8 senses extracellular hyperosmolarity in both corneal and dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons. Here, we show thermodynamically that because water-activity leverage at physiological hydration is steep ($\approx -55.8\text{ Osm/kg}$ per unit $a_w$), even a **99.8%-shielding stratum corneum + stratum granulosum (SG2) tight-junction barrier** still transmits $+56\text{ to }+89\text{ mOsm/kg}$ of evaporative hypertonicity directly to intraepidermal TRPM8+ terminals. Embedding this interface into a fixed-parameter electro-metabolic relaxation oscillator ($\text{TRPM8} - \text{Na}_\text{v}1.8 - \text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/1.3 - \text{MICU1/MCU/TMBIM5 } \text{H}^+$) reproduces physiological action-potential bursting, cutaneous dry-air priming and isothermal hyperosmolar bursting at 33 °C, human *TRPM8*/*SCN10A*/*MICU1* phenotypes, the protective diabetic *MCU*-knockout paradox, and neurovascular dihydropyridine confounds—delivering seven rigorous, low-cost falsification protocols.

## Abstract

Since the cloning of **CMR1 (TRPM8)** in 2002, mammalian cold perception has been largely conceptualized as a static rate-coded labeled line, while wetness and dry-cold perceptions have been attributed solely to evaporative cooling coupled with mechanoreception. This reductionism leaves unresolved seven decades of pre-molecular intact-fiber electrophysiology (1935–2002)—which showed that mammalian cold receptors operate as endogenous, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-controlled, $\text{CO}_2$-inhibited, metabolically coupled subthreshold oscillators—as well as non-evaporative TRPM8-dependent shaking, corneal-versus-cutaneous qualia divergence, and severe cold dysesthesia in metabolic neuropathies. Although TRPM8 is an established peripheral osmosensor in both trigeminal corneal and somatic dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, its cutaneous osmosensory viability across the hydrophobic stratum corneum (SC) has never been formalized quantitatively. Here, we combine a thermodynamic trans-epidermal water-activity ($a_w$) leverage analysis with a 5-variable electro-metabolic relaxation oscillator coupled to a fast $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8/\text{K}_\text{v}$ action-potential spiking layer and a dermal microvascular perfusion module. First, we demonstrate that because $dc_{\text{osm}}/da_w = -(M_w a_w)^{-1} \approx -55.8\text{ Osm/kg}$ (+55.8 mOsm/kg per $-0.001\,\Delta a_w$), an intact SC and stratum granulosum (SG2) tight-junction barrier blocking **99.8%** of dry-air water-activity loss still transmits **+55.8 mOsm/kg** (50% RH) to **+89.3 mOsm/kg** (20% RH heated winter air) directly to SG-terminating TRPM8+ free nerve endings—shifting the modeled cutaneous bursting threshold from 20.0 °C up to 29.0 °C while keeping intact skin silent at warm rest (33 °C), whereas barrier disruption (99.0% shielding) unmasks +280 mOsm/kg corneal-scale hypertonicity. Second, using one fixed parameter set, the oscillator reproduces: (1) self-sustained rhythmic action-potential bursting (~515 nM $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$, 180° phase-locked mitochondrial $\text{pH}_i$ dips to 6.88) abolished by $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ or L-type blockade and collapsed into toxic plateaus by mitochondrial uncoupling; (2) **isothermal hyperosmolar bursting (+89 to +98 mOsm at 33 °C, zero cooling)** and tonic $\text{H}^+$-insensitive menthol plateaus; (3) genotype-to-dynamics phenotypes of human *TRPM8* (D665N/R30Q, rs10166942[C]), *SCN10A* (I206M/G1662S), *MICU1* (25× baseline matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload), *TMBIM5* uncoupling, and diabetic nociceptor *MCU* deletion (−97% matrix overload); (4) microvascular warming and osmotic-washout confounding of cutaneous dihydropyridine (Bay K 8644/nifedipine) pharmacology; and (5) Monte Carlo proof that outdoor relative humidity inverts true indoor vapor pressure deficit (VPD) signaling, whereas within-subject crossovers achieve 80–96% power at $N = 10\text{–}20$. We conclude with seven decisive experimental falsification criteria spanning human psychophysics, avascular corneal pharmacology, and cellular electrophysiology.

## 1. Introduction

Molecular cloning frequently resolves transducer identity while inadvertently obscuring intact-system physiology established decades earlier. When the cold and menthol receptor **CMR1 (TRPM8)** was cloned in 2002 (McKemy, Neuhausser, & Julius, 2002; Peier et al., 2002) following characterization of native cold-evoked inward currents ($I_{\text{cold}}$) in dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons (Reid & Flonta, 2001), textbook models rapidly compressed peripheral cold coding into a linear labeled line: skin cooling opens TRPM8, discharge rate rises monotonically, and central pathways read out scalar cold intensity. Similarly, because mammals lack dedicated insect hygroreceptors, human perception of skin wetness and dry-cold air has been attributed predominantly to evaporative heat loss stimulating thermal TRPM8 paired with tactile mechanoreception (Filingeri et al., 2014; Typolt & Filingeri, 2020; Buoite Stella et al., 2022).

However, nearly seventy years of pre-molecular single-fiber electrophysiology (1935–2002) established six foundational properties that contradict a passive rate-coded thermometer:

1. **Bell-Shaped Static Ambiguity and Periodic Bursting (1935–1980):** Mammalian lingual, nasal, and primate cutaneous cold fibers fire spontaneously at normal resting skin temperatures (30–35 °C) with a bell-shaped static firing curve peaking at 20–28 °C, disambiguated by periodic grouped bursting whose interburst frequency increases with warming while intraburst spike count increases with cooling (Zotterman, 1936; Hensel & Zotterman, 1951a; Iggo, 1969; Darian-Smith, Johnson, & Dykes, 1973; Bade, Braun, & Hensel, 1979; Braun, Bade, & Hensel, 1980).
2. **Paradoxical Cold, Ischemia, and $\text{CO}_2$/Acidosis Depression (1951–1956):** Cold fibers fire again above ~45 °C ("paradoxical cold"; Dodt & Zotterman, 1952), respond acutely to ischemia (Hensel, 1953), and are selectively depressed by elevated $\text{CO}_2$ / tissue acidosis—an observation left unexplained until Andersson, Chase, and Bevan (2004) demonstrated that intracellular protons ($\text{H}^+$) inhibit cold- and icilin-activated TRPM8 while sparing menthol.
3. **Bioenergetic Coupling and Mitochondria-Packed Bulbar Endings (1974–1975):** Serial electron microscopy revealed that intraepithelial cold-receptor terminals form enlarged bulbar swellings densely packed with mitochondria and glycogen (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Heppelmann et al., 2001), and blocking nerve-terminal bioenergetics (e.g., ouabain) severely disrupts cold-receptor static discharge and dynamic adaptation (Pierau, Torrey, & Carpenter, 1974).
4. **Endogenous Subthreshold Oscillating Inward Currents (1979–2000):** Bursting originates from a slow oscillating inward current ($I_{sd}$) opposed by slow $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-dependent repolarizing feedback ($I_{sr}$) (Braun et al., 1980, 1994, 1998, 2000; Schäfer, Braun, & Hensel, 1982; Schäfer, Braun, & Rempe, 1991), paralleling intrinsic subthreshold membrane-potential oscillations and low-voltage-activated, dihydropyridine-sensitive L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ currents unmasked between spikes when adult trigeminal or DRG neurons sit at depolarized potentials (Puil, Gimbarzevsky, & Spigelman, 1988; Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996; Amir, Michaelis, & Devor, 1999).
5. **The Menthol–Calcium Paradox (1986–1987):** Sixteen years before CMR1 was cloned, Schäfer, Braun, and Isenberg (1986) showed that micromolar menthol produces non-monotonic reorganization of cold-fiber bursting that is **completely abolished by elevated external $\text{Ca}^{2+}$**, and Swandulla et al. (1987) demonstrated that menthol directly blocks voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels in DRG neurons—proving menthol perturbs the $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-coupled oscillator rather than acting as a clean thermal mimetic.
6. **Chronically Depolarized, TTX-Resistant Corneal Terminals and Prolonged-Holding Inward $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ Dynamics (1998–2009):** Cold specificity requires combinatorial balance between $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-permeable $I_{\text{cold}}$ (Suto & Gotoh, 1999; Reid & Flonta, 2001; Reid, Babes, & Pluteanu, 2002; de la Peña et al., 2005), slow outward $I_{\text{KD}}$ ($\text{K}_\text{v}1.1/1.2$), two-pore $\text{K}^+$ channels, and $I_h$ (HCN) (Viana, de la Peña, & Belmonte, 2002; Madrid et al., 2006, 2009; Noël et al., 2009; Orio et al., 2009, 2012; Morenilla-Palao et al., 2014; De-la-Torre et al., 2026). Crucially, focal extracellular recordings of nerve terminal impulses (NTIs) from single guinea-pig corneal free nerve endings—pioneered by Brock, McLachlan, and Belmonte (1998) and extended across cold versus polymodal terminals (Brock, Pianova, & Belmonte, 2001; Carr & Brock, 2002)—proved via prolonged (30–60 s) polarizing holding currents that cold nerve endings express high densities of TTX-resistant $\text{Na}^+$ channels ($\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$) while sitting **chronically depolarized ($\ge -40\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$) at physiological resting temperature** (Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002). Subsequent guinea-pig corneal NTI analyses demonstrated directional cooling/rewarming waveform hysteresis, an endogenous terminal burst pacemaker that bifurcates into continuous tonic discharge below 30 °C in ~31% of units (Carr et al., 2003), distinct $\text{K}^+$-channel control of bursting versus warming silence (Brock, Acosta, Al Abed, Pianova, & Belmonte, 2006), and mobile spike-initiation loci inside the terminal arbor (Carr, Pianova, McKemy, & Brock, 2009; Belmonte, Brock, & Viana, 2009), while direct optical $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ imaging in single intact corneal nerve terminals proved that electrically evoked $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ entry is mediated predominantly by nifedipine- and diltiazem-sensitive L-type voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels (Gover, Kao, & Weinreich, 2003).

A parallel molecular revolution across both guinea-pig and mouse corneal preparations established **TRPM8 as an endogenous peripheral osmosensor**. Adapting Brock's focal NTI paradigm to excised mouse eyes demonstrated that basal TRPM8 activity detects tear-film evaporation to drive tearing (Parra et al., 2010), physiological tear hyperosmolality linearly accelerates mouse corneal cold-receptor NTI bursting starting right above 340 mOsm/kg whereas polymodal nociceptors require $\ge 600\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (Parra, Gonzalez-Gonzalez, Gallar, & Belmonte, 2014), hyperosmolarity (>300 mOsm/kg) directly gates TRPM8 to regulate blinking while hypoosmolarity inhibits it (Quallo et al., 2015), inter-blink tear osmolality climbs dynamically from ~300 to >450 mOsm/kg across functionally distinct mouse TRPM8+ cold-receptor classes (Hirata et al., 2017; González-González et al., 2017; Alcalde et al., 2018; Vereertbrugghen & Galletti, 2022), chronic dryness or axonal injury remodels corneal TRPM8 excitability (Kovács et al., 2016; Belmonte, Nichols, Cox, Brock, et al., 2017; Piña et al., 2019)—culminating in pivotal Phase 3 COMET-2/COMET-3 clinical efficacy and dry-eye approval of the topical TRPM8 agonist acoltremon (Pattar et al., 2026; Malik et al., 2025)—and hyperosmolar eye drops trigger TRPM8-dependent (BCTC-blocked) reflex squinting that is blunted in diabetic peripheral neuropathy (Yorek MS et al., 2016), where mouse corneal TRPM8+ cold complex endings selectively degenerate (Alamri, Brock, et al., 2019). Critically, Quallo et al. (2015) demonstrated that **physiological hyperosmolarity directly excites isolated somatic DRG TRPM8+ neurons**—not solely corneal trigeminal afferents—while human skin biopsy studies proved that TRPM8, rather than TRPA1, is expressed directly on human intraepidermal nerve fibers and quantitatively tracks human skin cooling perception (Weyer-Menkhoff et al., 2019), establishing the intrinsic biophysical and human anatomical substrate for cutaneous osmosensation. Coupled with non-evaporative TRPM8-dependent "wet dog shakes" in mammals and birds (Selescu et al., 2025), orthogonal qualia across adjacent $V_1$ trigeminal territories (cornea vs. lid margin vs. eyelid skin), and combinatorial TRPM8-silencing requirements for warm perception (Paricio-Montesinos et al., 2020), these data motivate a unified mechanistic framework.

Here, we develop an open-source, fixed-parameter biophysical dynamical-systems model linking **cutaneous/corneal TRPM8 osmo-thermal co-gating** to an **electro-metabolic relaxation oscillator ($\text{TRPM8} - \text{Na}_\text{v}1.8 - \text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/1.3 - \text{mitochondrial MICU1/MCU/TMBIM5 } \text{H}^+$)**, test its predictions against human genetic cohorts, diabetic *MCU*-knockout data, and cutaneous microvascular dihydropyridine pharmacology, and specify seven severe experimental falsifications grounded in psychrometric Monte Carlo power modeling.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Thermodynamic Formulation of Osmo-Thermal Co-Gating

At exposed biological boundaries undergoing water loss (corneal tear film, lip vermilion, or epidermis undergoing transepidermal water loss, TEWL), evaporation simultaneously cools the surface ($\Delta T < 0$) and concentrates residual solutes ($\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{ext}} > 0$). Extending the voltage-shift gating model of Voets et al. (2004) and $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$/membrane-tension coupling (Rohacs et al., 2005) to incorporate osmotic free energy and intracellular proton inhibition yields:

$$P_{\text{open}}(V, T, \text{Osm}, \text{pH}_i) = \left[ 1 + \exp\left( \frac{\Delta G_{\text{total}}}{RT} \right) \right]^{-1} \qquad (1)$$

$$\Delta G_{\text{total}} = \Delta H(T) - T\Delta S(T) - zFV + \Delta G_{\text{osm}}(\text{Osm}_{\text{ext}} - \text{Osm}_{\text{int}}) + \Delta G_{\text{tens}}(\gamma) + \Delta G_{\text{H}^+}(\text{pH}_i) \qquad (2)$$

In the conductance model, external hyperosmolarity ($\Delta\text{Osm} > 0$, mOsm/kg above 300) shifts TRPM8 half-activation voltage $V_{1/2}$ hyperpolarized by $-0.72\text{ mV/mOsm}$ alongside thermal gating ($+5.0\text{ mV}/^\circ\text{C}$ relative to 25 °C).

### 2.2 Trans-Epidermal Water-Activity Leverage Across the Stratum Corneum and Stratum Granulosum

Unlike the corneal tear film—an exposed aqueous meniscus whose osmolality is measured directly in mOsm/kg—the cutaneous **stratum corneum (SC)** is an anucleated, hydrophobic lipid–keratin barrier devoid of continuous free interstitial fluid, precluding direct microdialysis or osmometry without barrier disruption. In skin biophysics, hydration state across the SC and its boundary with the viable **stratum granulosum (SG)** is therefore quantified via **water activity ($a_w$)** and **water mass fraction (% w/w)** (Warner, Myers, & Taylor, 1988; Caspers et al., 2001; Sparr & Wennerström, 2001; Sparr et al., 2013). At thermodynamic equilibrium, water chemical potential ($\mu_w = \mu_w^\circ + RT\ln a_w$) maps local $a_w$ directly to equivalent solution osmolality $c_{\text{osm}}$ ($\text{Osm/kg}$) and osmotic pressure $\Pi$ ($\text{Pa}$):

$$c_{\text{osm}} = -\frac{\ln(a_w)}{M_w}, \qquad \Pi = -\frac{RT}{\bar{V}_w}\ln(a_w) \qquad (3)$$

where $M_w = 0.018015\text{ kg mol}^{-1}$ is the molar mass of water and $\bar{V}_w \approx 1.81 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}$ is its partial molar volume at skin temperature ($T = 306.15\text{ K}$, $33\,^\circ\text{C}$). Linearizing Equation 3 around the physiological viable-epidermis boundary ($a_{w,0} = 0.9946$, corresponding to $c_{\text{osm},0} = 300.6\text{ mOsm/kg}$ and $\Pi_0 = 0.76\text{ MPa}$) yields the local osmotic leverage of small water-activity perturbations transmitted across an SC + SG2 tight-junction barrier with shielding efficiency $S_{\text{SC}} \in (0, 1)$:

$$\Delta c_{\text{osm,SG}} \approx -\frac{\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}}}{M_w\,a_{w,0}} = -\frac{(1 - S_{\text{SC}})\,\Delta a_{w,\text{surf}}}{M_w\,a_{w,0}}, \qquad \left(\frac{dc_{\text{osm}}}{da_w}\right)_{\!a_{w,0}} = -55.8\ \text{Osm kg}^{-1} \qquad (4)$$

Thus, every **0.001 (0.1% RH) drop in local water activity** inside the apical stratum granulosum generates **+55.8 mOsm/kg** ($\Delta \Pi \approx +0.14\text{ MPa}$) of **thermodynamic water-chemical-potential equivalence** ($\Delta c_{\text{osm,SG}}$). Importantly, because SG2 claudin-1/claudin-4 tight junctions restrict paracellular ion diffusion more strongly than trans-epidermal water flux (Kirschner et al., 2013) while monovalent ions per solvent water stay near dermal equilibrium (von Zglinicki et al., 1993), $\Delta c_{\text{osm,SG}}$ represents the local trans-membrane water chemical potential and aquaporin-3 (AQP3)-coupled granular keratinocyte cell-volume/membrane-tension strain (Ma et al., 2002; Zijl et al., 2025) experienced by apposed nerve endings—rather than requiring a macroscopic bulk hypertonic interstitial lake.

### 2.3 Five-Variable Electro-Metabolic Relaxation Oscillator

To evaluate whether coupling TRPM8, cold-resistant $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ (Brock et al., 1998; Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002; Zimmermann et al., 2007; Gasperini et al., 2011), terminal and small-sensory-neuron L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels ($\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ poised in the chronically depolarized $-45\text{ to }-25\text{ mV}$ window; Scroggs & Fox, 1992; Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996; Xu & Lipscombe, 2001; Gover, Kao, & Weinreich, 2003; Lipscombe, Helton, & Xu, 2004; Bourinet et al., 2014; Isensee et al., 2021; Su et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019), electroneutral plasma-membrane $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-ATPase ($\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$) extrusion (Gover, Moreira, Kao, & Weinreich, 2007; Hwang et al., 2011; Poburko, Santo Domingo, & Demaurex, 2011) paired with high-threshold axonal mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ uniporter (MCU/MICU1) sequestration (Werth & Thayer, 1994; Nita et al., 2016; Ashrafi et al., 2020), and coupled mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ antiport (TMBIM5–MICU1; Austin et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025) inside restricted femtoliter endings where matrix $\text{H}^+$ buffering is ~4-fold lower than cytosol (Poburko et al., 2011) produces self-sustained bursting, we formulated a 5-state ordinary differential equation (ODE) system tracking slow burst-envelope membrane potential $V$ (mV), cytosolic $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ ($c$, $\mu$M), mitochondrial matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ load $c_m$ (a.u.), cytosolic proton excess $\Delta[\text{H}^+]_i$ above baseline pH 7.30 ($H_0 = 50.12\text{ nM}$), and cooperative $\text{H}^+$-block gating $s_{\text{pH}} \in (0,1)$:

$$C_m \frac{dV}{dt} = -k \left[ I_{\text{TRPM8}}(V, T, \text{Osm}, s_{\text{pH}}) + I_{\text{Nav1.8}}(V) + I_{\text{CaL}}(V, c) + I_L(V, c) \right] \qquad (5)$$

$$\frac{dc}{dt} = k \left[ J_{\text{in,M8}} + J_{\text{in,CaL}} - J_{\text{PMCA}}(c) - J_{\text{MCU}}(c, \Psi_m) + J_{\text{m,out}}(c_m) \right] \qquad (6)$$

$$\frac{d(\Delta[\text{H}^+]_i)}{dt} = k \left( \alpha_H J_{\text{MCU}}(c, \Psi_m) - \frac{\Delta[\text{H}^+]_i}{\tau_H} \right), \qquad \frac{ds_{\text{pH}}}{dt} = k \frac{s_\infty([\text{H}^+]_i) - s_{\text{pH}}}{\tau_s} \qquad (7)$$

### 2.4 Coupled Fast $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8/\text{K}_\text{v}$ Action-Potential Spiking Layer

To visualize single-unit action-potential discharge alongside slow envelope bursting (temporal resolution $\Delta t = 5\text{ ms}$), we coupled an inhomogeneous spike generator to $V(t)$ incorporating: (i) low spontaneous resting discharge near $-46\text{ mV}$ (~0.25 Hz), (ii) steep voltage-dependent firing acceleration across $-43$ to $-24\text{ mV}$ (up to ~6 Hz), (iii) asymmetric broad $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8/\text{Ca}_\text{v}$ overshooting spikes (+48 mV) with afterhyperpolarization (AHP) and progressive $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ slow-inactivation amplitude tapering near the depolarized $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ peak ($-20$ to $-14\text{ mV}$; Blair & Bean, 2002, 2003; directly matching intraburst NTI amplitude decline and mobile terminal initiation sites recorded by Carr et al., 2003, and Carr, Pianova, McKemy, & Brock, 2009), and (iv) abrupt burst termination during mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$-driven repolarization ($\frac{dV}{dt} < -0.5\text{ mV/s}$).

### 2.5 Parameterization of Human Genetics, Mitochondrial Lesions, and Neurovascular Coupling

Human gene constraint metrics (loss-of-function observed/expected ratio [LoF o/e], probability of loss-of-function intolerance [pLI], and missense *Z*-score) and disease associations were retrieved from the Open Targets Platform (*TRPM8*, ENSG00000144481; *SCN10A*, ENSG00000185313; *CACNA1C*, ENSG00000151067; *MCU*, ENSG00000156026; *MICU1*, ENSG00000107745; *GHITM*/*TMBIM5*, ENSG00000165678; *LETM1*, ENSG00000168924). Verified molecular lesions were mapped directly onto single biophysical parameters: human *TRPM8* gain-of-function (D665N/R30Q: $\Delta V_{1/2}=-28\text{ mV}$); protective *TRPM8* rs10166942[C] ($0.45\times g_{\text{M8,max}}$); human *SCN10A* gain-of-function (I206M/G1662S: $\Delta V_{1/2,\text{Na}}=-4\text{ mV}$); human *MICU1* loss-of-function (MPXPS: $K_d=0.14\,\mu\text{M},\,n=1.8$); *TMBIM5* $\text{H}^+$-uncoupling ($\alpha_H \times 0.25$); and diabetic nociceptor *MCU* knockout (*MCU* $\times 0.03$). For cutaneous neurovascular pharmacology, local dermal temperature and osmotic gradient were coupled to perfusion shift $p \in [-1, +1]$ via $T_{\text{eff}} = T + \Delta T_{\text{vasc}}(p)$ and $\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{eff}} = \Delta\text{Osm}\,(1 - w_{\text{vasc}}(p))$, contrasting avascular/clamped tissue ($p=0$) against dihydropyridine vasodilation ($+2.0\,^\circ\text{C}$, 50% osmotic washout) and Bay K 8644 vasoconstriction ($-2.0\,^\circ\text{C}$, 30% reduced washout).

### 2.6 Psychrometric Confounding and Statistical Power Simulations

To quantify ecological confounding in mobile-app studies, we simulated $N = 2{,}000$ synthetic indoor/outdoor observations across seasonal temperatures ($-10$ to $+35\,^\circ\text{C}$) using Magnus–Tetens saturation vapor pressure equations converting outdoor dew point ($T_d$) into true indoor-corrected relative humidity and local VPD (kPa). Between-subject cross-sectional power was contrasted against within-subject repeated ecological momentary assessment (EMA, 14 check-ins/person) and paired crossover trials (Protocol H2-Gel and Protocol H4) via Monte Carlo resampling.

### 2.7 Numerical Integration and Code Availability

All ODE trajectories were integrated over 420 s (300 s stimulus window, $t \in [60, 360]\text{ s}$) using a stiff Radau ordinary differential equation solver in SciPy (relative tolerance $10^{-6}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-8}$; backward differentiation formula solver for the two-dimensional neurovascular parameter grid). Burst peaks were quantified using automated peak detection (minimum prominence $\ge 60\text{ nM}$), and sustained limit-cycle amplitude was measured as the steady-tail $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ excursion over the final two-thirds of stimulation. All Python simulation scripts, interactive browser-based 5-ODE oscillator and psychrometric VPD tools, and publication figures are openly available at https://github.com/yorekms/trpm8-osmo-cold and https://yorekms.github.io/trpm8-osmo-cold/.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Anatomical Asymmetry, Epidermal Water-Activity Leverage, and Worked Cutaneous Osmotic Examples

Comparing the proven corneal osmosensory interface against intact skin defines—and quantitatively resolves—the central biophysical objection to cutaneous osmosensation (**Figure 1**). In the avascular mouse and guinea-pig cornea, dense unmyelinated C- and Aδ-fiber trunks form the subbasal nerve whorl (**Figure 1A**) and project intraepithelial free terminals within microns of the evaporating tear film (**Figure 1C**), experiencing direct, unbuffered osmotic excursions (300–450+ mOsm/kg, linearly accelerating TRPM8+ cold-terminal NTI bursting immediately above 340 mOsm/kg while sparing polymodal nociceptors below 600 mOsm/kg; Parra et al., 2014) with zero microvascular perfusion (ΔFlow = 0; **Figure 1B, left**). Crucially, combined focal NTI recording and 3D confocal/immunohistochemical reconstruction across guinea-pig and mouse corneas proved that functionally identified TRPM8+ cold thermoreceptors—unlike thin ramifying TRPV1+ polymodal endings or *PIEZO2*+ mechanonociceptors (Bron, Wood, Brock, & Ivanusic, 2014; Alamri, Bron, Brock, & Ivanusic, 2015)—terminate exclusively in clustered **large *en passant* boutons and superficial bulbar "complex endings"** (Ivanusic, Wood, & Brock, 2013; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018), a specialized ultrastructure packed with dense accumulations of mitochondria (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Heppelmann et al., 2001) that places plasma-membrane TRPM8 and L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels in immediate femtoliter apposition to the MICU1/MCU/TMBIM5 proton-reset machinery.

In intact human skin (**Figure 1B, right**), ambient drying operates across a sharply stratified hydration and water-activity architecture rather than a uniform passive wall:

1. **Outer Stratum Corneum (*stratum disjunctum*):** Surface water content drops to ~15–30% w/w (Warner et al., 1988; Caspers et al., 2001) and equilibrates near ambient relative humidity ($a_w \approx 0.20\text{–}0.60$). By Equation 3, 50% RH ($a_w = 0.50$) represents an enormous thermodynamic suction ($\Pi \approx 97.5\text{ MPa}$, equivalent to $\sim 38{,}500\text{ mOsm/kg}$), rising to $\sim 226.5\text{ MPa}$ ($\sim 89{,}300\text{ mOsm/kg}$) in 20% RH heated winter air, though sequestered largely across dead lipid–keratin lamellae.
2. **Mid Stratum Corneum (NMF Osmotic Swelling Zone):** As corneocytes ascend into the intermediate $a_w = 0.80\text{–}0.95$ zone, enzymatic **filaggrin proteolysis** activates—a process shown by Scott & Harding (1986) to occur *exclusively* between 80% and 95% RH (blocked above ~95% and below ~80%). This floods mid-SC corneocytes with hygroscopic Natural Moisturizing Factor (NMF) amino acids (~20–30% dry weight; internal equivalent osmolality $\sim 2{,}850\text{–}12{,}400\text{ mOsm/kg}$).
3. **Basal Cornified Layer (*stratum compactum*, innermost 1–3 SC layers):** Electron-probe X-ray microanalysis revealed a sharp mass-fraction water discontinuity right at the SC–SG boundary (~70% w/w in viable SG dropping abruptly to ~30–40% in the lowest SC; Warner et al., 1988). Crucially, however, cryo-SEM proved that these deepest basal corneocytes **do not swell even when soaked in water** (Bouwstra et al., 2003) because high basal water activity ($a_w > 0.95$) suppresses filaggrin proteolysis (low NMF), while frozen-hydrated X-ray microanalysis showed monovalent ions ($\text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+, \text{Cl}^-$) per solvent water remain near equilibrium with the viable epidermis (von Zglinicki et al., 1993). Accordingly, mathematical skin-hydration models place the basal SC inner boundary at the physiological water-activity baseline $a_w \approx 0.9946\text{–}0.9960$ ($\approx 222\text{–}301\text{ mOsm/kg}$; Sparr & Wennerström, 2001; Sparr et al., 2013).
4. **Stratum Granulosum (SG1/SG2) — The Living Osmoregulatory & Neurosensory Frontier:** Directly beneath the basal SC—where proton-induced X-ray emission (PIXE) confirms steep $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{K}^+$ gradients peaking in the outer SG that collapse acutely upon barrier perturbation (Mauro et al., 1998), and claudin-1/4 tight junctions selectively partition ions versus water (Kirschner et al., 2013)—living SG keratinocytes demonstrably sense mild physiological hyperosmolarity: human keratinocytes upregulate betaine, *myo*-inositol, and **taurine (*SLC6A6*/TAUT)** transporters 3- to 5-fold at ~405 mOsm/L, peaking in the outermost granular layer to tighten claudin-1/4 seals (Janeke et al., 2003; Warskulat et al., 2004; El-Chami et al., 2014, 2021), governed by **NFAT5 (TonEBP)** alongside **AQP3** osmotic water permeability and cell-volume mechanosensing (Ma et al., 2002; Muhammad et al., 2021; Zijl et al., 2025). Critically, both rodent and human neuroanatomical studies place cutaneous cold sensors directly at this boundary: ultrastructural electron microscopy confirms unmyelinated human nerve fibers ascending into the human stratum granulosum (Hilliges, Wang, & Johansson, 1995), human intraepidermal TRPM8 nerve expression quantitatively predicts skin cooling perception (Weyer-Menkhoff et al., 2019), and genetically labeled **TRPM8+ free nerve endings ascend straight into the stratum granulosum** (Dhaka et al., 2008), where **SG2 tight junctions actively prune epidermal nerve terminals** immediately *underneath* the TJ seal in healthy skin—and lose pruning containment, breaching upward toward SG1 and the basal SC, during chronic dryness or barrier disruption (Takahashi et al., 2019).

**Worked Cutaneous Osmotic-Leverage Example (Equations 3–7).** Because water-activity leverage near physiological hydration is extraordinarily steep ($dc_{\text{osm}}/da_w = -55.8\text{ Osm/kg}$, i.e., **+55.8 mOsm/kg per $-0.001\,\Delta a_w$**; Equation 4), the stratum corneum and SG2 tight junctions can block **99.8% of the external humidity drop ($S_{\text{SC}} = 0.998$, a 500-fold attenuation)** and *still* transmit physiologically decisive **thermodynamic water-activity equivalence** ($\Delta c_{\text{osm,SG}}$) to SG-terminating TRPM8+ fibers (**Table 2**):

- **Case 1 — Hydrated Skin Control ($a_{w,\text{SG}} = 0.9946$, $c_{\text{osm}} = 300.6\text{ mOsm/kg}$, $\Delta\text{Osm} = 0$):** Under our fixed ODE parameters (Table 1), hydrated skin remains quiet at warm rest (33 °C: 96.5 nM), mild cool (29 °C: 99.5 nM), room temperature (25 °C: 114.7 nM), and even 22.0 °C (Protocol H2-Gel submersion temperature: 171.5 nM), requiring cooling to **20.0 °C** before igniting rhythmic bursting (2 peaks, 490.9 nM).
- **Case 2 — Intact Skin in Moderate Dry Air (50% RH, $\Delta a_{w,\text{surf}} = -0.50$, $S_{\text{SC}} = 0.998$):** Even with 99.8% barrier shielding, the 0.2% residual leakage lowers apical SG water activity by just $\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.0010$ ($0.9946 \to 0.9936$), elevating local osmolality from $300.6 \to 356.4\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (**$\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{SG}} = +55.8\text{ mOsm/kg}$**, $\Delta V_{1/2,\text{M8}} = -40.2\text{ mV}$, equivalent to $-8.0\,^\circ\text{C}$ of thermal priming). In the exact fixed-parameter ODE, intact dry skin **stays quiet at warm rest (33 °C: 114.9 nM, $\text{pH}_i = 7.29$)**—explaining why healthy skin does not spontaneously ache or burst in warm dry rooms—yet **shifts the cutaneous bursting threshold upward by +9 °C (from 20.0 °C to 29.0 °C: 2 peaks, 487.9 nM)** and fires **4 vigorous bursts (529.0 nM, $\text{pH}_{\min} = 6.87$) upon zero-evaporation 22.0 °C submersion (Protocol H2-Gel)**, where contralateral hydrated skin remains silent (171.5 nM). Even stricter **99.85% shielding** ($\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.00075$, $\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{SG}} = +41.9\text{ mOsm/kg}$) still ignites 3 full bursts at 22–25 °C (506.7–523.6 nM).
- **Case 3 — Intact Skin in Heated Winter Indoor Air (21 °C, 20% RH, VPD = 1.99 kPa, $\Delta a_{w,\text{surf}} \approx -0.80$, same $S_{\text{SC}} = 0.998$):** The exact same 99.8% barrier yields $\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.0016$ ($a_w: 0.9946 \to 0.9930$), raising SG osmolality to $389.9\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (**$\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{SG}} = +89.3\text{ mOsm/kg}$**, $\Delta V_{1/2,\text{M8}} = -64.3\text{ mV}$). In the ODE, $+89.3\text{ mOsm/kg}$ **ignites 3 full rhythmic bursts even isothermally at 33.0 °C (496.2 nM, tail oscillation amplitude 263.2 nM, $\text{pH}_{\min} = 6.88$)** with zero skin cooling.
- **Case 4 — Winter Xerosis / Barrier Disruption / Atopic Dermatitis ($S_{\text{SC}} = 0.990$, Impaired SG2 Pruning):** When tight junctions weaken and intraepidermal nerves sprout past SG2 toward the basal SC (Takahashi et al., 2019), reducing shielding merely from 99.8% to 99.0% ($\Delta a_{w,\text{SG}} = -0.0050$, $a_w = 0.9896$) drives apical SG osmolality to **$580.3\text{ mOsm/kg}$ ($\Delta\text{Osm}_{\text{SG}} = +279.8\text{ mOsm/kg}$, $\Pi = 1.47\text{ MPa}$)**—unmasking raw corneal-scale hypertonicity and directly predicting heightened dry-cold sensitivity and neurogenic itch in barrier-impaired skin.

Within either corneal or cutaneous TRPM8+ terminals, the proposed electro-metabolic oscillator operates through three tightly coupled phases (**Figure 2**): (1) basal TRPM8 inward current holds the terminal depolarized near $-45\text{ mV}$ at the $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ activation knee while resting $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i \approx 100\text{ nM}$ keeps the MICU1 gate closed (**Figure 2A**); (2) mild cooling ($\Delta T < 0$) and/or hyperosmolarity ($+\Delta\text{Osm}$) recruits regenerative $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ action-potential bursts and small-DRG L-type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ influx, driving $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ past the ~300 nM MICU1 threshold into the 500 nM range (**Figure 2B**); and (3) rapid MCU $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ sequestration coupled to the physical **TMBIM5–MICU1** $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ exchange complex pulses cytosolic $\text{H}^+$ ($\text{pH}_i$: $7.30 \to 6.88$), selectively closing cold/icilin/osmotic TRPM8 (while sparing menthol), repolarizing $V_m$ to $-51\text{ mV}$ until PMCA/NHE clearance resets the cycle (**Figure 2C**).

### 3.4 Microvascular Warming and Osmotic Washout Confound Cutaneous $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ Dihydropyridine Pharmacology

Testing L-type channels in perfused skin with dihydropyridines (DHPs) introduces three intersecting confounds: (1) high-affinity vascular smooth-muscle $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ ($IC_{50}\approx 10\text{–}50\text{ nM}$) vs. ~10-fold lower-affinity neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ activating ~25 mV more negative inside the $-45\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$ cold-terminal window (Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996; Xu & Lipscombe, 2001; Lipscombe, Helton, & Xu, 2004); (2) direct off-target activation of perivascular **TRPA1** by *both* DHP blockers (nifedipine, nimodipine) and agonist **Bay K 8644** (Fajardo, Meseguer, Belmonte, & Viana, 2008); and (3) bidirectional TRPM8→microvascular reflex vasodilation (>11-fold human skin conductance rise with topical menthol; Dillon, Lichter, & Alexander, 2021).

Coupling microvascular thermal and osmotic-washout dynamics into our ODE model (**Figure 5**) exposes sharp diagnostic signatures:
- **Avascular / Bath-Clamped Tissue ($\Delta\text{Flow} = 0$, Figure 5A):** With zero blood flow and background HC-030031 TRPA1 blockade, potentiating neuronal L-type channels with **(−)-(S)-Bay K 8644** converts mild osmotic bursting (+82 mOsm, 33 °C; 485.5 nM) into a **sustained non-adapting high-$\text{Ca}^{2+}$ plateau (804.7 nM, mean matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ load = 6.28 a.u.)**—mirroring classic nifedipine-sensitive L-type plateau potentials (Morisset & Nagy, 1999; Fossat et al., 2010)—because potentiated $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ sustains depolarization even after mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$ closes TRPM8. Co-applying **nifedipine** (or stereoisomer **(+)-(R)-Bay K 8644**) cleanly collapses the plateau back to quiet baseline (225.5 nM), consistent with systemic nifedipine and diltiazem preventing L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-driven TRPM8 hyperexcitability in vivo (Kawashiri et al., 2012).
- **Perfused-Skin Vasodilation Confound (Figure 5B):** On dry skin (+98 mOsm, 33 °C), a **pure vascular vasodilator (ACEi/ARB: +2.0 °C warming + 50% osmotic washout, neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}$ untouched)** completely silences bursting (101.5 nM flat), superimposing identically on low-dose perfused nifedipine (101.1 nM) even though the identical 40% partial neuronal block (0.60× baseline L-type conductance) still fires 4 vigorous bursts (376.3 nM) when perfusion is clamped.
- **Perfused-Skin Vasoconstriction Mirror Confound (Figure 5C):** From quiet mild-dry skin (+72 mOsm, 150.5 nM), **vascular-only vasoconstriction** (−2.0 °C cooling + 30% reduced washout) falsely ignites 3 full bursts (516.6 nM), while perfused Bay K 8644 jumps directly to an 873.5 nM plateau.
- **2D Neurovascular De-Confounding Map (Figure 5D):** Sweeping perfusion shift against neuronal $g_{\text{CaL}}$ separates three distinct dynamical phases (*Silent/Subthreshold*, *Rhythmic Bursting*, and *L-Type Plateau Overload*), demonstrating why the avascular central cornea—where single-terminal confocal imaging directly proved nifedipine- and diltiazem-sensitive L-type dominance of action-potential $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx (Gover, Kao, & Weinreich, 2003; cf. Mikulec et al., 1995)—the isolated skin–nerve preparation, and thermal-clamped human **DHP vs. ACEi/ARB** cohorts (Protocol H2-Gel) cleanly orthogonalize neural gating from hemodynamics.

### 3.5 Outdoor Relative Humidity Sign-Inversion and Small-$N$ Within-Subject Statistical Power

Monte Carlo psychrometric modeling ($N = 2{,}000$, **Figure 6**) uncovered a severe ecological-inference artifact: correlating *outdoor* relative humidity against indoor thermal ratings **inverts the true physical sign** ($r = -0.067, p = 0.01$) because humid 0 °C winter air (80% outdoor RH) drops to **19.7% RH ($\text{VPD} = 1.99\text{ kPa}$)** when heated indoors to 21 °C. Computing true indoor-corrected **Local VPD** from outdoor dew point cleanly recovers the underlying physical association ($r_{\text{partial}} = -0.178, p = 1.1 \times 10^{-15}$). Furthermore, eliminating between-subject clothing and baseline variance via within-subject repeated designs yields **96% statistical power at $N = 10$ participants** (14 mobile-app check-ins/person) and **80–92% power at $N = 15\text{–}20$ participants** for paired crossover challenges (Protocol H2-Gel and Protocol H4), versus $N \approx 180$ for cross-sectional single reports.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Unifying Classical Pre-Molecular and Corneal Cold-Fiber Anomalies

Our electro-metabolic relaxation oscillator provides a single quantitative mechanism for six long-orphaned observations in peripheral thermoreceptor physiology: (1) **Hensel's (1953) $\text{CO}_2$/acidosis depression** directly engages the intracellular $\text{H}^+$ brake on cold/osmotic TRPM8, aligning with direct trigeminal ganglion recordings showing that cytosolic $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ elevations drive intracellular acidification ($\downarrow\text{pH}_i$, recovered via NHE1/pNBC1) that inhibits trigeminal action potentials (Hwang et al., 2011); (2) **Schäfer, Braun, and Isenberg's (1986) biphasic menthol anomaly** and **Swandulla et al.'s (1987) VGCC block** arise because menthol both bypasses the $\text{H}^+$ reset clock (**Figure 3H**) and interferes directly with the L-type amplification limb; (3) **Pierau et al.'s (1974) bioenergetic sensitivity** and **the dense mitochondrial packing of TRPM8+ complex bulbar endings** across cutaneous and mouse/guinea-pig corneal epithelia (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Heppelmann et al., 2001; Ivanusic, Wood, & Brock, 2013; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018) reflect strict dependence of burst termination on local mitochondrial $\Delta\Psi_m$ and $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ exchange; (4) **Brock and Carr's chronic terminal depolarization, intraburst NTI tapering, and $\text{K}^+$ dissociation** (Brock et al., 2001, 2006; Carr, Pianova, & Brock, 2002; Carr et al., 2003; Carr, Pianova, McKemy, & Brock, 2009) place the intact cold ending squarely inside the persistent window inward current of $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ (Schäfer, Braun, & Rempe, 1991; Ferroni, Galli, & Mazzanti, 1996; Xu & Lipscombe, 2001; Gover et al., 2003) and slow $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ inactivation (Blair & Bean, 2003); (5) **subtype-selective diabetic and oxaliplatin cold neuropathies**—specifically the preferential degeneration of TRPM8+ corneal cold complex endings over quiescent TRPV1+ polymodal endings in diabetic/high-fat-fed mice (Alamri, Brock, et al., 2019; Yorek MS et al., 2016) and L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-coupled TRPM8 cold hyperalgesia prevented by nifedipine or diltiazem in oxaliplatin-treated rats (Kono et al., 2012; Kawashiri et al., 2012; Su et al., 2019), as distinct from bulk hemoglobin-buffering DRG acidosis that sensitizes noxious TRPA1 while recruiting compensatory TREK $\text{K}_{2\text{P}}$ brakes (Potenzieri et al., 2020)—emerge when partial $\Delta\Psi_m$ collapse breaks the $\text{H}^+$ reset clock and traps tonically active cold terminals in sustained mitochondrial matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload (**Figures 3F, 4E**); and (6) **George et al.'s (2022) protective *MCU* deletion** represents an exact dynamical trade-off—sacrificing acute $\text{H}^+$-clocked oscillatory coding to abolish toxic matrix $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ accumulation and preserve distal unmyelinated axons.

In addition, our human genetic analysis specifies two **testable clinical QST predictions** for sensory cohorts: (i) stratifying Protocol H2-Gel participants by ***TRPM8* rs10166942** genotype should predict non-evaporative dry-cold sensitivity; and (ii) quantitative cold, wetness, and corneal hyperosmolar blink testing in ***MICU1* (MPXPS)** and ***LETM1*** cohorts should uncover altered temporal burst adaptation even prior to frank nerve conduction loss.

### 4.3 Peripheral Molecular Specialization Across Cornea, Palpebra, and Skin

Should regional or temporal dissociations survive Falsifications 1–7, four peripheral molecular modifiers can tune the identical *Trpm8* locus differently across adjacent territories: (1) **TRPM8 Channel-Associated Factors (*TCAF1*/*TCAF2*)**, which exert opposing potentiating/inhibitory control over TRPM8 trafficking and gating (Gkika et al., 2015) and underwent human-specific duplication and divergent natural selection (Hsieh, Eichler et al., 2021); (2) **Asn934 N-glycosylation and lipid-raft partitioning**, which shift TRPM8 $V_{1/2}$ by +60 mV and cold threshold by ~6 °C (Morenilla-Palao et al., 2009; Pertusa et al., 2012; Alcalde et al., 2018; Piña et al., 2019); (3) **short splice isoforms (4TM-TRPM8/sM8)** regulating ER–mitochondria $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ transfer (Bidaux et al., 2016); and (4) **local terminal morphology and channel-exchanger stoichiometry**—contrasting superficial mitochondria-packed bulbar complex endings in mouse and guinea-pig cornea (Ivanusic, Wood, & Brock, 2013; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018) against SG2-pruned cutaneous endings ($\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$, $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$, $I_{\text{KD}}$, TREK-1/TRAAK [Noël et al., 2009], TASK-1/3 [Morenilla-Palao et al., 2014], $\text{K}_\text{v}7$/M-channels that selectively brake cold nociceptors while sparing corneal cold thermoreceptors [Vetter et al., 2013], HCN1/2, MICU1/MCU, and TMBIM5/LETM1).

### 4.4 Methodological Limitations

Four limitations bound the present computational work and explicitly motivate independent experimental testing: (1) **Single-Compartment Bulbar Approximation:** Although serial-EM and 3D confocal reconstructions confirm that cold-receptor terminals form restricted ~1–3 µm mitochondria-packed bulbar swellings that favor tight femtoliter $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ coupling (Hensel, Andres, & von Düring, 1974; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic, & Brock, 2018), the 5-variable ODE omits multi-branch spatial electrotonic cable summation and mobile spike-initiation shifts across the terminal cluster (Carr, Pianova, McKemy, & Brock, 2009); (2) **Phenomenological Fast-Spiking Layer:** Action potentials in Figure 3A–B are coupled unidirectionally to the slow envelope $V(t)$ rather than solved via full multi-timescale Hodgkin–Huxley conductance equations, though the qualitative frequency-acceleration and $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ slow-inactivation tapering mirror recorded corneal NTI burst structure (Carr et al., 2003; Blair & Bean, 2003); (3) **Synergistic $\text{H}^+$ Sources, $\text{PIP}_2$ Parallelism, & Thermodynamic $a_w$ Equivalence:** Cytosolic acidification is parameterized via a single effective high-threshold flux ($\alpha_H J_{\text{MCU}}$) capturing the directional synergy between >300 nM mitochondrial TMBIM5–MICU1 $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ antiport (Austin et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025) and electroneutral PMCA $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ counter-transport (Hwang et al., 2011; Poburko et al., 2011) without explicitly partitioning *TMBIM5*, *LETM1*, PMCA $\text{H}^+$ import, or parallel $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-PLC$\delta$ $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ depletion (Rohacs et al., 2005; Yudin et al., 2011; Yin et al., 2024)—which Falsification 6 directly resolves—and the trans-epidermal barrier is modeled as **thermodynamic water-activity equivalence** ($S_{\text{SC}}$ in Equation 4) across ion-selective SG2 claudin tight junctions (von Zglinicki et al., 1993; Kirschner et al., 2013) rather than asserting a standing bulk interstitial ion lake or solving a full moving-boundary lipid–corneocyte diffusion PDE (Sparr & Wennerström, 2001)—which Falsification 1 directly tests; and (4) **Theoretical Scope:** No new human psychophysical or wet-lab electrophysiological recordings were collected in this study; all behavioral and pharmacological predictions are placed openly into the public domain for empirical confirmation or refutation under the pre-specified criteria in Table 5.
