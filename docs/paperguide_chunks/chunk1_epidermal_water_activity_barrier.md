### MANUSCRIPT CHUNK 1: Trans-Epidermal Water-Activity Leverage Across the Stratum Corneum & SG2 Frontier

Unlike the corneal tear film—an exposed aqueous meniscus whose osmolality is measured directly in mOsm/kg (climbing from ~300 to >450 mOsm/kg between blinks and linearly exciting mouse corneal TRPM8+ cold endings right above 340 mOsm/kg; Parra et al., 2014)—the cutaneous stratum corneum (SC) is an anucleated lipid–keratin barrier quantified via water activity (a_w). At thermodynamic equilibrium, water chemical potential maps local a_w directly to equivalent solution osmolality:
- c_osm = -ln(a_w) / M_w, with local derivative dc_osm / da_w = -(M_w * a_w)^(-1) ≈ -55.8 Osm/kg near physiological viable-epidermis hydration (a_w,0 = 0.9946, corresponding to 300.6 mOsm/kg).
- Every 0.001 drop in local water activity inside the apical stratum granulosum therefore generates +55.8 mOsm/kg of extracellular hyperosmolarity.
- Consequently, an intact SC + SG2 tight-junction barrier that blocks 99.8% of an external dry-air humidity drop (S_SC = 0.998, 500-fold attenuation) still transmits Δa_w,SG = -0.0010 at 50% RH (+55.8 mOsm/kg, shifting TRPM8 V_1/2 by -40.2 mV) and Δa_w,SG = -0.0016 in 20% RH heated winter air (+89.3 mOsm/kg, igniting isothermal TRPM8 bursting at 33 °C), while barrier disruption (S_SC = 0.990) unmasks +280 mOsm/kg corneal-scale hypertonicity where SG2 nerve-pruning fails (Dhaka et al., 2008; Takahashi et al., 2019).
