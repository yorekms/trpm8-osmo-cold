Functionally identified TRPM8+ cold thermoreceptors in mouse and guinea-pig cornea (and cutaneous cold spots) terminate in enlarged ~1–3 µm superficial bulbar "complex endings" densely packed with mitochondria (Hensel, Andres & von Düring, 1974; Heppelmann et al., 2001; Ivanusic, Wood & Brock, 2013; Alamri, Wood, Ivanusic & Brock, 2018).
- In our model (Equations 6–7), linear PMCA extrusion clears resting/brief Ca2+ loads (<250 nM; Gover et al., 2007), whereas sustained osmo-thermal bursts surging past the ~300 nM MICU1 gatekeeper set-point (Logan et al., 2014) activate steep cooperative MCU Ca2+ uptake coupled to the physical TMBIM5–MICU1 mitochondrial Ca2+/H+ exchange complex (Zhang et al., 2025; alongside Werth & Thayer, 1994).
- Inside the restricted femtoliter bulbar swelling, this coupled proton efflux transiently lowers local cytosolic pH_i from 7.30 to 6.88 (180 degrees phase-locked to each [Ca2+]i burst peak), engaging steep intracellular H+ block of cold-, icilin-, and osmotically gated TRPM8 (pH_1/2 ≈ 7.13; Andersson, Chase & Bevan, 2004) to terminate the burst—while sparing menthol-activated TRPM8, which bypasses H+ inhibition and locks into a 793 nM tonic plateau.
- Partial mitochondrial depolarization (DeltaPsi_m = 0.42 in diabetes or oxaliplatin neuropathy) or FCCP uncoupling erases this H+ reset clock and traps chronically depolarized TRPM8+ complex endings in sustained 678–819 nM cytosolic and matrix Ca2+ overload.
