### MANUSCRIPT CHUNK 2: Chronically Depolarized TRPM8+ Terminals & the Nav1.8 – Cav1.2/1.3 Burst Amplifier

Focal extracellular suction-pipette recordings of nerve terminal impulses (NTIs) in guinea-pig and mouse cornea (Brock, McLachlan & Belmonte, 1998; Brock, Pianova & Belmonte, 2001; Carr, Pianova & Brock, 2002; Carr et al., 2003, 2009; Parra et al., 2014) demonstrated via 30–60 s constant polarizing currents that intact cold nerve endings express high densities of TTX-resistant Nav1.8 and sit chronically depolarized (>= -40 to -30 mV) at warm resting temperature.
- In our 5-ODE model, tonic basal TRPM8 leak poises resting V_m near -45 mV right at the activation knee of cold-resistant Nav1.8 (Zimmermann et al., 2007; Blair & Bean, 2002, 2003) and inside the persistent window inward Ca2+ current of low-voltage neuronal Cav1.3 (V_1/2 ≈ -40 mV; Xu & Lipscombe, 2001; Lipscombe, Helton & Xu, 2004) and slow-inactivating small-DRG Cav1.2 (Scroggs & Fox, 1992; Isensee et al., 2021).
- Confocal Ca2+ imaging inside single intact corneal nerve terminals confirmed that action-potential-evoked terminal Ca2+ influx is dominated by nifedipine- and diltiazem-sensitive L-type VGCCs (Gover, Kao & Weinreich, 2003), while brief low-amplitude spike trains are cleared linearly by PMCA (Gover et al., 2007).
- During mild cooling or hyperosmolarity (>340 mOsm/kg), regenerative Nav1.8 + Cav1.2/1.3 bursting drives cytosolic [Ca2+]i from ~100 nM to ~515 nM with progressive intraburst Nav1.8 slow-inactivation spike tapering, crossing the ~300 nM cooperative MICU1 threshold on terminal mitochondria.
