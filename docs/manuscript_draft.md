## Abstract

Since the cloning of the cold and menthol receptor **CMR1 (TRPM8)** in 2002, somatosensory physiology has largely framed cold perception as a static labeled line in which peripheral cooling opens TRPM8 to drive monotonic spike rates, while cutaneous "wetness" and "dry-cold" sensations are attributed solely to evaporative heat loss coupled with tactile mechanoreception. Yet this single-molecule reductionism leaves unresolved both a 70-year pre-molecular electrophysiological record (1935–2002)—which showed that mammalian cold fibers operate as endogenous, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-controlled, metabolically sensitive, $\text{CO}_2$-inhibited subthreshold oscillators—and modern anomalies including non-evaporative TRPM8-dependent "wet dog shakes," orthogonal qualia across adjacent trigeminal territories (cornea vs. eyelid margin vs. skin), and severe cold dysesthesia in metabolic and mitochondrial disorders. Although TRPM8 is now established as a peripheral osmosensor in the cornea—and critically, in isolated **dorsal root ganglion (DRG)** neurons—its osmosensory role has been treated largely as an ocular specialty. Here, we advance the unified hypothesis that **TRPM8 osmosensitivity extends to cutaneous sensory terminals, where external and internal osmotic gradients co-gate an electro-metabolic relaxation oscillator ($\text{TRPM8} - \text{Na}_\text{v}1.8 - \text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2 - \text{mitochondrial } \text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$) to shape human perceptions of cold, wetness, dryness, and shivering.** Using an open-source 5-variable dynamical-systems model, we show that a single parameter set reproduces rhythmic low-threshold bursting, pharmacologic dissociation from saturating activation, plateau collapse under mitochondrial uncoupling or partial metabolic derangement (modeling diabetes, hypothyroidism, and oxaliplatin neuropathy), isothermal hyperosmolar bursting at 33 °C, and a qualitative divergence between $\text{H}^+$-sensitive cold/osmolarity and $\text{H}^+$-insensitive menthol. Because extraordinary mechanistic syntheses demand severe empirical risk, we specify **seven Popperian falsifications**—anchored by a zero-evaporation **fast-cooling hydrogel submersion assay**, a **blinded 2×2 water-bolus crossover with infrared thermography**, and an open-source **dew-point psychrometric mobile platform**—that can cleanly refute each module of the theory in humans before molecular follow-up.

## 1. Introduction: Seventy Years of Cold-Fiber Physiology Before CMR1

A recurring hazard in molecular neuroscience is **historical amnesia**: the cloning of a primary transducer creates a vivid single-protein schematic that inadvertently erases decades of intact-fiber physiology that does not fit the cartoon. Nowhere is this clearer than in mammalian cold sensation.

When **CMR1** (Cold and Menthol Receptor 1, later standardized as **TRPM8**) was cloned in 2002 by McKemy, Neuhausser, and Julius (*Nature*) and independently by Peier et al. (*Cell*), it provided a triumphal molecular identity for the native cold- and menthol-activated inward cation current ($I_{\text{cold}}$) recorded in dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons one year earlier by Reid and Flonta (2001, 2002). Almost immediately, however, textbook diagrams compressed cold transduction into a linear labeled line: skin temperature drops, TRPM8 opens, afferent firing rate rises monotonically, and the brain reads out "cold." Under the same paradigm, because mammals lack dedicated insect-like hygroreceptors, human perception of skin wetness and dry-cold air has been explained primarily as a secondary inference from **evaporative cooling** (latent heat flux activating thermal TRPM8) combined with low-threshold mechanoreception (Filingeri et al., 2014; Typolt & Filingeri, 2020; Buoite Stella & Filingeri, 2022).

Yet nearly seventy years of electrophysiological research conducted **before** CMR1 was cloned demonstrated that mammalian cold fibers do not behave like passive thermometers wired to a scalar rate code:

1. **Spontaneous Activity, Bell-Shaped Ambiguity, and Periodic Bursting (1935–1980):** Beginning with Zotterman (1935, 1936) and Hensel and Zotterman (1951a), single-fiber recordings from cat lingual, nasal, and primate cutaneous cold fibers (Iggo, 1969; Darian-Smith, Johnson, & Dykes, 1973) established that cold thermoreceptors fire continuously at normal resting skin temperatures (30–35 °C). Crucially, their steady-state (static) firing rate is **bell-shaped** across temperature, peaking around 20–28 °C and falling off at both warmer and colder extremes—meaning mean firing rate alone is ambiguous. What disambiguates static temperature is **temporal burst structure**: cold fibers discharge in periodic grouped bursts whose interburst frequency increases with warming while the number of impulses per burst increases with cooling (Bade, Braun, & Hensel, 1979; Braun, Bade, & Hensel, 1980).
2. **Paradoxical Cold, Ischemia, and $\text{CO}_2$/Acidosis Inhibition (1951–1956):** Dodt and Zotterman (1952) proved that specific cold fibers fire vigorously again when heated above ~45 °C ("paradoxical cold"), undermining any naive one-to-one mapping between cold-fiber spikes and physical cooling. Almost simultaneously, **Hensel (1953, *Pflügers Arch* 257:371–383)** characterized how ischemia rapidly alters thermoreceptor discharge, and classical gas-tension studies demonstrated that elevated **$\text{CO}_2$ / local acidification depresses static cold-fiber discharge** while exciting warm fibers—an observation that remained biophysically orphaned for **five decades** until Andersson, Chase, and Bevan (2004) showed that intracellular protons ($\text{H}^+$) directly inhibit cold- and icilin-activated TRPM8 while sparing menthol.
3. **Metabolic & Bioenergetic Coupling (1974–1975):** Pierau, Torrey, and Carpenter (1974, 1975) and Spray (1974) showed that inhibiting cellular energy/ion-pumping homeostasis (e.g., ouabain blockade of the electrogenic $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase) profoundly disrupts cold-receptor discharge and adaptation, establishing that peripheral thermal sensitivity is tightly coupled to nerve-terminal bioenergetics.
4. **The Subthreshold Oscillating Inward Current & Calcium Control (1979–2000):** Across two decades, Hans A. Braun, Klaus Schäfer, Herbert Hensel, and colleagues proved that cold-fiber bursting originates from an **endogenous subthreshold membrane potential oscillation** driven by a slow oscillating inward current ($I_{sd}$, persistent $\text{Na}^+$/$\text{Ca}^{2+}$) opposed by a slow $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-regulated repolarizing feedback process ($I_{sr}$) (Braun, Bade, & Hensel, 1980; Schäfer, Braun, & Hensel, 1982; Braun et al., 1994; Braun, Huber, Dewald, Schäfer, & Voigt, 1998; Braun et al., 2000).
5. **The Forgotten Menthol–Calcium Paradox (1986–1987):** Sixteen years before CMR1 was cloned, **Schäfer, Braun, and Isenberg (1986, *J Gen Physiol*)** analyzed menthol on feline cold receptors and found that micromolar menthol produces **non-monotonic, temperature-dependent reorganization of bursting**—accelerating warm burst frequency, slowing mid-temperature burst frequency, and *suppressing* bursting and all activity at low temperatures—and that **applying external $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ completely abolished the stimulating effect of menthol**. Immediately afterward, **Swandulla, Carbone, Schäfer, and Lux (1986, 1987)** demonstrated in DRG neurons that menthol directly blocks low-threshold voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels and accelerates inactivation of high-threshold $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ currents. Menthol was never a clean, passive "cold mimetic"; it perturbed the calcium-coupled oscillator itself.
6. **Chronically Depolarized Terminals Packed with TTX-Resistant $\text{Na}^+$ Channels (2001–2002):** Immediately around CMR1's discovery, Viana, de la Peña, and Belmonte (2002) proved that cold specificity requires a specific combinatorial ionic complement ($I_{\text{cold}}$ balanced against slow outward $I_{\text{KD}}$ [$\text{K}_\text{v}1.1/1.2$] and $I_h$ [HCN]). In the exact same year, **Carr, Pianova, and Brock (2002, *J Physiol*)** recorded directly from isolated guinea-pig corneal cold nerve terminals under polarizing currents and reached a striking conclusion: corneal cold receptor terminals **express high densities of TTX-resistant $\text{Na}^+$ channels ($\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$)** and, under normal resting physiological conditions, **sit relatively depolarized**, holding those $\text{Na}^+$ conductances right at the boundary of activation and inactivation.

Taken together, this pre-molecular and early-CMR1 record proves that **the idea of cold perception as a metabolically coupled, ion-channel-integrated oscillatory code—rather than a passive TRPM8 labeled line—is not speculative; it is the original empirical foundation of the field.**

## 2. Why TRPM8 Osmosensation Should Extend to Cutaneous Perception

In 2010, Parra, Madrid, Belmonte, and colleagues (*Nature Medicine*) demonstrated that ongoing basal firing of TRPM8-expressing corneal cold thermoreceptors detects tear-film evaporation and drives basal lacrimation. Five years later, **Quallo et al. (2015, *Nature Communications*)** established the direct molecular link: small physiological increases in extracellular osmolality (**hyperosmolarity**, above ~300 mOsm/kg) directly activate heterologously expressed TRPM8 and excite corneal afferents to regulate eye blinking, whereas **hypoosmolarity inhibits** TRPM8. Subsequent in vivo work confirmed that tear-film osmolality spikes dynamically from ~300 to >450 mOsm/kg during inter-blink evaporation as read through corneal nerve activity (Hirata, Mizerska, Dallacasagrande, & Rosenblatt, 2017; reviewed in Vereertbrugghen & Galletti, 2022), that chronic dry eye remodels corneal TRPM8 firing into unpleasant dryness/pain sensations (Kovács et al., 2016), and that hyperosmolar eye drops evoke dose-dependent, TRPM8-antagonist-sensitive (BCTC-blocked) reflex eyelid squinting in rats that is markedly blunted in diabetic peripheral neuropathy (Yorek MS et al., 2016; **Figure 1**, `analysis/outputs/fig1_corneal_nerve_interface.png`).

Crucially, however, one pivotal result in Quallo et al. (2015) has been largely overlooked in human somatosensory research: **physiological hyperosmolarity excited isolated mouse dorsal root ganglion (DRG) TRPM8+ neurons—not only trigeminal corneal neurons.** Because DRG neurons innervate the somatic skin and viscera rather than the eye, **the intrinsic biophysical capacity for cutaneous TRPM8 osmosensation is already empirically demonstrated at the cellular level.**

Three further lines of evidence challenge both pure evaporative-cooling reductionism and pure labeled-line coding in extra-ocular tissues:

- **Non-evaporative wetness-expulsion behaviors ("Wet Dog Shakes"):** TRPM8 agonists such as icilin elicit robust wet-dog shaking across both mammals and birds with **zero surface moisture and zero evaporative cooling** (Selescu, Zimmermann, Babes et al., 2025). The nervous system treats a specific pattern of TRPM8 activation as an urgent wetness/chill signal even when no water is present.
- **Adjacent-Territory Qualia Divergence ($V_1$ Trigeminal):** The corneal epithelium, inner palpebral conjunctiva/lid margin, and outer eyelid skin sit millimeters apart along the same ophthalmic trigeminal branch and share TRPM8 expression and ambient evaporation. Yet corneal TRPM8 activation yields *freshness → ocular dryness → blink/squint → gritty sting*, whereas palpebral/cutaneous TRPM8 activation yields *coolness, damp/wet illusions, or chills*.
- **Combinatorial Thermal Coding:** Even warm perception in mammals is not a pure labeled line; Paricio-Montesinos et al. (2020, *Neuron*) proved that perceiving warmth requires **TRPM8 silencing** paired with warm-fiber excitation.

### Thermodynamic Formulation of Cutaneous Osmo-Thermal Co-Gating
At any exposed biological boundary—cornea, lip vermilion, or skin undergoing transepidermal water loss (TEWL)—evaporative flux simultaneously cools the surface ($\Delta T < 0$) and concentrates residual solutes ($\Delta \text{Osm}_{\text{ext}} > 0$). Extending the voltage-shift gating model of Voets et al. (2004) and $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$/membrane-tension coupling (Rohacs et al., 2005) to incorporate osmotic free energy and intracellular proton inhibition yields:

$$P_{\text{open}}(V, T, \text{Osm}, \text{pH}_i) = \left[ 1 + \exp\left( \frac{\Delta G_{\text{total}}}{RT} \right) \right]^{-1}$$

$$\Delta G_{\text{total}} = \Delta H(T) - T\Delta S(T) - zFV + \Delta G_{\text{osm}}(\text{Osm}_{\text{ext}} - \text{Osm}_{\text{int}}) + \Delta G_{\text{tens}}(\gamma) + \Delta G_{\text{H}^+}(\text{pH}_i)$$

Under this formulation, **external hyperosmolarity** (dry skin, elevated stratum corneum/interstitial tonicity under high vapor pressure deficit) shifts $V_{1/2}$ leftward so that TRPM8 conducts more strongly at normal ambient and skin temperatures (20–34 °C)—predicting that **dry skin will be perceived as colder even when physical evaporative cooling is eliminated.**

## 3. Re-Unifying the Classical Oscillator: TRPM8 – $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ – $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ – Mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$ Coupling

How does a graded osmotic/thermal shift at TRPM8 convert into the rhythmic bursting seen in classical cold fibers—and why do metabolic disorders so consistently derange cold perception?

Post-2002 models that re-inserted TRPM8 into the Huber–Braun cold-receptor framework (Orio et al., 2009, 2012; Olivares et al., 2015) successfully reproduced static and dynamic bursting by pairing TRPM8 with $I_h$ (HCN1/2), $I_{\text{KD}}$ ($\text{K}_\text{v}1.1/1.2$), slow inward currents, and generic $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-dependent $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ desensitization. However, four well-established physiological facts point to a tighter, **metabolically clocked electro-ionic feedback loop** operating specifically at low-to-moderate physiological stimulation:

1. **Basal TRPM8 Depolarization Poises the Terminal at the $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ Knee:** Consistent with Carr, Pianova, and Brock (2002), basal TRPM8 inward current at skin/room temperature holds cold terminals relatively depolarized (around −45 mV) over an HCN/$I_h$ + $I_{\text{KD}}$ resting equilibrium (~−52 mV). Cold-resistant **$\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$** (Zimmermann et al., 2007) sustains regenerative spike electrogenesis across cooling temperatures where TTX-sensitive channels partially inactivate, and functional **TRPM8 → $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ → voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel (VGCC)** coupling has been directly observed in small-diameter sensory neuron terminals (Gasperini et al., 2011).
2. **L-Type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ Amplification in Small-Diameter / C-Fiber Neurons Drives Large $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ Loads:** Action-potential bursts driven by TRPM8 + $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ recruit L-type VGCCs ($\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$/$\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$). Contrary to the misconception that L-type channels are confined to cardiac or large somatic neurons, **small-diameter (<27 µm, C-fiber) DRG neurons express robust dihydropyridine-sensitive L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ currents** (Scroggs & Fox, 1992, *J Physiol* 445:639–658; Bourinet et al., 2014, *Physiol Rev*). Specifically, $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ drives depolarization-induced feed-forward sensitization in peripheral nociceptors (Isensee et al., 2021, *J Cell Biol*, PMC8404467), mediates neuropathic plateau excitability and hypersensitivity (Fossat et al., 2010, *J Neurosci*; Li et al., 2019, PMC6929318), and contributes directly to oxaliplatin-induced cold hypersensitivity in small DRG neurons (Su et al., 2019, PMC6970200). L-type amplification elevates cytosolic $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ from ~100 nM into the 300–550 nM range—crossing the physiological mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ uniporter (MCU) set-point (~250–350 nM) (**Figure 2**, `analysis/outputs/fig2_mechanistic_circuit.png`).
3. **Mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ Buffering Generates Cytosolic Acidification:** Classic DRG physiology by **Werth and Thayer (1994, *J Neurosci*)** demonstrated that large depolarization-induced $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ loads in sensory neurons are rapidly sequestered by mitochondria via MCU, transiently uncoupling electron transport / driving mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ exchange and producing a reproducible, activity-dependent **drop in intracellular pH ($\uparrow[\text{H}^+]_i$)** that is abolished by mitochondrial uncouplers (e.g., FCCP). Remarkably, modern molecular biology has anchored this exact physical coupling: privileged **TRP–MCU–NCLX crosstalk** directly governs TRP desensitization and nociceptor firing in rat DRG neurons (Nita et al., 2016), and the inner-membrane mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ exchanger **TMBIM5** physically co-exists in the same macromolecular complex as the MCU $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-threshold gatekeeper **MICU1** (Zhang et al., 2025, *Commun Biol*, PMC12449474), alongside osmotic/$\text{K}^+$-$\text{H}^+$ partner **LETM1**.
4. **Intracellular Protons Selectively Close Cold/Icilin/Osmotic TRPM8—Completing the Clock:** **Andersson, Chase, and Bevan (2004, *J Neurosci*)** proved that intracellular acidification potently inhibits **cold- and icilin-activated TRPM8**, while **sparing menthol-activated TRPM8**. When mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ sequestration pulses cytosolic $\text{H}^+$ across TRPM8's inhibitory threshold ($\text{pH}_{1/2} \approx 7.12\text{–}7.20$), TRPM8 closes, the depolarizing trigger vanishes, $V_m$ repolarizes to ~−51 mV, $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$ and L-type channels close, $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ and $[\text{H}^+]_i$ clear via PMCA and $\text{Na}^+/\text{H}^+$ exchange (NHE), and TRPM8 unblocks to initiate the next cycle.

### Resolution of Five Classical Anomalies & Metabolic Cold Intolerance
This single electro-metabolic loop simultaneously accounts for:
- **Hensel's (1953) $\text{CO}_2$ depression of cold fibers:** Elevated $\text{CO}_2$ lowers intracellular $\text{pH}_i$, engaging the exact proton brake on TRPM8.
- **Schäfer, Braun, & Isenberg's (1986) biphasic menthol anomaly & Swandulla et al. (1987):** Because menthol bypasses $\text{H}^+$ block on TRPM8 *and* directly impedes voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels, menthol uncouples the native oscillatory reset rather than faithfully mimicking physiological cooling.
- **Pierau et al. (1974) metabolic sensitivity & clinical cold derangements:** Because burst termination depends on mitochondrial membrane potential ($\Delta\Psi_m$) driving MCU $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ uptake and $\text{H}^+$ release, **metabolic and mitochondrial dysfunction directly breaks peripheral cold-receptor coding**:
  - In **diabetic small-fiber neuropathy**, chronic bioenergetic stress and nociceptor hyperexcitability drive excessive MCU-mediated mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload and mitochondrial fission *prior* to cutaneous small-fiber loss—and **selectively deleting `MCU` in DRG nociceptors restores mitochondrial morphology, prevents axonal degeneration, and reverses diabetic neuropathic pain** (George et al., 2022, *Pain*, PMC8720329; see also Li et al., 2025). This resolves the progression from orderly rhythmic bursting to sustained depolarizing $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload, distal epidermal/corneal unmyelinated fiber die-back, and loss of TRPM8 hyperosmolar squinting (Yorek MS et al., 2016).
  - In **oxaliplatin-induced peripheral neuropathy**, acute DRG mitochondrial toxicity combined with TRPM8 sensitization (Kono et al., 2012) strips away the $\text{H}^+$ adaptation brake, locking innocuous cooling into painful non-adapting discharge.
  - In **hypothyroidism and systemic metabolic suppression**, reduced mitochondrial electron-transport reserve alters terminal $\Delta\Psi_m$ and baseline $\text{pH}_i$, shifting cold-receptor oscillatory gain and thermoregulatory shivering thresholds.

### 4.2 Human Genetic Convergence & MCU/MICU1/TMBIM5 Model Predictions

Independent human genetics strongly corroborates this multi-node circuit while explaining *why* clinical sensory phenotypes cluster at specific molecular nodes (queried via Open Targets / gnomAD constraint and primary clinical studies):

- **`TRPM8` & `SCN10A` ($\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$) Converge Directly on Human Corneal & Small-Fiber Phenotypes:** Both genes are loss-of-function-tolerant in humans (`TRPM8` LoF o/e = 0.823, pLI = 0.000; `SCN10A` LoF o/e = 0.949, pLI = 0.000), allowing rare gain-of-function (GoF) and common regulatory variants to manifest cleanly in peripheral sensory neurons. Rare human `TRPM8` GoF mutations (`D665N`, `V915M`) hyperpolarize $V_{1/2}$ and increase spontaneous trigeminal firing in patients with **persistent ocular pain after corneal LASIK axotomy** (Ghovanloo et al., 2024, PMC11567650), and `TRPM8 p.Arg30Gln` elevates basal current and resting $[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i$ in **familial trigeminal neuralgia** (Gualdani et al., 2021, PMC8105906); rare heterozygous `TRPM8` variants also associate with severe painful small-fiber neuropathy (SFN) in `SCN`-negative cohorts (Ślęczkowska et al., 2022, PMC9696564). Strikingly, a human `SCN10A` GoF variant (`p.I206M`) causes the **exact same post-LASIK corneal trigeminal pain syndrome** (Ghovanloo et al., 2026, PMC13004580), alongside Mendelian **Familial Episodic Pain Syndrome 2 (FEPS2)** and `SCN10A G1662S` painful SFN (which alters cool sensitivity when knocked into mice; Chidiac et al., 2021, PMC8671994). At the population level, the upstream `TRPM8` variant **`rs10166942`** exhibits one of the steepest latitudinal clines in the human genome (**5% in Nigeria to 88% in Finland**; Key et al., 2018, PMC5933706): its protective `[C]` allele reduces human DRG `TRPM8` expression, **attenuates cold-pain sensitivity**, and lowers migraine risk (Gavva et al., 2019, PMC6927963), while coding-region SNP **`rs11562975`** modulates both human cold/menthol sensitivity and cold-induced systemic metabolic/respiratory responses (Kozyreva et al., 2011).
- **Extreme Evolutionary Constraint on `CACNA1C` and `MCU` Shifts Human Mendelian Disease to `MICU1` and `LETM1`:** By contrast, both `CACNA1C` ($\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$: LoF o/e = 0.131, pLI = 1.000; GoF causes lethal cardiac **Timothy syndrome**) and `MCU` (LoF o/e = 0.266, pLI = 0.999; common loci modestly linked to Type 2 diabetes and hypertension) are severely constrained systemically, precluding isolated human null-channel sensory syndromes. Instead, human Mendelian disorders of mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ homeostasis present in the **MCU $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-threshold gatekeeper `MICU1`**—where biallelic LoF removes the ~300 nM resting gate, causing constitutive baseline mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload and **Myopathy with Extrapyramidal Signs (MPXPS)** (Logan et al., 2014; 62-patient cohort in Beheshti et al., 2025)—and in the mitochondrial osmotic/$\text{K}^+$-$\text{H}^+$ exchanger **`LETM1`** (childhood-onset mitochondrial neurodegeneration with optic atrophy in 83%, sensorineural hearing loss in 78%, ataxia in 78%, and diabetes in 27%; Kaiyrzhanov et al., 2022).

### 4.3 The $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ Neurovascular & Dihydropyridine Pharmacology Conundrum

Testing L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel involvement in intact perfused skin using classic dihydropyridines (DHPs)—such as the L-type antagonist **nifedipine** (or nimodipine/amlodipine) and the L-type agonist **`(−)-(S)-Bay K 8644`**—exposes a **triple neurovascular–pharmacological confound** that must be explicitly separated in both animal and human experiments (**Figure 5**, `analysis/outputs/fig5_neurovascular_pharmacology.png`):

1. **Vascular Smooth-Muscle $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ Creates Twin Thermal and Osmotic-Washout Confounds:** Cutaneous arteriolar tone is controlled by high-affinity smooth-muscle $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ ($IC_{50} \approx 10\text{–}50\text{ nM}$ for nifedipine). Administering nifedipine in perfused skin dilates dermal arterioles, rushing warm (~37 °C) arterial blood into the dermis—which simultaneously **warms skin thermally** (closing TRPM8) and **accelerates capillary interstitial washout** (clamping periterminal fluid back toward ~290 mOsm/kg and erasing local drying-induced hyperosmolarity). Conversely, **Bay K 8644** potently *vasoconstricts* cutaneous arterioles, cooling skin and slowing interstitial washout so that pure vasoconstriction mimics neuronal L-type excitation. Moreover, because chronically depolarized cold terminals (~−45 mV; Carr et al., 2002) are poised inside the hyperpolarized activation window of **$\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$** (which has ~10-fold lower DHP affinity than vascular $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$), low-dose systemic nifedipine vasodilates skin *before* fully blocking neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$.
2. **Direct Off-Target Activation of `TRPA1` by Both `Nifedipine` and `Bay K 8644`:** **Fajardo, Meseguer, Belmonte, and Viana (2008, *Channels*, PMID: 18971630)** proved that four clinical 1,4-dihydropyridine antagonists (`nifedipine`, `nimodipine`, `nicardipine`, `nitrendipine`) **and** the agonist **`Bay K 8644`** directly activate **TRPA1** channels on peptidergic sensory nerve endings independently of $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$ (abolished by `HC-030031` or `Trpa1-/-`). In perfused skin, perivascular TRPA1 activation triggers neurogenic CGRP vasodilation—compounding nifedipine's smooth-muscle vasodilation while confounding Bay K 8644's sensory readout.
3. **Bidirectional TRPM8–Microvascular Coupling:** The neurovascular unit feeds back in both directions: **Dillon, Lichter, and Alexander (2021, *Microvasc Res*, PMID: 34717968)** showed topical 5% menthol elevates human skin vascular conductance >11-fold locally and across the contralateral dermatome via sensory-nerve / EDHF reflex vasodilation, confirming that peripheral cold terminals and dermal microvessels form a coupled **neurovascular–osmotic loop**.

Coupling local perfusion-driven temperature ($\Delta T_{\text{vasc}}$) and interstitial osmotic washout directly into our ODE model (`analysis/trpm8_oscillator_ode.py`, **Figure 5**) exposes both the pitfalls and four clean de-confounding designs:

- **In Avascular or Bath-Clamped Tissue (`ΔFlow = 0`, Figure 5A):** Removing hemodynamics isolates pure neuronal L-type gating. Under mild osmotic bursting (+82 mOsm at 33 °C, with background `HC-030031` TRPA1 blockade), **`(−)-(S)-Bay K 8644`** converts rhythmic oscillations into a **sustained non-adapting high-$\text{Ca}^{2+}$ plateau** (paralleling classic nifedipine-sensitive L-type plateau potentials in sensory/dorsal horn neurons; Morisset & Nagy, 1999; Fossat et al., 2010), because potentiated $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ sustains depolarization even after mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$ closes TRPM8. Co-applying **nifedipine** (or the stereoisomeric L-type antagonist **`(+)-(R)-Bay K 8644`**) cleanly collapses the plateau back to baseline. Crucially, the **avascular central cornea**—where non-DHP **diltiazem** was already shown to depress corneal C-fiber cold discharge while sparing Aδ mechanoreceptors (Mikulec et al., 1995)—and the **isolated ex vivo skin–nerve preparation** eliminate vascular confounds completely.
- **In Perfused Skin (Figure 5B–D):** Pure vascular vasodilation (+2.0 °C warming + 50% osmotic washout, modeling a non-DHP vasodilator such as an **ACE inhibitor/ARB**) **completely silences** osmotic bursting (`101 nM` flat) even with neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}$ untouched—making low-dose perfused nifedipine indistinguishable from vascular warming even though the same neuronal block still oscillates vigorously in clamped tissue (**Figure 5B**). Mirroring this, vascular-only vasoconstriction (−2.0 °C cooling + 30% reduced washout) **falsely ignites 3-peak bursting** from quiet skin (**Figure 5C**). The **2D Neurovascular De-Confounding Map (Figure 5D)** therefore specifies a decisive human natural experiment: comparing hypertensive patients already taking chronic **DHP blockers (`amlodipine`/`nifedipine`)** against patients on **non-DHP vasodilators (`ACEi`/`ARBs`)** during **thermal-clamped Fast-Cooling Gel Submersion (`Protocol H2-Gel`)** with IR skin-temperature matching and photoplethysmography (PPG) perfusion covariates cleanly separates neuronal $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2/\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.3$ inhibition from microvascular warming.

## 5. Seven Severe Popperian Falsifications: How This Theory Can Die

A theoretical framework is scientifically useful only to the extent that it exposes itself to decisive refutation. Extending TRPM8 osmosensation from the avascular cornea to intact human skin faces **seven genuinely plausible failure modes**. Below, we formalize each kill-switch and specify the exact human behavioral or cellular experiment capable of falsifying that module.

### Falsification 1 — The Stratum Corneum & Vascular Buffering Kill-Switch
- **How the theory could fail:** Unlike the cornea—where unmyelinated terminals pierce directly to the evaporating tear-film surface (**Figure 1**) and experience rapid tear hyperosmolarity—cutaneous TRPM8+ terminals sit beneath a hydrophobic **stratum corneum** and adjacent to perfused dermal capillaries that clamp interstitial fluid near ~290 mOsm/kg. If dermal perfusion and barrier impermeability prevent external drying from altering periterminal osmolality by the ~15–30 mOsm/kg needed to shift TRPM8 $V_{1/2}$, cutaneous TRPM8 osmosensation will be physiologically silent in intact skin (operating only at mucosae, vermilion lip border, or barrier-damaged xerotic/eczematous skin).
- **Crucial Test (Protocol H2-Gel — Fast-Cooling Gel Submersion, $J_{\text{evap}} = 0$):** Submerging both hands simultaneously into temperature-matched ($\pm 0.1\,^\circ\text{C}$) aqueous hydrogels physically eliminates the air–skin interface ($J_{\text{evap}} \equiv 0$). Two orthogonal contrasts are run counterbalanced across Left/Right hands:
  1. *Dry-Preconditioned vs. Hydrated Hand into the Identical $22.0\,^\circ\text{C}$ Gel Bath* (matched for pre-immersion IR skin temperature within $\pm 0.2\,^\circ\text{C}$). Crucially, hydrated stratum corneum possesses slightly **higher** thermal conductivity ($k$) than dry stratum corneum—so pure heat-transfer physics predicts the hydrated hand loses heat *faster* and should feel colder! If the **dry-preconditioned hand nevertheless feels colder**, pure thermal physics is falsified in favor of cutaneous osmotic sensitization.
  2. *Hyperosmotic (600–900 mOsm) vs. Isoosmotic (300 mOsm) Gel* at both $22.0\,^\circ\text{C}$ and skin-neutral $33.0\,^\circ\text{C}$, comparing **NaCl** against **non-ionic mannitol** (controlling for ionic strength/conductivity), and comparing intact volar skin against vermilion lip border.
- **Falsification Criterion:** If dry-preconditioned and hyperosmotic-submerged skin feel equal or warmer than hydrated/isoosmotic controls across $N \ge 15\text{–}21$ paired within-subject crossovers ($p > 0.05$, $d_z < 0.2$), cutaneous osmotic modulation in intact skin is **falsified**.

### Falsification 2 — The Pure Evaporative Cooling Kill-Switch
- **How the theory could fail:** Dry air (high Vapor Pressure Deficit, VPD) accelerates TEWL and sweat evaporation, physically lowering epidermal temperature via latent heat ($L_v \approx 2.45\text{ MJ/kg}$). "Dry skin feels colder" could be 100% evaporative cooling.
- **Crucial Test (Paired-Forearm Vapor Occlusion, Protocol H2):** Apply a vapor-impermeable barrier (petrolatum or Tegaderm film—halting evaporation immediately without rehydrating tissue) to one randomized forearm of dry-skinned subjects while leaving the contralateral forearm bare, matching IR surface temperature.
- **Falsification Criterion:** If vapor occlusion eliminates the entire perceived coldness excess of dry skin once IR surface temperatures match, cutaneous osmotic cold gating is **falsified** in favor of pure evaporative cooling.

### Falsification 3 — The Systemic Water-Drinking Sign Paradox & TRPV4 Osmopressor Kill-Switch
- **How the theory could fail:** Rapid water ingestion induces transient **hypoosmolarity**—yet Quallo et al. (2015) proved **hypoosmolarity directly inhibits** TRPM8! Why, then, do many individuals experience sudden chills or shivering after drinking a large water bolus quickly? Three mutually exclusive hypotheses compete:
  - *H-Direct (Transmembrane Gradient / Rebound Model):* Systemic hypoosmolarity either widens the relative outward osmotic gradient ($\text{Osm}_{\text{ext}} - \text{Osm}_{\text{int}}$) at cutaneous endings or transiently silences basal TRPM8, clearing cytosolic $\text{H}^+$ block and triggering sensitized rebound firing.
  - *H-Rival 1 (Hepatic TRPV4 Osmopressor Reflex):* Portal-vein hypoosmolarity activates hepatic/thoracic DRG **TRPV4** osmoreceptors (Lechner et al., 2011; May & Jordan, 2011), driving a spinal sympathetic reflex → **cutaneous vasoconstriction** → peripheral skin temperature drops 2–4 °C → cutaneous TRPM8 fires *thermally*, not osmotically!
  - *H-Rival 2 (Visceral Thermal Sink):* Ingesting 500 mL of water below 37 °C cools splanchnic/vagal thermoreceptors directly.
- **Crucial Test (Blinded 2×2 Water Bolus Crossover with IR Thermography, Protocol H4):** Rapid ingestion of 500 mL (<2 min) across four counterbalanced arms: (1) **37 °C plain water** (hypoosmotic, isothermal), (2) **37 °C isotonic saline/ORS** (isoosmotic, isothermal control), (3) **15 °C isotonic saline** (isoosmotic, cold), and (4) **15 °C plain water** (both), while logging fingertip/forearm IR skin temperature and cold/shiver ratings every 2–5 min for 30 min.
- **Falsification Criterion:** If **37 °C plain water** elicits chills *only after* (and proportional to) fingertip IR skin cooling—or fails to elicit chills when skin temperature is clamped while 15 °C saline does—then direct peripheral hypoosmotic activation of cutaneous TRPM8 is **falsified** (and the hepatic TRPV4 osmopressor / thermal-sink mechanisms win).

### Falsification 4 — The Non-TRPM8 Osmosensor Specificity Kill-Switch
- **How the theory could fail:** Cutaneous responses to hyperosmolar solutions could be mediated by TRPV1, TRPV4, TRPA1, ASICs, PIEZO2, or volume-regulated anion channels rather than TRPM8.
- **Crucial Test:** Combine **non-ionic mannitol vs. NaCl** iso-osmolarity controls with topical **TRPM8 desensitization / selective TRPM8 antagonism** (comparing against capsaicin/TRPV1 control).
- **Falsification Criterion:** If hyperosmolar cold enhancement occurs only with NaCl (not mannitol) or survives complete TRPM8 blockade/desensitization, TRPM8-specific cutaneous osmosensation is **falsified**.

### Falsification 5 — The Pure Labeled-Line & Temporal-Pattern Kill-Switch
- **How the theory could fail:** Regional differences between cornea, eyelid margin, and skin—and between "cool," "wet," and "shiver"—could stem solely from central anatomical projection targets of identical, rate-coded TRPM8 fibers.
- **Crucial Tests (Protocols H1 & H3):**
  1. *Isothermal $V_1$ Territory Mapping (H1):* Deliver strictly isothermal (34 °C) mildly hypertonic ophthalmic saline and isothermal dry vs. humid air puffs selectively to cornea, inner palpebral lid margin, and outer eyelid skin, scoring 5 independent qualia (`Cool`, `Wet`, `Dry/Gritty`, `Fresh`, `Blink/Shudder urge`).
  2. *Andersson $\text{H}^+$ Dissociation in Humans (H3A):* Compare time-courses of occluded topical **menthol** ($\text{H}^+$-insensitive) vs. intensity-matched **24 °C cooling** ($\text{H}^+$-sensitive) before and immediately after 90 s of localized isometric forearm grip exercise (transient local tissue lactate/$\text{H}^+$ acidosis).
  3. *Oscillatory vs. Steady Cooling (H3B):* Deliver $24.0 \pm 1.5\,^\circ\text{C}$ sinusoidal cooling at **0.03 Hz** (matching the ~33–50 s burst oscillator period) vs. identical-mean steady $24.0\,^\circ\text{C}$ via a PID Peltier thermode.
- **Falsification Criterion:** If local tissue acidosis affects cold and menthol identically, AND steady vs. 0.03 Hz oscillatory cooling at identical mean temperature elicit indistinguishable `Wet/Chill/Shiver` qualia, then the **$\text{H}^+$-clocked temporal-pattern hypothesis is falsified**.

### Falsification 6 — The Mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$ vs. $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ Cellular Clock Kill-Switch (Phase 2 Bench Test)
- **How the theory could fail:** Burst termination in native cold receptors may be dominated by $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-PLC$\delta$ depletion of $\text{PI(4,5)P}_2$ (Rohacs et al., 2005; Olivares et al., 2015) or $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-activated $\text{K}^+$ channels rather than mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$ block.
- **Crucial Test:** Whole-cell/perforated-patch recordings in native TRPM8+ DRG/TG neurons comparing low-stimulus cold/icilin vs. menthol under heavy cytosolic pH clamping (50–100 mM HEPES or $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ alkalinization) vs. intracellular $\text{diC}_8$-$\text{PIP}_2$, alongside MCU/TMBIM5 blockade (Ru360) vs. PMCA blockade (extracellular pH 8.8 / caloxin).
- **Falsification Criterion:** If clamping $\text{pH}_i$ fails to disrupt cold/osmotic oscillatory reset while $\text{PIP}_2$ supplementation abolishes adaptation identically for cold and menthol, the mitochondrial $\text{H}^+$ clock is **falsified**.

### Falsification 7 — The Ecological Negligibility Kill-Switch & Why Small-$N$ Crossovers Beat Confounded Big Data
- **How the theory could fail:** Even if cutaneous TRPM8 osmosensation exists under laboratory 600–900 mOsm challenges, everyday swings in ambient VPD (0.3–2.2 kPa) and hydration might be too small ($|d| < 0.1$) to impact daily human cold perception.
- **Psychrometric & Statistical Design (`analysis/power_and_simulation.py`, Figure 6):** Monte Carlo simulation ($N = 2{,}000$) reveals a severe trap in naive weather-app studies: correlating *outdoor* relative humidity against indoor thermal ratings **inverts the true sign** ($r = -0.067, p = 0.01$) because humid 0 °C winter air (80% outdoor RH) drops to **19.7% RH ($\text{VPD} = 1.99\text{ kPa}$)** when heated indoors to 21 °C. Converting outdoor dew point ($T_d$) into true indoor-corrected **Local VPD** cleanly recovers the underlying physical signal ($r_{\text{partial}} = -0.178, p = 1.1 \times 10^{-15}$). Furthermore, within-subject repeated designs eliminate massive between-person clothing/baseline variance: **14 repeated app check-ins/person achieves 96% power at $N = 10$ participants**, and **paired Fast-Cooling Gel Submersion / Water Bolus crossovers achieve ~80–92% power at just $N = 15\text{–}20$ participants** (compared to $N \approx 180$ for noisy cross-sectional single reports).
- **Falsification Criterion:** If within-subject regression (`analysis/analyze_ema_export.py`) across $N \ge 20$ participants shows $\beta_{\text{VPD}|\text{T}} \approx 0$ ($p > 0.05$) after controlling for local air temperature and vapor occlusion, then ecological tissue-osmolarity modulation in healthy intact skin is **falsified** (restricting clinical significance to dry/compromised skin, mucosae, and neuropathy).

## 6. Phase 2 Outlook: Molecular Specialization Across Cornea, Palpebra, and Skin

Following a strict **Human Behaviors First → Molecular Specialization** workflow, any regional or temporal dissociation surviving Falsifications 1–7 immediately prioritizes four concrete peripheral molecular candidates that can tune the identical *Trpm8* gene differently across cornea, palpebral margin, and cutaneous DRG endings:

1. **TRPM8 Channel-Associated Factors (TCAF1 & TCAF2):** Gkika et al. (2015) showed TCAF1 potentiates TRPM8 trafficking and open probability while TCAF2 inhibits it; remarkably, *TCAF* genes underwent human-specific duplication and opposing evolutionary selection (Hsieh, Eichler et al., 2021)—making differential TCAF1/TCAF2 stoichiometry an ideal mechanism for region- and species-specific tuning of cutaneous vs. corneal cold/osmo-sensitivity.
2. **N-Glycosylation at Asn934 & Lipid-Raft Partitioning:** Deglycosylation of TRPM8 shifts its voltage-activation curve ($V_{1/2}$) by +60 mV and cold threshold by ~6 °C while altering lipid-raft localization (Pertusa et al., 2012; Morenilla-Palao et al., 2009), providing a potent post-translational dial for osmotic membrane-tension sensitivity.
3. **Short Splice Isoforms (4TM-TRPM8 / sM8):** Modulate both full-length TRPM8 channel gating and ER–mitochondria $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ transfer (Bidaux et al., 2016).
4. **Partner-Channel & Mitochondrial Exchanger Stoichiometry:** Local co-expression ratios of $\text{Na}_\text{v}1.8$, L-type $\text{Ca}_\text{v}1.2$, $I_{\text{KD}}$ ($\text{K}_\text{v}1.1/1.2$), HCN1/2, and the mitochondrial $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{H}^+$ exchanger **TMBIM5/LETM1** determine whether a given terminal operates as a silent nociceptor, a tonic tear-film monitor, or a metabolically clocked burst oscillator.

## 7. Conclusion

The hypothesis that TRPM8 osmosensation extends to cutaneous sensory endings and shapes human thermal and wetness perception is neither an isolated speculation nor a contradiction of established physiology. Rather, it is the logical synthesis of **nine decades of cold-fiber research**: from Hensel's 1951–1953 bursting and $\text{CO}_2$-inhibited cold fibers, Pierau's 1974 bioenergetic coupling, and Braun and Schäfer's 1980–1986 $\text{Ca}^{2+}$-controlled oscillating inward current, through Carr and Brock's 2002 depolarized TTX-resistant cold terminals, McKemy and Peier's 2002 CMR1/TRPM8, Andersson's 2004 intracellular proton block, and Quallo's 2015 demonstration of DRG TRPM8 osmosensation. By pairing an explicit electro-metabolic relaxation-oscillator model with seven severe, low-cost human Popperian falsifications—starting with zero-evaporation fast-cooling gel submersion and dew-point-corrected within-subject psychophysics—the theory can now be rigorously tested, refined, or decisively refuted.
